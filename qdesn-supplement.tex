\section{Prior specification and single-level computation}
\label{sec:appendix-prior-computation}
\label{sec:supp_relation_main}

\subsection{Distributional conventions}
\label{sec:dist_conventions}

For MCMC, coordinate-ascent variational inference (CAVI), and the evidence
lower bound (ELBO), the inverse-gamma and generalized inverse Gaussian
conventions are, for \(x>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\IG(a,b):\quad p(x)&=\frac{b^a}{\Gamma(a)}x^{-a-1}\exp(-b/x),\\
\GIG(\lambda,\chi,\psi):\quad p(x)&\propto
x^{\lambda-1}\exp\{-\tfrac12(\chi/x+\psi x)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The IG rate is in reciprocal scale; \(\TN(m,V)\) means
\(\Normal(m,V)\) truncated to \((0,\infty)\), with \(V\) a variance;
\(\Phi,\phi\) are the standard Normal distribution and density functions.

For a fixed target quantile \(p_0\in(0,1)\), the AL constants are
\[
A_0=\frac{1-2p_0}{p_0(1-p_0)},\qquad
B_0=\frac{2}{p_0(1-p_0)}.
\]
The quantile-fixed generalized asymmetric Laplace distribution
\(\exAL\) of \citet{yan2025new} identifies its location \(\mu\) as the
target conditional quantile. Its shape parameter is \(\gamma\in(L,U)\), where \(L<0<U\)
satisfy \(g(L)=1-p_0\) and \(g(U)=p_0\). Define
\[
g(\gamma)=2\Phi(-|\gamma|)\exp(\gamma^2/2),\qquad
p_\gamma=\ind\{\gamma<0\}
+\frac{p_0-\ind\{\gamma<0\}}{g(\gamma)}.
\]
Then, with the fixed probability level shown explicitly when needed,
\[
\begin{alignedat}{2}
A_{p_0}(\gamma)\equiv A_\gamma
&=\frac{1-2p_\gamma}{p_\gamma(1-p_\gamma)},
&\qquad B_{p_0}(\gamma)\equiv B_\gamma
&=\frac{2}{p_\gamma(1-p_\gamma)},\\
C_\gamma&=\{\ind\{\gamma>0\}-p_\gamma\}^{-1},
& D_\gamma\equiv D_{p_0}(\gamma)&=C_\gamma|\gamma|.
\end{alignedat}
\]
At \(\gamma=0\), omit the \(s_t\) variables and take the AL constants
\(A_0,B_0\), with \(D_0=0\). Numerical derivatives for nonzero shape remain
within the corresponding sign region.

\label{sec:desn_features}
For the fixed feature map of main Section 2, \(T\) fitting observations and
\(r\) slopes, write
\(\vect x_t=(1,\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top)^\top\) and
\(\vect\beta=(\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_r)^\top\).
Inference conditions on \(\mat X\), its reservoir weights, transformations
and selected inputs. Direct inputs may augment the reservoir features as
specified in Appendices D and E.

\subsection{Likelihoods, priors and augmented target}
\label{sec:model_joint}

Use \(\sigma\sim\IG(a_\sigma,b_\sigma)\), with positive hyperparameters,
and for exAL a proper shape density
\(\pi_\gamma(\gamma)\ind\{L<\gamma<U\}\), independent of the coefficient
prior. Each analysis specifies its numerical settings and support restrictions.

With \(\mu_t=\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta\), the common observation hierarchy is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
v_t\mid\sigma &\sim \Exp(\text{rate}=1/\sigma),&
s_t&\sim\TN(0,1),\\
y_t\mid\vect\beta,\sigma,\gamma,v_t,s_t,\mat X
&\sim
\Normal\left(
\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta+\sigma D_\gamma s_t+A_\gamma v_t,
\sigma B_\gamma v_t
\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_exal_hierarchy}
\end{equation}
\label{eq:supp_al_hierarchy}
Integrating out \((v_t,s_t)\) gives the exAL likelihood with conditional
\(p_0\)-quantile \(\mu_t\). AL sets \(\gamma=0\), uses \(A_0,B_0\),
and omits \(s_t\) and its density.

\subsubsection{Coefficient priors}
\label{sec:priors}

For ridge regularization, use the general positive-definite Gaussian prior
\begin{equation}
\vect\beta\sim\Normal(\vect b_\beta,\mat V_\beta),
\qquad
\mat P_\beta=\mat V_\beta^{-1}.
\label{eq:supp_ridge_prior}
\end{equation}
Diagonal slope penalties with weak intercept regularization are a special case.

The Nishimura--Suchard (NS) regularized-horseshoe construction
\citep{CarvalhoPolsonScott2010HS,PiironenVehtari2017RHS,NishimuraSuchard2023SSS}
uses an independent intercept \(\beta_0\sim\Normal(b_0,V_0)\) and slope variances
\begin{equation}
\beta_j\mid\lambda_j,\tau,\zeta
\sim
\Normal(0,V_j),\qquad
V_j=\left(\zeta^{-2}+\tau^{-2}\lambda_j^{-2}\right)^{-1}
=\frac{\zeta^2\tau^2\lambda_j^2}{\zeta^2+\tau^2\lambda_j^2}.
\label{eq:supp_rhs_cond_beta}
\end{equation}
The auxiliary half-Cauchy augmentation
\citep{MakalicSchmidt2016SimpleSampler} is
\begin{align}
\lambda_j^2\mid\nu_j&\sim\IG(1/2,1/\nu_j),&
\nu_j&\sim\IG(1/2,1),\\
\tau^2\mid\xi&\sim\IG(1/2,1/\xi),&
\xi&\sim\IG(1/2,1/\tau_0^2),
\label{eq:supp_hs_aux}\\
\zeta^2&\sim\IG(a_\zeta,b_\zeta),
\label{eq:supp_zeta_prior}
\end{align}
Omit the slab prior when \(\zeta^2\) is fixed. The Gaussian regularizing
factor changes the joint scale distribution: writing
\(a=\tau^2\lambda_j^2\) and \(b=\zeta^2\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Gaussian product:}\quad
\Normal(\beta_j\mid0,a)\Normal(0\mid\beta_j,b)
&=\Normal(0\mid0,a+b)\Normal(\beta_j\mid0,ab/(a+b)).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_ns_product_factor}
\end{equation}
Discarding the scale-dependent first factor gives the direct normalized
RHS prior with independent half-Cauchy scales: the coefficient variance
agrees, but the joint prior differs. The NS updates retain this mass.

Let \(\phi_1(\cdot;m,V)\) denote the Gaussian density with mean \(m\)
and variance \(V\), and let
\(\Theta_\beta=(\vect\lambda^2,\vect\nu,\tau^2,\xi,\zeta^2)\).
The full augmented NS hierarchy is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\pi_{\mathrm{NS}}(\vect\beta,\Theta_\beta)
&\propto \phi_1(\beta_0;b_0,V_0)
  p(\tau^2\mid\xi)p(\xi)p(\zeta^2)\\
&\quad\times\prod_{j=1}^r
 \left\{\phi_1(\beta_j;0,\tau^2\lambda_j^2)
 \phi_1(0;\beta_j,\zeta^2)
 p(\lambda_j^2\mid\nu_j)p(\nu_j)\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_ns_full_prior}
\end{equation}
Every density retains its normalizing factors; the proportionality constant
depends only on fixed hyperparameters. For a fixed slab, omit
\(p(\zeta^2)\) and its entry in \(\Theta_\beta\), but retain the Gaussian
regularizing factor.

\subsubsection{Reference calibration of the global scale}
\label{subsec:supp-rhs-global-scale}

For \(r\) standardized slopes and \(N_{\mathrm{fit}}\) fitting observations,
the Gaussian reference of \citet{PiironenVehtari2017RHS} gives
\begin{equation}
\text{Prior-scale reference:}\quad
\tau_{0,\mathrm{ref}}
=\frac{m_0}{r-m_0}\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{ref}}}{\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{fit}}}},
\qquad 0<m_0<r.
\label{eq:supp-rhs-tau0-gaussian-reference}
\end{equation}
Here \(m_0\) references slopes escaping strong shrinkage and
\(\sigma_{\mathrm{ref}}\) is a residual standard deviation. Derived for an
orthogonal Gaussian design with ordinary horseshoe scales, this is a
prior-specification heuristic for correlated NS features, not an exact
effective-size identity or numerical initialization. Likelihoods may share
the reference; coefficient or difference blocks may have separate values.

If final features are centered as
\(\mat Z_c=\mat Z-\ones{T}\vect a^\top\), the equivalent intercept is
\(\beta_{0c}=\beta_0+\vect a^\top\vect\beta_{\mathrm{slope}}\).
Preserving the model requires the transformed joint prior
\(\pi_c(\beta_{0c},\vect\beta_{\mathrm{slope}},\Theta)
=\pi_{\mathrm{NS}}(\beta_{0c}-\vect a^\top\vect\beta_{\mathrm{slope}},
\vect\beta_{\mathrm{slope}},\Theta)\), with unit Jacobian.
A proper coefficient prior does not require sample orthogonality:
\(\mat X^\top\mat W\mat X+\mat P\) is positive definite for positive
observation precisions and positive-definite prior precision \(\mat P\).

Let \(f\in\{\mathrm{AL},\mathrm{exAL}\}\), let
\(\vect a_f=\vect v\) for AL and
\(\vect a_f=(\vect v^\top,\vect s^\top)^\top\) for exAL, and let
\(\eta_f\) be empty for AL and equal to \(\gamma\) for exAL. The common
augmented target is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&p(\vect y,\vect a_f,\vect\beta,\sigma,\eta_f,
\Theta_h\mid\mat X)\\
&\quad\propto
\prod_{t=1}^T
\left\{p_f(y_t\mid\vect\beta,\sigma,\eta_f,\vect a_{f,t},\vect x_t)
p_f(\vect a_{f,t}\mid\sigma)\right\}\,
p_h(\vect\beta,\Theta_h)\,p(\sigma)\,p_f(\eta_f),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp-generic-augmented-target}
\end{equation}
where \(h\in\{\mathrm{ridge},\mathrm{RHS}\}\). For ridge,
\(\Theta_h\) is empty and \(p_h(\vect\beta,\Theta_h)\) is
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_ridge_prior}. For RHS,
\(\Theta_h=(\vect\lambda^2,\vect\nu,\tau^2,\xi,\zeta^2)\), and
\(p_h=\pi_{\mathrm{NS}}\) in Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_ns_full_prior}.
The prior enters once, outside the observation product. The likelihood and
latent densities use Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_exal_hierarchy}; AL omits \(s_t\)
and has \(p_f(\eta_f)=1\), whereas exAL includes \(p(s_t)\) and
\(p_f(\eta_f)=\pi_\gamma(\gamma)\ind\{L<\gamma<U\}\).

\subsection{MCMC full conditionals}
\label{sec:mcmc}

Ridge and NS share the likelihood updates below. Only the coefficient-prior
precision and shrinkage updates differ; the exAL scale--shape block is
nonconjugate.

For exAL, define
\begin{equation}
y_t^\star=y_t-\sigma D_\gamma s_t-A_\gamma v_t,\qquad
w_t=(\sigma B_\gamma v_t)^{-1},\qquad
\mat W_y=\diag(w_1,\ldots,w_T).
\end{equation}
Let \(\vect y^\star=(y_1^\star,\ldots,y_T^\star)^\top\). For a ridge prior,
set \(\mat P=\mat P_\beta\) and \(\vect b=\vect b_\beta\). For
the regularized-horseshoe prior, set
\begin{equation}
\mat P=\diag\left(V_0^{-1},
\tau^{-2}\lambda_1^{-2}+\zeta^{-2},\ldots,
\tau^{-2}\lambda_r^{-2}+\zeta^{-2}\right),
\qquad
\vect b=(b_0,0,\ldots,0)^\top.
\label{eq:supp_prior_precision_mcmc}
\end{equation}
The conditional \(\vect\beta\mid\cdot\sim
\Normal(\vect m_{\beta\mid\cdot},\mat\Sigma_{\beta\mid\cdot})\) has
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mat\Sigma_{\beta\mid\cdot}
&=(\mat X^\top\mat W_y\mat X+\mat P)^{-1},\\
\vect m_{\beta\mid\cdot}
&=\mat\Sigma_{\beta\mid\cdot}
(\mat X^\top\mat W_y\vect y^\star+\mat P\vect b).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_beta_mcmc}
\end{equation}
For AL, set \(D_\gamma=0\) and use \(A_0,B_0\).

\subsubsection{Latent variables and AL scale}

With \(\delta_t=y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta-\sigma D_\gamma s_t\),
\begin{equation}
v_t\mid\cdot\sim\GIG\left(\frac12,
\frac{\delta_t^2}{\sigma B_\gamma},
\frac{A_\gamma^2/B_\gamma+2}{\sigma}\right).
\label{eq:supp_v_mcmc}
\end{equation}
\label{eq:supp_al_v_mcmc}
AL sets \(\delta_t=y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta\), uses \(A_0,B_0\),
and omits \(s_t\). For exAL, with
\(\mu_{0t}=y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta-A_\gamma v_t\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
a_{s,t}&=1+\frac{\sigma D_\gamma^2}{B_\gamma v_t},&
m_{s,t}&=\frac{D_\gamma\mu_{0t}}{B_\gamma v_t+\sigma D_\gamma^2},\\
s_t\mid\cdot&\sim\TN(m_{s,t},a_{s,t}^{-1}).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_s_mcmc}
\end{equation}
The AL scale conditional is
\begin{equation}
\sigma\mid\cdot
\sim
\IG\left(
a_\sigma+\frac{3T}{2},
b_\sigma+\sum_{t=1}^T v_t
+\frac12\sum_{t=1}^T
\frac{(y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta-A_0v_t)^2}{B_0v_t}
\right).
\label{eq:supp_al_sigma_mcmc}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{exAL scale and shape}

At fixed coefficients and latent variables, set
\(r_{t,\gamma}=y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta-A_\gamma v_t\).
The exAL scale--shape block is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Joint kernel:}\quad
\pi(\sigma,\gamma\mid\cdot)
&\propto\exp(c_\gamma)\sigma^{\nu-1}
 \exp\!\left[-\tfrac12\{\chi_\gamma/\sigma+\psi_\gamma\sigma\}\right],\\
\nu&=-a_\sigma-3T/2,\\
\chi_\gamma&=2b_\sigma+2\sum_t v_t+
 \sum_t r_{t,\gamma}^2/(B_\gamma v_t),\\
\psi_\gamma&=D_\gamma^2\sum_t s_t^2/(B_\gamma v_t),\\
c_\gamma&=\log\pi_\gamma(\gamma)-\tfrac T2\log B_\gamma+
 \sum_t D_\gamma s_t r_{t,\gamma}/(B_\gamma v_t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_sigmagam_joint_kernel}
\end{equation}
The kernel is zero outside \(\sigma>0,\ L<\gamma<U\).
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\text{Conditional scale:}\quad
\sigma\mid\gamma,\cdot\sim\GIG(\nu,\chi_\gamma,\psi_\gamma).
\label{eq:supp_exal_sigma_mcmc}
\end{equation}
Holding scale fixed gives the one-dimensional shape conditional
\begin{equation}
\pi(\gamma\mid\cdot)\propto
\exp(c_\gamma)\exp[-\{\chi_\gamma/\sigma+
\psi_\gamma\sigma\}/2]\,\ind\{L<\gamma<U\}.
\label{eq:supp_gamma_mcmc}
\end{equation}
A conditional scale-collapsed transition instead integrates \(\sigma\):
\begin{equation}
\pi(\gamma\mid\text{remaining blocks})
\propto\exp(c_\gamma)Z_\sigma(\gamma)\ind\{L<\gamma<U\},
\label{eq:supp_collapsed_shape_kernel}
\end{equation}
where \(Z_\sigma\) is given by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_structured_scale_integral}.
It then draws scale from Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_exal_sigma_mcmc} before any
remaining block is updated. A transformed shape transition includes its
Jacobian. Appendix D identifies the transitions actually reported; the
integration here is conditional on latent variables, whereas VB averages
over their factors.

\subsubsection{Shrinkage scales}
\label{sec:rhs_ns}

The NS scale conditionals, given \(\vect\beta\), are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_j^2\mid\cdot
&\sim
\IG\left(1,\frac{1}{\nu_j}+\frac{\beta_j^2}{2\tau^2}\right),\qquad
\nu_j\mid\cdot\sim
\IG\left(1,1+\frac{1}{\lambda_j^2}\right),\\
\tau^2\mid\cdot
&\sim
\IG\left(\frac{r+1}{2},
\frac{1}{\xi}+\frac12\sum_{j=1}^r\frac{\beta_j^2}{\lambda_j^2}\right),\qquad
\xi\mid\cdot\sim
\IG\left(1,\frac{1}{\tau_0^2}+\frac{1}{\tau^2}\right),\\
\zeta^2\mid\cdot
&\sim
\IG\left(a_\zeta+\frac{r}{2},
b_\zeta+\frac12\sum_{j=1}^r\beta_j^2\right),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_rhs_mcmc}
\end{equation}
with the last line omitted for a fixed slab. The global shape \((r+1)/2\)
and slab shape \(a_\zeta+r/2\) are distinct.

\begin{suppalgorithm}{Single-level Q--DESN MCMC sampler}
\label{alg:qdesn_mcmc}
\emph{Input:} \(\vect y,\mat X,p_0\), AL or exAL likelihood, ridge or NS
prior, iteration allocation and transition tuning.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Initialize \(\vect\beta,\sigma,\vect v\), and, for exAL,
\(\gamma,\vect s\), within their supports; initialize NS scales if used.
\item Draw \(v_t\) by Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_v_mcmc} and, for exAL, \(s_t\)
by Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_s_mcmc}.
\item Draw coefficients by Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_beta_mcmc}.
\item If using NS, draw its scales by Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_rhs_mcmc}.
\item Draw AL scale by Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_al_sigma_mcmc}, or apply the
study's exAL transition: joint Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_sigmagam_joint_kernel},
conditional Eqs.~\eqref{eq:supp_exal_sigma_mcmc}--\eqref{eq:supp_gamma_mcmc},
or collapsed shape Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_collapsed_shape_kernel} immediately
followed by conditional scale Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_exal_sigma_mcmc}.
\item Repeat steps 2--5 for the allocation; return post-burn-in draws and
diagnostics, applying thinning only if specified.
\end{enumerate}
\end{suppalgorithm}

\subsection{Variational approximations}
\label{sec:cavi}

The fixed-design variational family is
\begin{equation}
q=
q_\beta(\vect\beta)\,q_\Theta(\Theta_\beta)\,
q_{\sigma,\gamma}(\sigma,\gamma)
\prod_{t=1}^T q_v(v_t)q_s(s_t),
\label{eq:supp_generic_vb_family}
\end{equation}
with independent shrinkage factors
\begin{equation}
q_\Theta(\Theta_\beta)
=q_{\tau^2}(\tau^2)q_\xi(\xi)q_{\zeta^2}(\zeta^2)
 \prod_{j=1}^r q_{\lambda_j^2}(\lambda_j^2)q_{\nu_j}(\nu_j),
\label{eq:supp_generic_vb_scale_family}
\end{equation}
and fixed-scale factors omitted. The coefficient factor retains full
covariance, and \(q_{\sigma,\gamma}\) retains scale--shape dependence.
AL omits \(q_s,\gamma\); fixed ridge omits \(q_\Theta\).
For the mean shift \(d_t=\sigma D_\gamma s_t+A_\gamma v_t\) and variance
\(\omega_t=\sigma B_\gamma v_t\) in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_exal_hierarchy}, define
\[
\bar w_t=\E_q(\omega_t^{-1}),\qquad
\bar r_t=\E_q\{\omega_t^{-1}(y_t-d_t)\}.
\]
The Gaussian factor \(q_\beta=\Normal(\vect m_\beta,\mat\Sigma_\beta)\)
uses prior canonical parameters
\((\bar{\mat P}_\beta,\bar{\vect h}_\beta)\):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mat\Sigma_\beta&=
\left(\mat X^\top\bar{\mat W}\mat X+\bar{\mat P}_\beta\right)^{-1},\\
\vect m_\beta&=
\mat\Sigma_\beta\left(\mat X^\top\bar{\vect r}
+\bar{\vect h}_\beta\right),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_generic_vb_beta}
\end{equation}
where \(\bar{\mat W}=\diag(\bar w_1,\ldots,\bar w_T)\) and
\(\bar{\vect r}=(\bar r_1,\ldots,\bar r_T)^\top\). For general ridge,
\(\bar{\mat P}_\beta=\mat V_\beta^{-1}\) and
\(\bar{\vect h}_\beta=\mat V_\beta^{-1}\vect b_\beta\).
For NS the intercept has precision \(V_0^{-1}\) and canonical mean
\(V_0^{-1}b_0\); the slope precisions are
\[
\bar P_{\beta,j}
=
\E_q(\tau^{-2})\E_q(\lambda_j^{-2})+\E_q(\zeta^{-2}),
\qquad j=1,\ldots,r .
\]

For AL, the conjugate updates are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
q_v(v_t)
&=
\GIG\left(
\frac12,\
B_0^{-1}\E_q(\sigma^{-1})\overline e_t^{\,2},\
\E_q(\sigma^{-1})\left(\frac{A_0^2}{B_0}+2\right)
\right),\\
q_\sigma(\sigma)
&=\IG(a_\sigma^\star,b_\sigma^\star),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_generic_vb_al_latents}
\end{equation}
where
\(\overline e_t^{\,2}
=\{y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect m_\beta\}^2
+\vect x_t^\top\mat\Sigma_\beta\vect x_t\), and
\[
a_\sigma^\star=a_\sigma+\frac{3T}{2},\qquad
b_\sigma^\star=b_\sigma+\sum_{t=1}^T\E_q(v_t)
+\frac{1}{2B_0}\sum_{t=1}^T
\left[
\overline e_t^{\,2}\E_q(v_t^{-1})
-2A_0\{y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect m_\beta\}
+A_0^2\E_q(v_t)
\right].
\]
For exAL, the GIG and positive-truncated Normal updates use expected
canonical parameters from Eqs.~\eqref{eq:supp_v_mcmc}--\eqref{eq:supp_s_mcmc}.
Required mixed moments include
\(\E\{(\sigma B_\gamma)^{-1}\}\),
\(\E(D_\gamma/B_\gamma)\), and
\(\E\{A_\gamma^2/(\sigma B_\gamma)+2/\sigma\}\).
The Laplace--Delta (LD) method of \citet{wang2013nonconjugatevb} or the
structured factor below supplies these moments; the GloFAS factorization
is specified separately in Section~\ref{subsec:glofas_latent_path}.

For NS CAVI, retain the five IG families and shapes in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_rhs_mcmc}, using the following exact rate substitutions:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_j^2&\mapsto M_j=m_{\beta,j}^2+(\mat\Sigma_\beta)_{jj},\\
\beta_j^2\tau^{-2}&\mapsto M_j\E_q(\tau^{-2}),\qquad
\beta_j^2\lambda_j^{-2}\mapsto M_j\E_q(\lambda_j^{-2}),\\
(\nu_j^{-1},\lambda_j^{-2},\xi^{-1},\tau^{-2})
&\mapsto\bigl(\E_q(\nu_j^{-1}),\E_q(\lambda_j^{-2}),
\E_q(\xi^{-1}),\E_q(\tau^{-2})\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_generic_vb_rhs}
\end{equation}
Independence in Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_generic_vb_scale_family} also gives
\(\E_q(\tau^{-2}\lambda_j^{-2})=
\E_q(\tau^{-2})\E_q(\lambda_j^{-2})\).
In particular the global and slab shapes remain \((r+1)/2\) and
\(a_\zeta+r/2\), respectively; omit the slab update when fixed.

For LD, transform \(\sigma=\exp(\eta_1)\) and
\(\gamma=L+(U-L)\expit(\eta_2)\), where
\(\expit(a)=\{1+\exp(-a)\}^{-1}\). For this map \(T\), the absolute
Jacobian is \(J(\vect\eta)=\sigma(U-L)\expit(\eta_2)
\{1-\expit(\eta_2)\}\). The transformed expected log density is
\[
\ell_{\mathrm{LD}}(\vect\eta)
=
\E_{q_{-(\sigma,\gamma)}}\{\log p(\vect y,\vect v,\vect s
\mid\vect\beta,\sigma,\gamma,\mat X)\}
+\log p(\sigma)+\log\pi_\gamma(\gamma)+\log J(\vect\eta).
\]
At its mode \(\hat{\vect\eta}\), set
\(\mat K_\eta=-\nabla^2\ell_{\mathrm{LD}}(\hat{\vect\eta})\) and
\(q_\eta=\Normal(\hat{\vect\eta},\mat K_\eta^{-1})\).
The induced density is
\(q_{\sigma,\gamma}(\sigma,\gamma)=q_\eta\{T^{-1}(\sigma,\gamma)\}/
J\{T^{-1}(\sigma,\gamma)\}\), not an original-scale Gaussian.
For branchwise smooth \(h\), the Delta moment rule is
\begin{equation}
\E_q\{h(\sigma,\gamma)\}
\approx
h\{T(\hat{\vect\eta})\}
+\frac12\tr\left[
\nabla^2_\eta h\{T(\hat{\vect\eta})\}\mat K_\eta^{-1}
\right],
\label{eq:supp_generic_delta}
\end{equation}
The first term alone is the mode approximation. Derivatives stay within
one nonzero-shape branch; the zero-shape case uses AL.

\begin{suppalgorithm}{Single-level Q--DESN variational updates}
\label{alg:qdesn_vb_ld}
\emph{Input:} \(\vect y,\mat X,p_0\), likelihood, prior, scale--shape
method (LD or structured), iteration limit and stopping tolerances.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Initialize the supported factors in Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_generic_vb_family}.
\item Update coefficient moments by Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_generic_vb_beta}.
\item Update AL latent and scale factors by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_generic_vb_al_latents}; for exAL use the GIG and
truncated-Normal expected canonical parameters.
\item Update NS factors by Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_generic_vb_rhs}, if present.
\item For exAL update scale--shape by LD and Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_generic_delta},
or the structured calculation below; recompute its mixed moments.
\item Repeat steps 2--5 until the specified objective or parameter-change
tolerance, or iteration limit. Return factors, summaries and diagnostics;
objective conventions are given in Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_generic_elbo}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{suppalgorithm}

\subsubsection{Structured exAL scale--shape factor}
\label{subsec:supp_structured_exal_vb}

For the unweighted \(v\) augmentation, the structured scale--shape factor
is independent of the coefficient and latent factors. Use the constants,
support and AL limit in Section~\ref{sec:dist_conventions}. With intercepts
held at their current point values when point optimization is used, let
\(e_t=y_t-\mu_t\), and write moments under the remaining factors as
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bar e_t&=\E_q(e_t),& e_t^{(2)}&=\E_q(e_t^2),&
\bar v_t&=\E_q(v_t),\\
v_t^-&=\E_q(v_t^{-1}),& \bar s_t&=\E_q(s_t),&
s_t^{(2)}&=\E_q(s_t^2).
\end{aligned}
\]
The coefficient, \(v\), and \(s\) factors are mutually independent.
For \(\sigma\sim\IG(a_\sigma,b_\sigma)\) and supported shape-prior density
\(\pi_\gamma\), expansion of the expected augmented log density gives
\begin{align}
\nu&=-a_\sigma-\frac{3T}{2},\nonumber\\
\chi_\gamma&=2b_\sigma+2\sum_t\bar v_t+
 \frac{1}{B_\gamma}\sum_t
 \left(e_t^{(2)}v_t^- -2A_\gamma\bar e_t+A_\gamma^2\bar v_t\right),
 \nonumber\\
\psi_\gamma&=\frac{D_\gamma^2}{B_\gamma}\sum_t s_t^{(2)}v_t^-,
 \nonumber\\
c_\gamma&=\log\pi_\gamma(\gamma)-\frac{T}{2}\log B_\gamma+
 \frac{D_\gamma}{B_\gamma}\sum_t\bar s_t
 \left(\bar e_tv_t^- -A_\gamma\right).
\label{eq:supp_structured_scale_shape_terms}
\end{align}
Here \(T\) counts fitting rows for one level, and the unindexed \(\nu\) is
the GIG order, not a horseshoe auxiliary \(\nu_j\). Up to a constant
independent of \((\sigma,\gamma)\), the coordinate factor is
\begin{equation}
q^*(\sigma,\gamma)\propto
 \exp(c_\gamma)\sigma^{\nu-1}
 \exp\!\left[-\frac12\left(\frac{\chi_\gamma}{\sigma}
 +\psi_\gamma\sigma\right)\right]
 \ind\{\sigma>0,\ L<\gamma<U\}.
\label{eq:supp_structured_scale_shape_kernel}
\end{equation}
Consequently \(q(\sigma\mid\gamma)=\GIG(\nu,\chi_\gamma,\psi_\gamma)\).
The integral of its unnormalized scale kernel is
\begin{equation}
Z_\sigma(\gamma)=
\begin{cases}
2(\chi_\gamma/\psi_\gamma)^{\nu/2}
 K_\nu(\sqrt{\chi_\gamma\psi_\gamma}),&\psi_\gamma>0,\\
\Gamma(-\nu)(\chi_\gamma/2)^\nu,&\psi_\gamma=0,\quad\nu<0,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:supp_structured_scale_integral}
\end{equation}
where \(K_\nu\) is the modified Bessel function of the second kind.
Retaining this shape-dependent mass yields
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Z_\gamma&=\left(\int_L^0+\int_0^U\right)
 \exp(c_\gamma)Z_\sigma(\gamma)\,d\gamma,\\
q(\gamma)&=\exp(c_\gamma)Z_\sigma(\gamma)/Z_\gamma.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_structured_shape_factor}
\end{equation}
Thus the scale integral is analytic. One-dimensional quadrature over the
bounded shape support, split at zero, supplies the normalization and mixed
moments. For an integrable function \(h\),
\begin{equation}
\E_q\{h(\sigma,\gamma)\}
=\frac{1}{Z_\gamma}\left(\int_L^0+\int_0^U\right)
 \exp(c_\gamma)Z_\sigma(\gamma)
 \E\{h(\sigma,\gamma)\mid\gamma\}\,d\gamma.
\label{eq:supp_structured_mixed_moments}
\end{equation}
The conditional scale moments are
\begin{equation}
\E(\sigma^a\mid\gamma)=
\begin{cases}
(\chi_\gamma/\psi_\gamma)^{a/2}
 \dfrac{K_{\nu+a}(\sqrt{\chi_\gamma\psi_\gamma})}
 {K_\nu(\sqrt{\chi_\gamma\psi_\gamma})},&\psi_\gamma>0,\\[5pt]
(\chi_\gamma/2)^a\dfrac{\Gamma(-\nu-a)}{\Gamma(-\nu)},
 &\psi_\gamma=0,\quad -\nu>a.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:supp_structured_scale_moments}
\end{equation}
Also \(\E(\log\sigma\mid\gamma)=\partial_\nu\log Z_\sigma(\gamma)\),
holding \(\chi_\gamma\) and \(\psi_\gamma\) fixed. Coordinate updates use
mixed moments such as \(\E_q\{(B_\gamma\sigma)^{-1}\}\),
\(\E_q\{A_\gamma/(B_\gamma\sigma)\}\),
\(\E_q(D_\gamma/B_\gamma)\), and
\(\E_q(\sigma D_\gamma^2/B_\gamma)\). Replacing these by products of
separate scale and shape expectations would define a different approximation.
The numerical branch transformation includes its integration Jacobian;
its tolerance and tail treatment are checked independently of the scalar
MCMC mixing diagnostics.

\subsubsection{Objectives and numerical stopping}
\label{sec:elbo}

For a continuous family, the original and transformed objectives are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\elbo(q)&=\E_q\{\log p(\vect y,\vect v,\vect s,\vect\beta,
\Theta_\beta,\sigma,\gamma\mid\mat X)\}+H(q)\\
&=\E_{q_uq_\eta}
 \log[p\{\vect y,\vect u,T(\vect\eta)\mid\mat X\}J(\vect\eta)]
 +H(q_u)+H(q_\eta),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_generic_elbo}
\end{equation}
\label{eq:supp_elbo_transformed_coordinates}
where \(\vect u=(\vect v,\vect s,\vect\beta,\Theta_\beta)\) and \(p\)
is the complete target in Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp-generic-augmented-target}.
Omit \(s,\gamma\) for AL and fixed shrinkage factors for ridge.
The Jacobian enters exactly once; equivalently,
\(H(q_{\sigma,\gamma})=H(q_\eta)+\E_{q_\eta}\log J\).
Approximate expectations need not give a lower bound or a monotone monitor.
The MQ simulation optimizes ordered point intercepts and monitors selected
objective terms, not this continuous-factor ELBO: point masses have no finite
differential entropy. Appendices D and E specify the reported stopping and
uncertainty conventions.

\section{Multiquantile computation}
\label{sec:joint_qdesn_supp}

For \(0<p_1<\cdots<p_K<1\), let
\(\mu_{k,t}=\beta_{0k}+\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top\vect\beta_k\), with
\(\vect\beta_k\in\R^r\) and common non-intercept design
\(\mat Z=[\tilde{\vect x}_1^\top;\ldots;\tilde{\vect x}_T^\top]\).
The composite likelihood multiplies \(K\) contributions to the same response.
Use Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_exal_hierarchy} at each level with
\begin{equation}
(p_0,\mu_t,\sigma,\gamma,v_t,s_t)
\mapsto(p_k,\mu_{k,t},\sigma_k,\gamma_k,v_{k,t},s_{k,t}),
\label{eq:supp_joint_exal_hierarchy}
\end{equation}
writing \(A_k=A_{p_k}(\gamma_k)\), and similarly \(B_k,D_k\).
AL fixes \(\gamma_k=0\) and omits \(s_{k,t}\).

\subsection{Difference hierarchy and augmented target}
\label{subsec:supp_joint_qvp_prior}

Define \(\vect\Delta_1=\vect\beta_1\) and
\(\vect\Delta_k=\vect\beta_k-\vect\beta_{k-1}\), \(k\ge2\).
Each block has the full NS hierarchy of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_ns_full_prior}, with its own local/global scales and
separately specified intercepts. Its conditional variance is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{k,j}\mid\Theta_\Delta\sim\Normal(0,V^\Delta_{k,j}),\qquad
V^\Delta_{k,j}=
\left\{\zeta_\Delta^{-2}
 +(\tau_k^\Delta)^{-2}(\lambda_{k,j}^\Delta)^{-2}\right\}^{-1}.
\label{eq:supp_joint_innovation_var}
\end{equation}
There is no latent baseline: \(K=1\) recovers the single-level slope prior.
For stacked slopes \(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}\), define the block
first-difference matrix by
\(\mat H\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}=(\vect\Delta_1^\top,\ldots,
\vect\Delta_K^\top)^\top\) and let
\(\mat D_\Delta=\diag(V^\Delta_{k,j})\). The conditional prior is
\begin{equation}
p(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}\mid\mat D_\Delta)
\propto|\mat D_\Delta|^{-1/2}
 \exp\{-\tfrac12\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}^\top
 \mat H^\top\mat D_\Delta^{-1}\mat H\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}\}.
\label{eq:supp_joint_qvp_prior}
\end{equation}
The unit-determinant difference transformation adds no Jacobian. As in
Appendix A, the full NS prior also retains the scale-dependent mass
\[
C_\Delta(\Theta_\Delta)=\prod_{k,j}
 \Normal\{0\mid0,(\tau_k^\Delta)^2(\lambda_{k,j}^\Delta)^2+
 \zeta_\Delta^2\}.
\]

Write \(\vect y_{\mathrm{st}}=\ones{K}\otimes\vect y\),
\(\mat Z_{\mathrm{st}}=\Id{K}\otimes\mat Z\), and
\(\vect\beta_{0,\mathrm{st}}=\vect\beta_0\otimes\ones{T}\).
Stack \(d_{k,t}=\sigma_kD_ks_{k,t}+A_kv_{k,t}\) in \(\vect d\) and
\(\omega_{k,t}=\sigma_kB_kv_{k,t}\) in \(\mat\Omega=\diag(\omega_{k,t})\).
The Gaussian density evaluation
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{aug}}=
\phi_{KT}(\vect y_{\mathrm{st}};
 \vect\beta_{0,\mathrm{st}}+\mat Z_{\mathrm{st}}\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}
 +\vect d,\mat\Omega)
\label{eq:supp_joint_stacked_likelihood}
\end{equation}
equals the product of the scalar Gaussian factors. The repeated response
vector is its evaluation argument, not a random vector with that
nonsingular sampling distribution. The augmented composite posterior is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\pi(\cdot\mid\vect y,\mat Z)&\propto
 L_{\mathrm{aug}}p(\vect\beta_0)
 p(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}\mid\mat D_\Delta)
 C_\Delta(\Theta_\Delta)p(\Theta_\Delta)\\
&\quad\times\prod_k\left[p(\sigma_k)\pi_{\gamma_k}(\gamma_k)
 \prod_t p(v_{k,t}\mid\sigma_k)p(s_{k,t})\right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_joint_complete_posterior}
\end{equation}
All likelihood/auxiliary factors retain their normalizers. Shape support
and the AL omissions are those of Appendix A.

\subsection{Coefficient, intercept and shrinkage updates}

With \(\vect r_\beta=\vect y_{\mathrm{st}}-\vect\beta_{0,\mathrm{st}}
-\vect d\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mat K_\beta&=\mat Z_{\mathrm{st}}^\top\mat\Omega^{-1}
 \mat Z_{\mathrm{st}}+\mat H^\top\mat D_\Delta^{-1}\mat H,\\
\vect m_\beta&=\mat K_\beta^{-1}\mat Z_{\mathrm{st}}^\top
 \mat\Omega^{-1}\vect r_\beta,\qquad
\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}\mid\cdot\sim\Normal(\vect m_\beta,\mat K_\beta^{-1}).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_joint_beta_conditional}
\end{equation}
Use sparse factorization rather than a dense inverse. For
\(\beta_{0k}\sim\Normal(b_{0k},V_{0k})\), set
\(e^0_{k,t}=y_t-d_{k,t}-\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top\vect\beta_k\).
Its conditional precision and mean are
\[
K_{0k}=V_{0k}^{-1}+\sum_t\omega_{k,t}^{-1},\qquad
m_{0k}=K_{0k}^{-1}\{V_{0k}^{-1}b_{0k}
+\sum_t\omega_{k,t}^{-1}e^0_{k,t}\}.
\]
An ordered Gaussian intercept prior restricts
\(\beta_{01}\le\cdots\le\beta_{0K}\); the conditional is
\(\Normal(m_{0k},K_{0k}^{-1})\) truncated between neighboring intercepts,
with unbounded extreme endpoints. Different slopes can still cross.

Apply Appendix A's latent and likelihood-parameter updates using
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_joint_exal_hierarchy}. For difference blocks, use
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_rhs_mcmc} with
\((\beta_j,\lambda_j,\tau,\xi,\tau_0)\mapsto
(\Delta_{k,j},\lambda^\Delta_{k,j},\tau^\Delta_k,\xi^\Delta_k,
\tau^\Delta_{0,k})\). Each global-variance conditional has shape
\((r+1)/2\). If a random slab is shared across all \(K\) blocks, its
conditional is
\[
\zeta_\Delta^2\mid\cdot\sim
\IG\left(a_{\zeta_\Delta}+\frac{Kr}{2},
 b_{\zeta_\Delta}+\frac12\sum_{k,j}\Delta_{k,j}^2\right);
\]
omit this update for a fixed slab.

\begin{suppalgorithm}{MCMC for MQ--DESN}
\label{alg:joint_qdesn_mcmc}
Given \(\vect y,\mat Z\), grid, priors and iteration settings:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Initialize supported coefficients, likelihood variables and difference scales.
\item Draw indexed \(v_{k,t}\) and exAL \(s_{k,t}\) by Appendix A, then
stacked slopes by Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_joint_beta_conditional} and intercepts
by their Gaussian or truncated-Gaussian conditionals.
\item Update first-slope and adjacent-difference scales, then AL likelihood
scales or, for the reported exAL simulation, collapsed shapes
by Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_collapsed_shape_kernel} immediately followed by
conditional scales.
\item Repeat steps 2--3 for the allocation; return post-burn-in draws and diagnostics.
\end{enumerate}
\end{suppalgorithm}

The AL GIG variables in the reported MQ analysis are sampled by slice
sampling on logarithmic coordinates. The variational slope update replaces
the likelihood and prior canonical terms in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_joint_beta_conditional} by their expectations.
Its Gaussian factor retains covariance across levels. In particular,
\begin{equation}
\text{Difference second moments:}\quad
\E_q(\vect\Delta\circ\vect\Delta)
=(\mat H\vect m)\circ(\mat H\vect m)
 +\operatorname{diag}(\mat H\mat\Sigma\mat H^\top).
\label{eq:supp_joint_increment_moments}
\end{equation}
These moments replace squared differences in the inverse-gamma updates.
The reported simulation uses the structured scale--shape factor in
Appendix A and optimizes ordered intercepts as point values; its
variational intervals omit intercept uncertainty. The GloFAS quantile-block
approximation is specified separately in Appendix E.

\section{Forecast construction and evaluation}
\label{sec:forecast_synthesis}

\subsection{Recursive responses and reported quantile paths}

At origin \(o\), initialize states from observed responses. Retain one
parameter draw
\(\theta_p^{(b)}=(\vect\beta_p^{(b)},\sigma_p^{(b)},\gamma_p^{(b)})\)
throughout a trajectory and generate
\[
\text{Future response:}\quad
\tilde Y_{o+h}^{(b,p)}
\sim\exAL_p\!\left(\vect x_{o+h}^{(b,p)\top}\vect\beta_p^{(b)},
\sigma_p^{(b)},\gamma_p^{(b)}\right).
\]
The fixed-weight DESN recursion uses observed lags through \(o\), then
trajectory responses and supplied covariates, with fresh errors at each
horizon. At new origins, reconstruct states from observed history without refitting.

Grid forecasts evaluate \(q_{o,h,k}^{(b)}=\beta_{0k}^{(b)}+
\tilde{\vect x}_{o+h}^{(b)\top}\vect\beta_k^{(b)}\) at the same trajectory's
features. Generate responses by linear inverse-CDF interpolation at a
uniform draw, clamping to the outermost fitted values outside \([p_1,p_K]\).
Independent fits pair levels by prespecified within-chain permutations;
MQ--DESN retains joint-draw dependence. Both use this recursive law.

The primary multiquantile score calculation averages each model's future
features over \(M\) simulated trajectories and then evaluates readout draws:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Mean future features:}\quad
\bar{\vect x}_{o+h}
 &=\frac1M\sum_{j=1}^M\tilde{\vect x}_{o+h}^{[j]},\\
\text{Scored location:}\quad
q_{o,h,k}^{(b)}
 &=\beta_{0k}^{(b)}+\bar{\vect x}_{o+h}^{\top}\vect\beta_k^{(b)}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp-forecast-mean-design}
\end{equation}
Here \([j]\) indexes trajectories, \(b\) scored draws, and
\(\bar{\vect x}\) excludes the intercept. Intervals condition on this
averaged design; trajectory-specific sensitivity pairs coefficient draws
with their recursive features, adding trajectory variation. Neither
calculation inverts the mixture over future histories: conditional and
averaged locations generally differ from mixture quantiles. Single-level
results score conditional-quantile paths; Appendix E specifies the distinct
GloFAS and PriceFM forecasts.

\subsection{Ordering, scores and uncertainty}

For ordinates \(\widehat{\vect q}_t\) on an ordered probability grid,
the multiquantile simulation applies
\begin{equation}
\text{Isotonic projection:}\quad
\vect q_t^*=\argmin_{z_1\le\cdots\le z_K}
 \sum_{k=1}^K w_k(\widehat q_{k,t}-z_k)^2,\qquad w_k=1.
\label{eq:supp_monotone_projection}
\end{equation}
Projection is not sorting \citep{BarlowBrunk1972IsotonicDual,chernozhukov2010},
nor is projecting summaries equivalent to averaging projected draws.
Before ordering each scored grid, count negative adjacent gaps
\(g_{k,t}=(\beta_{0k}-\beta_{0,k-1})+
\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top(\vect\beta_k-\vect\beta_{k-1})\)
over the stated opportunities, with mean and maximum absolute adjustments.
GloFAS quantiles remain unadjusted; score weights differ from adjustment weights.

The finite-grid score is twice the weighted check loss from main Section 4:
\begin{equation}
\aCRPS_{p_1:p_K}(y;\vect q^*)
=2\sum_{k=1}^K\omega_k\rho_{p_k}(y-q_k^*),\qquad
\rho_p(e)=e\{p-\ind(e<0)\}.
\label{eq:supp-acrps}
\end{equation}
The multiquantile simulation uses
\((\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_7)=(0.025,0.10,0.20,0.25,0.20,0.10,0.025)\).
Weights sum to 0.90 without renormalization; GloFAS has the same endpoints
but a different interior grid. Compare within studies. Oracle scores average
expected check loss over independent DGP futures conditional on each observed
origin history, with common DGP paths across regressions. Means and equal-tailed
95\% limits describe draw-wise scores conditional on the fitted series,
selected features and averaged design, not future-response intervals.
Model contrasts are descriptive under their declared draw coupling, not
a joint posterior. Scoring a posterior-mean grid differs from averaging
scores. GloFAS scores realized observations.

For central interval \([l,u]\) with noncoverage \(\alpha\) and
\((a)_+=\max(a,0)\), the interval-score relation
\citep{BracherEtAl2021WIS} gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
\operatorname{IS}_\alpha(l,u;y)=(u-l)+\frac{2}{\alpha}
 \{(l-y)_++(y-u)_+\},\\
\rho_{\alpha/2}(y-l)+\rho_{1-\alpha/2}(y-u)
 =\frac\alpha2\operatorname{IS}_\alpha(l,u;y),\\
\aCRPS=.25|y-q_{.50}|+.0025\operatorname{IS}_{.10}
 +.020\operatorname{IS}_{.20}+.100\operatorname{IS}_{.50}.
\end{gathered}
\]
The last combination differs from conventional WIS, twice the unweighted
mean check loss on the symmetric grid.

\label{subsec:supp-independent-generating-design}

For observation-error family \(f\), the six-dimensional state
\(\vect\theta_{t,f}\) contains a local level, slope, and two sine--cosine
harmonic pairs. The evolution matrix \(\mat G_\theta\) combines a local
linear trend with seasonal period 90 and harmonics 1 and 2, following the
dynamic linear model construction of \citet{WestHarrison1997}. The
observation vector is \(\vect F=(1,0,1,0,1,0)^\top\), so
\(\mu_{t,f}=\vect F^\top\vect\theta_{t,f}\). Independent Gaussian state
disturbances have covariance \(\mat W_\theta\), with
\[
\text{State disturbance scales:}\quad
\sqrt{\diag(\mat W_\theta)}
=(0.005,0.00002,0.004,0.004,0.003,0.003).
\]
Each state trajectory starts deterministically at its family-specific level,
slope, and harmonic coordinates. For amplitude \(a_j\) and phase
\(\phi_j\), those coordinates are
\((a_j\cos\phi_j,a_j\sin\phi_j)\). The stored initial covariance
\(\mat C_0=0.01\Id{6}\) belongs to the model construction; it is not used
to sample the generating initial state.

The unshifted observation error \(e_{t,f}\) has distribution function
\(H_f\). At each level \(p\in\{0.05,0.25,0.50\}\), the response is
\(y_{t,p,f}=\mu_{t,f}+e_{t,f}-H_f^{-1}(p)\). Thus the shifted error has
\(p\)-quantile zero and the state location is the conditional quantile.
Within a family, both the latent trajectory and the unshifted errors are
shared across levels; only the centering constant changes.

\subsection{Single-quantile posterior metric summaries}
\label{subsec:supp-independent-metrics}

Let \(q_{p,t}^{(b)}\) be retained draw \(b\) of the conditional
\(p\)-quantile at fitting time \(t\), and
\(q_{p,\ell}^{(b)}\) its forecast counterpart for origin--horizon pair
\(\ell\). With \(N_{\mathrm{fit}}\) fitting observations and
\(N_{\mathrm{eval}}\) forecast pairs, root mean squared error (RMSE), mean
absolute error (MAE), and check loss are calculated draw-wise as
\begin{align}
\text{Fit RMSE:}\quad R_{\mathrm{fit}}^{(b)}
  &=\left\{\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{fit}}}\sum_{t=1}^{N_{\mathrm{fit}}}
    \bigl(q_{p,t}^{(b)}-\mu_t\bigr)^2\right\}^{1/2}, \\
\text{Forecast MAE:}\quad A_{\mathrm{fore}}^{(b)}
  &=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{eval}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{eval}}}
    \left|q_{p,\ell}^{(b)}-\mu_\ell\right|, \\
\text{Forecast check loss:}\quad C_{\mathrm{fore}}^{(b)}
  &=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{eval}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{eval}}}
    \rho_p\!\left(y_\ell-q_{p,\ell}^{(b)}\right).
\end{align}
Here \(\mu_\ell\) and \(y_\ell\) are the state-conditional quantile and
realized response for pair \(\ell\), and \(\rho_p\) is the check loss
defined in Section~\ref{sec:forecast_synthesis}. Fit RMSE and forecast MAE
measure recovery of the state-conditional path, which need not equal the
predictive quantile after integrating over future states. Check loss scores
the forecast against realized observations.

For each criterion \(M^{(b)}\), the summaries give the mean
\(B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^{B}M^{(b)}\) and empirical 0.025 and 0.975 quantiles
over \(B\) retained draws. Active forecast figures report MCMC; archived VB
fitting summaries use variational draws but do not support forecast
intervals for either evaluation period. Nonlinear draw-wise means generally
differ from scoring a single posterior point path.
Q--DESN uses fitting-sample preprocessing, and ridge fits are omitted.

The Q--DESN specifications are criterion-specific and may differ between
metrics and inference methods. Historical refinements retained configurations
with lower forecast MAE or check loss on the evaluation series. The
subsequent posterior metric calculations reuse those selected configurations;
they do not constitute a new, untouched-test evaluation of the selection
procedure. The displayed intervals condition on the selected specification,
simulated data, evaluation design, and reservoir realization, excluding
repeated-simulation and selection uncertainty. Comparisons of MCMC and VB
are therefore not controlled comparisons of approximation error.

\subsection{Single-quantile computation and diagnostics}
\label{subsec:supp-independent-exal-mcmc-details}

The single-level Q--DESN exAL--RHS sampler uses the \(v\) augmentation
and the collapsed shape--scale option in Algorithm~\ref{alg:qdesn_mcmc}:
update shape on its logit-transformed support with scale integrated out,
then immediately draw the conditional scale.

The exDQLM baseline refresh used \pkg{exdqlm} version 1.1.1
\citep{DeLeonEtAl2026Exdqlm}; inherited Q--DESN fits identify version 1.0.0
and were not refreshed. Unrestricted exDQLM uses the collapsed shape--scale
MCMC transition and the structured variational factor
\(q(\gamma)q(\sigma\mid\gamma)\).
For retained pairs \((\sigma_b,\gamma_b)\),
\(b=1,\ldots,B_{\mathrm{post}}\), let
\[
m_{\varepsilon,b}=\sigma_b\{A_p(\gamma_b)+D_p(\gamma_b)\sqrt{2/\pi}\},\qquad
V_{\varepsilon,b}=\sigma_b^2\{B_p(\gamma_b)+A_p(\gamma_b)^2
  +D_p(\gamma_b)^2(1-2/\pi)\}.
\]
The rolling-origin filter uses
\(\bar m_\varepsilon=B_{\mathrm{post}}^{-1}\sum_b m_{\varepsilon,b}\) and
\(\bar V_\varepsilon=B_{\mathrm{post}}^{-1}
\sum_b(V_{\varepsilon,b}+m_{\varepsilon,b}^2)-\bar m_\varepsilon^2\).
The parameter posterior from the initial fit is retained, while observations
available at successive origins update the state filter before forecasts are
propagated over the requested horizons.

Each of the three independently initialized MCMC chains uses 5,000 burn-in
iterations followed by 20,000 sampling iterations, without sampler thinning.
The metric calculations select aligned subsets of 4,000 draws per chain,
giving 12,000 pooled evaluations. The Q--DESN AL--RHS Gaussian \(p=0.05\)
forecast MAE and check-loss summaries instead use 1,000 draws per chain,
giving 3,000 pooled evaluations. These scoring subsets are distinct from
the complete retained samples. Diagnostics assess between-chain agreement
for each draw-wise criterion.

The selected single-level VB fits allow at most 300 iterations. Their
variational objectives are defined in Section~\ref{sec:elbo}.
Recorded elapsed times include forecasting and preparation of the metric
summaries, so they do not isolate the fitting-time difference between VB
and MCMC.

\subsection{Multiquantile MCMC evaluation details}
\label{subsec:supp-mq-computation}

One scenario-specific DESN/RHS specification is common to the four
regressions. The readout contains every reservoir layer and an intercept;
response lags enter only through reservoir inputs. The evaluation realization
is used for final fitting and evaluation. Chains within each model and
mechanism have identical data, likelihood and priors, differing only in
starts supplied through Figure~\ref{fig:supp-quantile-sequential-initialization}.
Every exAL chain uses the exact collapsed shape--scale transition.
Intercept prior centers and scales are calibrated once from the Gaussian
reference fit and held common across the four regressions and all chains;
MQ--DESN intercepts are ordered. The first quantile slope anchors the RHS hierarchy,
with no additional latent baseline.

Each model uses five MCMC chains. Under AL, each chain runs for 4,000
iterations, discards the first 1,000 and retains every fourth remaining
draw, giving 750 draws per chain and 3,750 pooled draws. Under exAL,
8,000 iterations with 2,000 discarded and the same thinning interval give
1,500 retained draws per chain. The exAL score calculations use pooled
subsets of 3,750 draws, except for both exAL regressions under the Laplace
mechanism, which use all 7,500 draws. Retained sampling allocations and
the subsets used for scoring are therefore reported separately.

Writing \(q_G\) for the Gaussian RHS variational reference, the fixed
calibration quantities are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Reference residual scale:}\quad
&s_G=\sqrt{\E_{q_G}(\sigma_G^2)},\\
\text{Intercept prior center:}\quad
&b_{0k}=\E_{q_G}(\beta_{0G})+s_G\Phi^{-1}(p_k),\\
\text{Intercept prior variance:}\quad
&V_{0k}=(2s_G)^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp-joint-intercept-calibration}
\end{equation}
These data-calibrated priors are not the Gaussian initializer's separate
intercept variance \(10^6\), nor are they chain-specific starting values.
The \(\exAL\) variational initializers use the \(v\) augmentation and the
structured factor \(q(\gamma_k)q(\sigma_k\mid\gamma_k)\) in
Section~\ref{subsec:supp_structured_exal_vb}. Scale integration is analytic;
quadrature over the bounded shape support supplies its normalization and
mixed moments.

\subsection{Streamflow reference--discrepancy model}
\label{sec:glofas_discrepancy}

Historical GloFAS reference-only, discrepancy-only and combined regressions
produce 30-day recursive paths; the issued-ensemble correction instead fits
a latent 28-day reference path using individual issued members
\citep{AlfieriEtAl2013GloFAS,HarriganEtAl2023GloFASForecasts}. Both comparisons

baselines; raw GloFAS is added in the issued window. Two distinct fixed
DESNs supply reference and discrepancy predictors.

\subsubsection{Observed quantities and latent design}
\label{subsec:glofas_aug_design}

The reference is daily streamflow measured by the United States Geological
Survey (USGS) at gauge 11160500 on the San Lorenzo
River at Big Trees, California. The historical GloFAS series is the v3.1
LISFLOOD consolidated product. Discharge values are expressed in
\(\mathrm{m}^3/\mathrm{s}\) before transformation. Let \(T\) denote
25~December~2022 and let \(y_t=\log(y_t^{\mathrm{USGS}}+1)\) be transformed
reference streamflow.
Write \(g_t^{\mathrm{ret}}\) for transformed retrospective GloFAS and
\(g_{T+h,e}^{\mathrm{ens}}\) for transformed issued member \(e\) at horizon
\(h\). The three historical models have a 30-day recursive horizon, ending
on 24~January~2023. For issued-ensemble correction, the latent future path
\(\vect y_F=(y_{T+1},\ldots,y_{T+28})^\top\) ends on 22~January~2023,
matching the available issued ensemble. All 51 members are used at each
horizon. The withheld USGS values are excluded from fitting and retained
separately for scoring. Forecast-period precipitation and soil-moisture
covariates use realized values from the Parameter-elevation Regressions on
Independent Slopes Model (PRISM) and the fifth-generation reanalysis (ERA5)
of the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF). The
reference-only model retains 12,455 historical dates from
19~November~1988 through \(T\), after a 540-day initial-history exclusion.
The discrepancy-only design also contains 12,455 historical dates;
the combined historical model uses reference and retrospective-product
observations at those 12,455 dates, giving 24,910 stacked observations.

With reference coefficients \(\vect\beta_k\) and discrepancy coefficients
\(\vect\delta_k\), the locations in main Section~6 satisfy
\begin{equation}
q_{k,t}^{\mathrm{glo}}=q_{k,t}+d_{k,t},\qquad
q_{k,t}=\vect x_t^{\mathrm{ref}\top}\vect\beta_k,\quad
d_{k,t}=\vect x_t^{\mathrm{disc}\top}\vect\delta_k.
\label{eq:supp_glofas_quantile_decomposition}
\end{equation}
The additive relation specifies a quantile-indexed location adjustment;
it is not a general identity between the quantiles of a difference and
those of its two components. MQ--DESN links adjacent quantile-specific
coefficients separately within the reference and discrepancy components.
Historical reference predictors use the observed gauge history and weather
covariates. The discrepancy predictors use weather and the history of
\(a_t=g_t^{\mathrm{ret}}-y_t\). This observed difference supplies inputs;
it is not an additional independent response in the combined likelihood.

Beyond \(T\), the missing gauge values are needed to continue the reference
reservoir. They also enter the discrepancy history, whose future inputs use
the ensemble center minus the candidate reference value:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Ensemble center:}\quad
\bar g_{T+j}^{\mathrm{ens}}
&=\frac1{51}\sum_{e=1}^{51}g_{T+j,e}^{\mathrm{ens}},\\
\text{Discrepancy input:}\quad
a_{T+j}(\vect y_F)&=\bar g_{T+j}^{\mathrm{ens}}-y_{T+j}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_glofas_future_discrepancy_input}
\end{equation}
The center averages the transformed member values. Only indices \(j<h\)
enter the design row for \(T+h\): both reservoirs depend on earlier latent
values but not on \(y_{T+h}\). The ensemble center is a predictor summary;
all 51 issued members still enter their individual likelihood factors.

For one probability level, stack the reference and discrepancy coefficients as
\[
\vect\theta_k=
\begin{bmatrix}\vect\beta_k\\ \vect\delta_k\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\vect h_t^{\mathrm{ref}}=
\begin{bmatrix}\vect x_t^{\mathrm{ref}}\\ \vect 0\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\vect h_t^{\mathrm{glo}}=
\begin{bmatrix}\vect x_t^{\mathrm{ref}}\\
\vect x_t^{\mathrm{disc}}\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Then \(\vect h_t^{\mathrm{ref}\top}\vect\theta_k=q_{k,t}\) and
\(\vect h_t^{\mathrm{glo}\top}\vect\theta_k=q_{k,t}+d_{k,t}\).
Conditional on \(\vect y_F\), these are ordinary fixed-design Q--DESN
regressions. The product observations couple the two coefficient blocks in
the posterior even when their priors are specified separately.

and every discrepancy component of the combined historical model. The
discrepancy-only Gaussian DESN (RHS) baseline instead learns its slab under
\(\IG(2,4)\).
Intercepts are exempt from RHS shrinkage. These fixed historical
\(\tau_0\) hyperparameters differ from learned effective global scales,
\(\{\E_q(\tau^{-2})\}^{-1/2}\). Their complete 24-row catalogue by family,
level and adjacent-quantile block remains in the
\href{https://github.com/AntonioAPDL/Article-Q-DESN---Version-2/blob/a9220a25e978b50180beabe3e243bcab963f2ca1/tables/glofas_reference_discrepancy_priors_display.tex}{public historical-prior table},
indexed in the computational-materials archive.

The three historical Gaussian DESN (ridge) fits use coefficient prior variance
\(10^4\) and intercept variance \(10^6\). The sixteen issued-window
quantile configurations instead fix
\(\tau_{0,\mathrm{ref}}=5.2043087\times10^{-5}\) and
\(\tau_{0,\mathrm{disc}}=1.159\times10^{-4}\), learn both slabs under
\(\IG(2,4)\), use intercept precision \(10^{-9}\), and assign each
source scale \(\IG(2,1)\). These issued priors, authenticated against
the frozen task-configuration manifest in the computational-materials
archive, are not the historical fixed-discrepancy-slab specification or
the starting moments transferred between fits. Gaussian baseline priors
remain those of their separately authenticated configurations; raw GloFAS
has no fitted coefficient prior.

\subsubsection{Issued-member likelihood and augmentation}
\label{subsec:glofas_likelihoods}

For source \(c\in\{Y,G\}\), let \(z_i^c\) be an included response,
\(\vect h_i^c\) its stacked design row and \(\vect b_k=\vect\theta_k\)
the reference and discrepancy coefficient vector. Specialize
Appendix~A's augmentation at level \(k\) by
\begin{equation}
(y_t,\vect x_t,\vect\beta,\sigma,\gamma)
\longmapsto(z_i^c,\vect h_i^c,\vect b_k,\sigma_{c,k},\gamma_{c,k}),
\label{eq:supp_glofas_al_hierarchy}
\end{equation}

Write \(L_{H,k}\) for the historical likelihood and \(\Theta_k\) for
the coefficients, scales, and shape parameters at level \(p_k\).
Historical and issued GloFAS contributions share their source-specific
scale and shape parameters. At each horizon, the 51 issued members are
modeled as conditionally independent given the regression parameters,
the level-specific future reference path
\(\vect y_{F,k}=(y_{T+1,k},\ldots,y_{T+28,k})^\top\), and preceding input
histories. Set
\(\mu^G_{k,h}=q_{k,T+h}+d_{k,T+h}\), and write \(D\) for the historical
observations and issued ensemble. The quantile-regression target is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Future-reference distribution:}\quad
L_{F,k}
&=\prod_{h=1}^{28}f_{p_k}\!\left(y_{T+h,k}\mid
q_{k,T+h},\sigma_{Y,k},\gamma_{Y,k}\right),\\
\text{Target:}\quad
\pi_k(\Theta_k,\vect y_{F,k}\mid D)
&\propto \pi_k(\Theta_k)L_{H,k}\,
 L_{F,k}\\
&\quad\times\prod_{h=1}^{28}\prod_{e=1}^{51}
 f_{p_k}\!\left(g^{\mathrm{ens}}_{T+h,e}\mid
 \mu^G_{k,h},\sigma_{G,k},\gamma_{G,k}\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_glofas_unit_member_target}
\end{equation}
Here \(L_{F,k}=L_{F,k}(\vect y_{F,k}\mid\Theta_k)\) is the conditional
future-reference density and \(f_{p_k}\) is the AL or exAL density. The dependence of both
locations on earlier path values and supplied weather covariates is
implicit. The future-reference distribution uses the same source-specific scale
and shape as the historical reference likelihood. Its normalization follows
by integrating the sequential conditional densities backwards from the
last horizon: each predictor depends on earlier responses only. Equivalently,
the transformation from future responses to their conditional residuals has
a triangular Jacobian with unit diagonal. No replacement Gaussian path
prior is introduced. The issued forecasts are available at \(T\), although
their target dates are later; they update the parameters and latent path
jointly. Future USGS observations remain excluded from this target.
In MQ--DESN, the joint coefficient prior replaces
the separate priors and multiplies the seven level-specific historical,
future-reference, and issued-member factors. Each level retains its own path
\(\vect y_{F,k}\).

Every included historical observation and issued member contributes one
density factor. In particular, the issued ensemble contributes 1,428
factors per level, rather than a total weight of 28. This conditional
independence assumption treats members as replicate information; it does
not account for additional residual dependence among ensemble members.
The 28 future-reference factors describe unobserved responses in the
sequential model; they are not future gauge observations supplied to fitting.
Earlier fits transfer compatible starting moments only. The destination
priors and posterior target remain fixed.

The substitution in Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_glofas_al_hierarchy} uses the
\(v\) augmentation in Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_exal_hierarchy}; AL sets
\(\gamma_{c,k}=0\) and omits \(s_{i,k}^c\). Integrating its auxiliary
variables gives each ordinary source density.

The source counts, including latent future-reference responses, are
\begin{equation}
n_Y=12{,}455+28=12{,}483,\qquad
n_G=12{,}455+28\times51=13{,}883.
\label{eq:supp_glofas_source_counts}
\end{equation}
Suppress \(k\), let \(\mathcal I_c\) index those factors, and set
\(r_i=z_i^c-\vect h_i^{c\top}\vect b_k\).
For \(\sigma_c\sim\IG(a_c,b_c)\), Appendix~A's AL conditionals use
\begin{equation}
T=n_c,\qquad (a_\sigma,b_\sigma)=(a_c,b_c),\qquad
\{r_t,v_t\}_{t=1}^T=\{r_i,v_i\}_{i\in\mathcal I_c}.
\label{eq:supp_glofas_unit_al_updates}
\end{equation}
The variational analogues are Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_generic_vb_al_latents}
with \(T=n_c\), taking residual moments over both coefficients and future
path. The future-reference factors contribute to \(n_Y\) although their
responses are unobserved.

For exAL, source-specific \(\gamma_c\) determines \(A_\gamma,B_\gamma,
D_\gamma\). The scale conditional has GIG parameters
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\nu_c&=-a_c-3n_c/2,\\
\chi_c&=2b_c+2\sum_i v_i+
 \sum_i(r_i-A_\gamma v_i)^2/(B_\gamma v_i),\\
\psi_c&=\frac{D_\gamma^2}{B_\gamma}\sum_i s_i^2/v_i.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_glofas_unit_exal_updates}
\end{equation}
Sums are over \(\mathcal I_c\). Apply Appendix A's structured scale--shape
factor separately for each source, substituting \(n_c\) and the residual
moments from the coefficient/path factors. The source-specific shape
normalizers and cross terms are retained, including intercept uncertainty
in the residual moments.

For coefficients, add to the two-block prior precision and linear term
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mat P_{\mathrm{data}}
 &=\sum_c\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}
 \frac{\vect h_i^c\vect h_i^{c\top}}{\sigma_cB_\gamma v_i},\\
\vect\eta_{\mathrm{data}}
 &=\sum_c\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}
 \frac{\vect h_i^c(z_i^c-A_\gamma v_i-\sigma_cD_\gamma s_i)}
 {\sigma_cB_\gamma v_i}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_glofas_unit_coefficient_precision}
\end{equation}
The Gaussian update then follows Appendix A. AL sets \(D_\gamma=0\).
VB takes the indicated moments, retaining reference--discrepancy
coefficient covariance and the response--design moment below.
For the Normal comparators, replace all source densities by Normal
densities and set
\begin{equation}
q(\sigma_c^2)=\IG\!\left(a_c+n_c/2,\,
 b_c+\tfrac12\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}\E_q(r_i^2)\right).
\label{eq:supp_glofas_unit_normal_update}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{MQ--DESN and latent-path approximation}
\label{subsec:glofas_latent_path}

The fitted grid is
\[
(0.05,\ 0.20,\ 0.35,\ 0.50,\ 0.65,\ 0.80,\ 0.95).
\]
For the MQ--DESN families, the seven reference coefficient blocks and seven
discrepancy coefficient blocks are linked by separate adjacent-quantile RHS
priors. Computation uses mean-field quantile-block coordinate updates
initialized from the completed independent fits. The construction shares
information through these priors; it is not a single exact Gaussian update
over every coefficient and latent-path block. Independent families fit each
level separately.

Each probability level has its own variational coefficient and future-path
factors. The coefficient factor retains the covariance between the
reference and discrepancy coefficients at that level. The path update
uses both the future-reference factors and issued-member factors in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_glofas_unit_member_target}; it does not import a
future-path prior from a Gaussian reference fit.

At each coordinate update, the reservoir design is linearized around the
current path mean. Let \(\widetilde\ell_{F,k}\) denote the resulting
quadratic expected augmented log density, with the other variational
factors held fixed. The local Gaussian update gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Latent-path factor:}\quad
q_{F,k}(\vect y_{F,k})&\approx\Normal(\vect m_k,\mat V_k),\\
\text{Local precision:}\quad
\mat V_k^{-1}&=-\nabla^2\widetilde\ell_{F,k}+\epsilon\mat I_{28}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_glofas_future_path_factor}
\end{equation}
The numerical precision stabilizer is \(\epsilon=10^{-8}\), and the MQ
coefficient updates retain a \(10^{-12}\) ridge floor. These are recorded
numerical regularizations, not a separately specified Gaussian future distribution.
For design row \(\vect h_i^c(\vect y_{F,k})\), let \(\mat J_i\) be its
Jacobian at the current mean, and suppress \(k\) in the next display. The
first-order moments propagated to the remaining updates are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Propagated design moments:}\quad
\E\{\vect h_i^c(\vect y_F)\}
&\approx \vect a_i^c,\\
\E\{\vect h_i^c\vect h_i^{c\top}\}
&\approx \vect a_i^c\vect a_i^{c\top}
+\mat J_i\mat V\mat J_i^\top,\\
\text{Response--design moment:}\quad
\E\{\vect h_i^c(\vect y_F)y_{T+h}\}
&\approx\vect a_i^c m_h+\mat J_i\mat V\vect e_h.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_glofas_future_path_delta}
\end{equation}
Here \(\vect a_i^c=\vect h_i^c(\vect m)\), and \(\vect e_h\) is the
\(h\)th coordinate vector. In particular, future-reference updates retain
the covariance between a latent response and its predictor; they do not
replace this moment by a product of separate means. Coefficient covariance
also enters the quadratic path update. Repeated first-order updates give
a local Gaussian--Delta approximation to the nonlinear posterior.
Held-out USGS measurements do not enter the latent-path approximation,
and no post-hoc crossing correction is applied.

The forecast at each level is the corresponding marginal response quantile
of its ensemble-conditioned path factor:
\begin{equation}
\text{Reported forecast:}\quad
\widehat q_{p_k,h}=m_{k,h}+\Phi^{-1}(p_k)\sqrt{V_{k,hh}}.
\label{eq:supp_glofas_reporting_operator}
\end{equation}
The Gaussian comparisons use the same analytic marginal-quantile
rule for their response-path factors. No additional observation error is
added to a factor that already describes the future response. The
probability-indexed regressions have separate path distributions; their
assembled quantiles need not be ordered even when each fit has converged.
These are approximate response quantiles, not posterior credible limits
for a quantile functional. On the original flow scale, each reported
quantile is inverse-transformed with \(\exp(\widehat q_{p_k,h})-1\).

The historical curves in the main figure summarize a different quantity.
For AL and exAL, the fitted conditional quantile is
\(\vect h_t^{\mathrm{ref}\top}\E_q(\vect\theta_k)\), with zero
discrepancy columns in \(\vect h_t^{\mathrm{ref}}\). For a Gaussian fit,
the mean conditional quantile is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Historical Gaussian curve:}\quad
\bar q_{p,t}^{\mathrm{fit}}
&=\vect h_t^{\mathrm{ref}\top}\E_q(\vect\theta)
  +\Phi^{-1}(p)\E_q(\sigma_Y),\\
\text{Mean conditional scale:}\quad
\E_q(\sigma_Y)
&=\sqrt{b_Y^\star}\,
\frac{\Gamma(a_Y^\star-1/2)}{\Gamma(a_Y^\star)},
\qquad a_Y^\star>1/2,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_glofas_historical_quantiles}
\end{equation}
where \(q(\sigma_Y^2)=\IG(a_Y^\star,b_Y^\star)\). This moment is not
\(\sqrt{\E_q(\sigma_Y^2)}\). Historical curves are fitted conditional
quantiles on the transformed scale, not historical posterior-predictive
quantiles or credible bands.

\subsection{Electricity-price benchmark}
\label{subsec:supp_pricefm_diagnostics}

Each of the 38 PriceFM regions has one fixed specification and seven
univariate VB quantile regressions per fold; neighboring predictors do not
define a joint regional posterior. Reservoir and RHS-scale selection uses
three expanding validation periods within original Fold~1 training:
September--December~2023, January--April~2024 and May--August~2024.
Outer Fold~1 validation, September--December~2024, selects AL or exAL.
The specification is then fixed across all three test folds, with no
test-driven changes or substitutions from the earlier case-specific
comparison. Those calendar periods had been examined previously:
prospective selection describes the present rule, not unseen periods.

periods. Training begins on 1~January~2022 and ends at the start of each
validation period. All intervals are half-open. Dates use the released UTC

calendar. Each daily test origin has 96 quarter-hourly targets.

Historical inputs contain price, load, solar and wind for the target region.
Selected spatial specifications either concatenate the corresponding
neighboring-region histories or add their means, with or without standard
deviations; other specifications use the target region alone. These histories
can include neighboring prices as well as exogenous variables. Selected lag
windows contain 48, 96, 168, or 240 quarter-hourly observations. Predictor and
response scales are estimated separately using training-only median and
interquartile-range scaling.

The future covariates are the released load, solar and wind columns, identified
as day-ahead forecasts by \citet{YuEtAl2026PriceFM}, rather than their future
realizations. Their availability at each forecast origin was not separately
verified, so the comparison does not establish operational information parity.
At each origin, the fixed DESN maps the observed regional histories to a
reservoir representation. Each horizon's predictors combine the final-layer
state, the corresponding released covariate forecasts, and horizon features:
a scaled horizon, sine and cosine phase, and horizon indicators. An intercept
completes the design. The quantile forecasts evaluate the fitted variational
coefficient means directly at these origin--horizon predictors. Future price
responses are not simulated and fed into subsequent horizons in this analysis.
Forecasts are mapped back to EUR/MWh by reversing the training-only response
transformation before evaluation.
The 38 selected specifications use AL in 11 regions and exAL in 27.

Q--DESN has lower AQL in 16 of the 38 regional means. Aggregate and
fold-level comparisons, including the higher Q--DESN aggregate loss,
are reported in the main article.
