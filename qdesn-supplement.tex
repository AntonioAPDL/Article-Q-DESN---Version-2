\subsection{Conventions and prior factors}
\label{sec:dist_conventions}

For \(T\) fitting observations and \(r\) penalized slopes, write
\(\vect x_t=(1,\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top)^\top\) and
\(\vect\beta=(\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_r)^\top\). The feature matrix
\(\mat X\) is fixed during estimation. The distributional conventions are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{Inverse gamma:}\quad
 p(x\mid a,b)&=b^a x^{-a-1}\exp(-b/x)/\Gamma(a),\qquad x>0,\\
\text{GIG:}\quad
 p(x\mid\lambda,\chi,\psi)&\propto
 x^{\lambda-1}\exp\{-\tfrac12(\chi/x+\psi x)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The positive-truncated Normal \(\TN(m,V)\) has underlying Normal location
\(m\) and variance \(V\); \(\Phi\) and \(\phi\) denote the standard
Normal distribution and density functions.

At target level \(p_0\), the exAL constants in the main article's
augmented likelihood are determined by
\[
\begin{aligned}
g(\gamma)&=2\Phi(-|\gamma|)\exp(\gamma^2/2),\qquad
p_\gamma=\ind\{\gamma<0\}
 +\frac{p_0-\ind\{\gamma<0\}}{g(\gamma)},\\
A_\gamma&=\frac{1-2p_\gamma}{p_\gamma(1-p_\gamma)},\qquad
B_\gamma=\frac{2}{p_\gamma(1-p_\gamma)},\qquad
D_\gamma=\frac{|\gamma|}{\ind\{\gamma>0\}-p_\gamma}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The support is \(L<\gamma<U\), where \(L<0<U\),
\(g(L)=1-p_0\) and \(g(U)=p_0\). AL uses
\(A_0=(1-2p_0)/\{p_0(1-p_0)\}\),
\(B_0=2/\{p_0(1-p_0)\}\) and \(D_0=0\), and omits the positive
auxiliaries \(s_t\). Derivatives at nonzero shape stay within its sign
branch. Unless fixed, \(\sigma\sim\IG(a_\sigma,b_\sigma)\), with
positive hyperparameters, and exAL has a proper supported shape density
\(\pi_\gamma\).

For ridge, the coefficient prior is
\(\Normal(\vect b_\beta,\mat V_\beta)\), with positive-definite
\(\mat V_\beta\). For the Nishimura--Suchard (NS) construction, use the
auxiliary hierarchy in main Section 2.3 and retain its Gaussian product
mass. For positive variances \(a,b\),
\begin{equation}
\text{Product mass:}\quad
\Normal(\beta;0,a)\Normal(0;\beta,b)
=\Normal(0;0,a+b)\Normal\!\left(\beta;0,\frac{ab}{a+b}\right).
\label{eq:supp_ns_product_factor}
\end{equation}
Thus, with \(V_j=(\zeta^{-2}+\tau^{-2}\lambda_j^{-2})^{-1}\) and
\(\Theta_\beta=(\vect\lambda^2,\vect\nu,\tau^2,\xi,\zeta^2)\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\pi_{\mathrm{NS}}(\vect\beta,\Theta_\beta)
&\propto\Normal(\beta_0;b_0,V_0)\,p(\Theta_\beta)
 C_\beta(\Theta_\beta)\prod_{j=1}^r\Normal(\beta_j;0,V_j),\\
C_\beta(\Theta_\beta)
&=\prod_{j=1}^r\Normal(0;0,\tau^2\lambda_j^2+\zeta^2).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_ns_full_prior}
\end{equation}
Here \(p(\Theta_\beta)\) is the full product of the auxiliary and slab
densities in the main article. Omitting \(C_\beta\) gives a different
joint prior, despite the same conditional slope variances. For a fixed
slab, omit its prior and variational factor, not the Gaussian regularizing
mass. Main Section 2.3 gives the Gaussian reference for \(\tau_0\);
expanded reference and feature-centering calculations are retained in the
computational materials.

Let \(d_t=\sigma D_\gamma s_t+A_\gamma v_t\) and
\(\omega_t=\sigma B_\gamma v_t\). With \(\phi_1\) a Gaussian density
and \(\phi_+\) its positive-truncated version, the augmented target is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\pi(\cdot\mid\vect y,\mat X)&\propto
 p_h(\vect\beta,\Theta_\beta)p(\sigma)\pi_\gamma(\gamma)\\
&\quad\times\prod_t\{
 \phi_1(y_t;\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta+d_t,\omega_t)
 p(v_t\mid\sigma)\phi_+(s_t;0,1)\},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp-generic-augmented-target}
\end{equation}
where \(p(v_t\mid\sigma)\) is exponential with rate \(1/\sigma\), and
\(p_h\) is the ridge prior or Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_ns_full_prior}.
AL omits \(s_t,\gamma\) and their densities. All factors retain their
normalizers; the prior enters once, outside the observation product.

\subsection{MCMC full conditionals}
\label{sec:mcmc}

Set \(\vect y^\star=\vect y-\vect d\) and
\(\mat W=\diag(\omega_t^{-1})\). Ridge uses
\((\mat P,\vect b)=(\mat V_\beta^{-1},\vect b_\beta)\); NS uses
\(\vect b=(b_0,0,\ldots,0)^\top\) and
\(\mat P=\diag\{V_0^{-1},\tau^{-2}\lambda_j^{-2}+\zeta^{-2}\}_{j=1}^r\),
with the intercept entered once. The coefficient conditional is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Coefficients:}\quad
\mat\Sigma_{\beta\mid\cdot}&=(\mat X^\top\mat W\mat X+\mat P)^{-1},\\
\vect\beta\mid\cdot&\sim\Normal\!\left[
 \mat\Sigma_{\beta\mid\cdot}
 (\mat X^\top\mat W\vect y^\star+\mat P\vect b),
 \mat\Sigma_{\beta\mid\cdot}\right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_beta_mcmc}
\end{equation}
With \(\delta_t=y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta-\sigma D_\gamma s_t\),
the latent conditionals are
\begin{align}
\text{Exponential auxiliary:}\quad
v_t\mid\cdot&\sim\GIG\!\left(\tfrac12,
 \frac{\delta_t^2}{\sigma B_\gamma},
 \frac{A_\gamma^2/B_\gamma+2}{\sigma}\right),
\label{eq:supp_v_mcmc}\\
\text{Positive auxiliary:}\quad
s_t\mid\cdot&\sim\TN\!\left(
 \frac{D_\gamma(y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta-A_\gamma v_t)}
 {B_\gamma v_t+\sigma D_\gamma^2},
 \left[1+\frac{\sigma D_\gamma^2}{B_\gamma v_t}\right]^{-1}\right).
\label{eq:supp_s_mcmc}
\end{align}
AL omits the second line, takes \(\delta_t=y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta\),
and has scale conditional
\begin{equation}
\sigma\mid\cdot\sim\IG\!\left(a_\sigma+\frac{3T}{2},\,
 b_\sigma+\sum_t v_t+\frac{1}{2B_0}
 \sum_t\frac{(y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta-A_0v_t)^2}{v_t}\right).
\label{eq:supp_al_sigma_mcmc}
\end{equation}

For exAL, at the current coefficients and auxiliaries, define
\(r_{t,\gamma}=y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta-A_\gamma v_t\). The
scale--shape conditional kernel has parameters
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\nu&=-a_\sigma-3T/2,\qquad
\chi_\gamma=2b_\sigma+2\sum_t v_t+
 \frac1{B_\gamma}\sum_t r_{t,\gamma}^2/v_t,\\
\psi_\gamma&=\frac{D_\gamma^2}{B_\gamma}\sum_t s_t^2/v_t,\qquad
c_\gamma=\log\pi_\gamma(\gamma)-\tfrac T2\log B_\gamma
 +\frac{D_\gamma}{B_\gamma}\sum_t s_tr_{t,\gamma}/v_t,\\
\pi(\sigma,\gamma\mid\cdot)&\propto
 e^{c_\gamma}\sigma^{\nu-1}
 \exp\{-\tfrac12(\chi_\gamma/\sigma+\psi_\gamma\sigma)\},
 \quad\sigma>0,\ L<\gamma<U.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_sigmagam_joint_kernel}
\end{equation}
Hence \(\sigma\mid\gamma,\cdot\sim\GIG(\nu,\chi_\gamma,\psi_\gamma)\).
At fixed scale, the shape density is proportional to
\(e^{c_\gamma-(\chi_\gamma/\sigma+\psi_\gamma\sigma)/2}\) on its
support. A scale-collapsed shape transition instead uses
\begin{equation}
\text{Collapsed shape:}\quad
\pi(\gamma\mid\text{remaining blocks})\propto
 e^{c_\gamma}Z_\sigma(\nu,\chi_\gamma,\psi_\gamma)\ind\{L<\gamma<U\},
\label{eq:supp_collapsed_shape_kernel}
\end{equation}
with \(Z_\sigma\) defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_structured_scale_integral}.
Immediately draw scale from its GIG conditional before updating another
block. A transformed shape transition includes its Jacobian. These
conditional parameters use current variables, not the variational moments
defined below.

The NS shrinkage conditionals are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_j^2\mid\cdot&\sim\IG\!\left(1,\nu_j^{-1}
 +\frac{\beta_j^2}{2\tau^2}\right),&
\nu_j\mid\cdot&\sim\IG(1,1+\lambda_j^{-2}),\\
\tau^2\mid\cdot&\sim\IG\!\left(\frac{r+1}{2},\xi^{-1}
 +\frac12\sum_j\beta_j^2\lambda_j^{-2}\right),&
\xi\mid\cdot&\sim\IG(1,\tau_0^{-2}+\tau^{-2}),\\
\zeta^2\mid\cdot&\sim\IG\!\left(a_\zeta+\frac r2,
 b_\zeta+\frac12\sum_j\beta_j^2\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_rhs_mcmc}
\end{equation}
The global shape \((r+1)/2\) counts penalized slopes; the distinct slab
shape is \(a_\zeta+r/2\). Omit the last update for a fixed slab.

\begin{suppalgorithm}{Q--DESN and MQ--DESN MCMC}
\label{alg:qdesn_mcmc}
\emph{Input:} response and fixed features, probability levels, likelihood,
priors, supported starting values, iteration allocation and transition settings.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Update \(v_t\) and exAL \(s_t\) using
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:supp_v_mcmc}--\eqref{eq:supp_s_mcmc}, with the study's specified transitions.
\item Update coefficients by Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_beta_mcmc}; for MQ--DESN,
use the stacked slope and ordered-intercept updates in Appendix B.
\item Update NS scales by Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_rhs_mcmc}, applying Appendix
B's difference-block and shared-slab substitutions for MQ--DESN.
\item Update AL scale by Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_al_sigma_mcmc}, or use the
specified exAL joint, conditional, or collapsed transition above. A
collapsed shape update is immediately followed by its conditional scale draw.
\item Repeat for the iteration allocation; retain post-burn-in draws and
diagnostics, with thinning only where specified in Appendix D.
\end{enumerate}
\end{suppalgorithm}
Available conditional distributions do not determine every numerical
transition: the reported MQ AL fit samples its GIG variables on logarithmic
coordinates by slice sampling. Appendix D identifies the actual schemes.

\subsection{Variational factors and coordinate updates}
\label{sec:cavi}

The fixed-design variational family is
\begin{equation}
q=q_\beta(\vect\beta)q_\Theta(\Theta_\beta)
 q_{\sigma,\gamma}(\sigma,\gamma)\prod_t q_v(v_t)q_s(s_t),
\label{eq:supp_generic_vb_family}
\end{equation}
where \(q_\beta\) retains full covariance and
\begin{equation}
q_\Theta=q_{\tau^2}q_\xi q_{\zeta^2}
 \prod_j q_{\lambda_j^2}q_{\nu_j}.
\label{eq:supp_generic_vb_scale_family}
\end{equation}
The coefficient, \(v\), \(s\), and shrinkage factors are mutually
independent and independent of the joint scale--shape factor. AL omits
\(q_s,\gamma\); fixed ridge omits \(q_\Theta\). In coordinate ascent,
take expectations of conditional \emph{precision and linear coefficients},
not of conditional means and variances. With
\(\bar w_t=\E_q(\omega_t^{-1})\) and
\(\bar r_t=\E_q\{(y_t-d_t)/\omega_t\}\), the Gaussian update is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Coefficient factor:}\quad
\mat\Sigma_\beta&=(\mat X^\top\bar{\mat W}\mat X+
 \bar{\mat P})^{-1},\\
\vect m_\beta&=\mat\Sigma_\beta
 (\mat X^\top\bar{\vect r}+\bar{\vect h}),\qquad
 q_\beta=\Normal(\vect m_\beta,\mat\Sigma_\beta).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_generic_vb_beta}
\end{equation}
Here \(\bar{\mat W}=\diag(\bar w_t)\) and
\((\bar{\mat P},\bar{\vect h})=(\mat V_\beta^{-1},
\mat V_\beta^{-1}\vect b_\beta)\) for ridge. NS has intercept canonical
parameters \((V_0^{-1},V_0^{-1}b_0)\), zero slope linear coefficients,
and slope precisions \(\E_q(\tau^{-2})\E_q(\lambda_j^{-2})+
\E_q(\zeta^{-2})\).

Define residual moments
\(\bar e_t=y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect m_\beta\) and
\(e_t^{(2)}=\bar e_t^2+\vect x_t^\top\mat\Sigma_\beta\vect x_t\).
The exAL latent factors are \(q_v(v_t)=\GIG(1/2,\chi_t^v,\psi_t^v)\)
and \(q_s(s_t)=\TN(h_t^s/K_t^s,1/K_t^s)\), with
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\chi_t^v&=e_t^{(2)}\E_q\{(\sigma B_\gamma)^{-1}\}
 -2\bar e_t\E_q(D_\gamma/B_\gamma)\E_q(s_t)
 +\E_q(\sigma D_\gamma^2/B_\gamma)\E_q(s_t^2),\\
\psi_t^v&=\E_q\{A_\gamma^2/(\sigma B_\gamma)+2/\sigma\},\\
K_t^s&=1+\E_q(\sigma D_\gamma^2/B_\gamma)\E_q(v_t^{-1}),\\
h_t^s&=\bar e_t\E_q(D_\gamma/B_\gamma)\E_q(v_t^{-1})
 -\E_q(A_\gamma D_\gamma/B_\gamma).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_generic_vb_exal_latents}
\end{equation}
Every expectation involving both scale and shape is under their joint
factor. For \(S\sim\TN(m,V)\), its actual moments, with
\(a=m/\sqrt V\), are
\begin{equation}
\text{Truncated moments:}\quad
\E(S)=m+\sqrt V\frac{\phi(a)}{\Phi(a)},\qquad
\E(S^2)=m^2+V+m\sqrt V\frac{\phi(a)}{\Phi(a)}.
\label{eq:supp_truncated_normal_moments}
\end{equation}
In particular, \(\TN(0,1)\) has mean \(\sqrt{2/\pi}\), not zero,
and second moment one. AL omits \(s_t\), uses \(A_0,B_0,D_0=0\) in
the \(v\) update, and has
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
q_\sigma&=\IG(a_\sigma+3T/2,b_\sigma^\star),\\
b_\sigma^\star&=b_\sigma+\sum_t\E_q(v_t)+\frac1{2B_0}
 \sum_t\{e_t^{(2)}\E_q(v_t^{-1})-2A_0\bar e_t+A_0^2\E_q(v_t)\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_generic_vb_al_latents}
\end{equation}
The five NS factors retain the inverse-gamma families and shapes in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_rhs_mcmc}; their rates use
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_j^2&\mapsto M_j=m_{\beta,j}^2+(\mat\Sigma_\beta)_{jj},\\
\beta_j^2\tau^{-2}&\mapsto M_j\E_q(\tau^{-2}),\qquad
\beta_j^2\lambda_j^{-2}\mapsto M_j\E_q(\lambda_j^{-2}),\\
(\nu_j^{-1},\lambda_j^{-2},\xi^{-1},\tau^{-2})
&\mapsto\{\E_q(\nu_j^{-1}),\E_q(\lambda_j^{-2}),
 \E_q(\xi^{-1}),\E_q(\tau^{-2})\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_generic_vb_rhs}
\end{equation}
These products follow from the factor independence above. The global and
slab shapes remain \((r+1)/2\) and \(a_\zeta+r/2\), respectively.

\subsubsection{Structured exAL scale--shape calculation}
\label{subsec:supp_structured_exal_vb}

Write \(\bar v_t=\E_q(v_t)\), \(v_t^-=\E_q(v_t^{-1})\),
\(\bar s_t=\E_q(s_t)\) and \(s_t^{(2)}=\E_q(s_t^2)\). The coordinate
scale--shape factor has separately defined, expected kernel parameters
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar\nu&=-a_\sigma-3T/2,\\
\bar\chi_\gamma&=2b_\sigma+2\sum_t\bar v_t+
 \frac1{B_\gamma}\sum_t
 (e_t^{(2)}v_t^- -2A_\gamma\bar e_t+A_\gamma^2\bar v_t),\\
\bar\psi_\gamma&=\frac{D_\gamma^2}{B_\gamma}
 \sum_t s_t^{(2)}v_t^-,\\
\bar c_\gamma&=\log\pi_\gamma(\gamma)-\tfrac T2\log B_\gamma+
 \frac{D_\gamma}{B_\gamma}
 \sum_t\bar s_t(\bar e_tv_t^- -A_\gamma).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_structured_scale_shape_terms}
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}
q^*(\sigma,\gamma)\propto
 e^{\bar c_\gamma}\sigma^{\bar\nu-1}
 \exp\{-\tfrac12(\bar\chi_\gamma/\sigma+
 \bar\psi_\gamma\sigma)\}\ind\{\sigma>0,L<\gamma<U\}.
\label{eq:supp_structured_scale_shape_kernel}
\end{equation}
The GIG order \(\bar\nu\) is distinct from the NS auxiliaries \(\nu_j\).
For either the conditional MCMC kernel or this variational kernel, the
scale integral, with its own corresponding parameters, is
\begin{equation}
\text{Scale mass:}\quad
Z_\sigma(\nu,\chi,\psi)=
\begin{cases}
2(\chi/\psi)^{\nu/2}K_\nu(\sqrt{\chi\psi}),&\psi>0,\\
\Gamma(-\nu)(\chi/2)^\nu,&\psi=0,\ \nu<0.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:supp_structured_scale_integral}
\end{equation}
Here \(K_\nu\) is the modified Bessel function of the second kind;
\(\chi>0\) under the proper scale prior. Set
\(\bar Z_\sigma(\gamma)=
Z_\sigma(\bar\nu,\bar\chi_\gamma,\bar\psi_\gamma)\). Its
shape-dependent mass must remain in the normalized factor:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Z_\gamma&=\left(\int_L^0+\int_0^U\right)
 e^{\bar c_\gamma}\bar Z_\sigma(\gamma)\,d\gamma,\\
q(\gamma)&=e^{\bar c_\gamma}\bar Z_\sigma(\gamma)/Z_\gamma,\qquad
q(\sigma\mid\gamma)=\GIG(\bar\nu,\bar\chi_\gamma,\bar\psi_\gamma).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_structured_shape_factor}
\end{equation}
For integrable \(h\), one-dimensional quadrature gives
\begin{equation}
\E_q\{h(\sigma,\gamma)\}=\frac1{Z_\gamma}
 \left(\int_L^0+\int_0^U\right)
 e^{\bar c_\gamma}\bar Z_\sigma(\gamma)
 \E\{h(\sigma,\gamma)\mid\gamma\}\,d\gamma.
\label{eq:supp_structured_mixed_moments}
\end{equation}
For a GIG scale conditional with parameters \((\nu,\chi,\psi)\),
the inner power moments are
\begin{equation}
\E(\sigma^a\mid\gamma)=
\begin{cases}
(\chi/\psi)^{a/2}
 \dfrac{K_{\nu+a}(\sqrt{\chi\psi})}{K_\nu(\sqrt{\chi\psi})},
 &\psi>0,\\[4pt]
(\chi/2)^a\dfrac{\Gamma(-\nu-a)}{\Gamma(-\nu)},
 &\psi=0,\ \nu<0,\ a<-\nu.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:supp_structured_scale_moments}
\end{equation}
With positive \(\chi,\psi\), all real power moments exist; the second
case is the proper inverse-gamma limit. In the variational calculation,
insert the barred parameters. Mixed scale--shape moments cannot generally
be replaced by products of their separate expectations. Branchwise
numerical transformations include their integration Jacobian; quadrature
accuracy is assessed separately from scalar MCMC mixing.

\subsubsection{Laplace--Delta approximation and objectives}
\label{sec:elbo}

The alternative Laplace--Delta approximation
\citep{wang2013nonconjugatevb} transforms
\(\sigma=e^{\eta_1}\),
\(\gamma=L+(U-L)\expit(\eta_2)\), with
\(\expit(a)=\{1+e^{-a}\}^{-1}\). For this map \(T\),
\(J(\vect\eta)=\sigma(U-L)\expit(\eta_2)
\{1-\expit(\eta_2)\}\). Maximize the expected augmented log density
plus \(\log J\), and let \(\hat{\vect\eta}\) and \(\mat K_\eta\)
be its mode and negative Hessian. The approximation
\(q_\eta=\Normal(\hat{\vect\eta},\mat K_\eta^{-1})\) induces density
\(q_{\sigma,\gamma}=q_\eta\{T^{-1}(\sigma,\gamma)\}/
J\{T^{-1}(\sigma,\gamma)\}\), not an original-scale Gaussian. For
branchwise smooth \(h\), its Delta moment approximation is
\begin{equation}
\E_q h\approx h\{T(\hat{\vect\eta})\}
 +\tfrac12\tr\!\left[
 \nabla_\eta^2 h\{T(\hat{\vect\eta})\}\mat K_\eta^{-1}\right].
\label{eq:supp_generic_delta}
\end{equation}
For a continuous variational family and the complete augmented target
\(p\) in Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp-generic-augmented-target}, the objective is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\elbo(q)&=\E_q\log p+H(q)\\
&=\E_{q_uq_\eta}\log[p\{\vect y,\vect u,T(\vect\eta)\mid\mat X\}
 J(\vect\eta)]+H(q_u)+H(q_\eta),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_generic_elbo}
\end{equation}
\label{eq:supp_elbo_transformed_coordinates}
where \(\vect u\) contains the remaining variables. The Jacobian enters
exactly once. Approximate expectations need not yield a lower bound or a
monotone monitor. The reported MQ calculation optimizes ordered point
intercepts and monitors selected objective terms, not a full continuous
ELBO; point masses have no finite differential entropy. Appendices D and E
give the study-specific stopping rules and uncertainty scope.

\section{Multiquantile computation}
\label{sec:joint_qdesn_supp}

The main article defines the anchored differences for the \(K\) slope
vectors. Let \(\mat Z\) be the common non-intercept design and
\(\mat H\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}=\vect\Delta\) their stacked
first-difference transformation, with determinant one. Write
\(\mat D_\Delta=\diag(V^\Delta_{k,j})\), where
\(V^\Delta_{k,j}=\{\zeta_\Delta^{-2}+
(\tau_k^\Delta)^{-2}(\lambda_{k,j}^\Delta)^{-2}\}^{-1}\).
The conditional slope prior and retained NS mass are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
p(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}\mid\mat D_\Delta)
&\propto|\mat D_\Delta|^{-1/2}
 \exp\{-\tfrac12\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}^\top
 \mat H^\top\mat D_\Delta^{-1}\mat H\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}\},\\
C_\Delta(\Theta_\Delta)
&=\prod_{k,j}\Normal\{0;0,
 (\tau_k^\Delta)^2(\lambda_{k,j}^\Delta)^2+\zeta_\Delta^2\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_joint_qvp_prior}
\end{equation}
Use Appendix A's indexed constants and auxiliaries at each \(p_k\).
Stack \(d_{k,t}=\sigma_kD_ks_{k,t}+A_kv_{k,t}\), set
\(\mat\Omega=\diag(\sigma_kB_kv_{k,t})\), and write
\(\vect y_{\mathrm{st}}=\ones{K}\otimes\vect y\),
\(\mat Z_{\mathrm{st}}=\Id{K}\otimes\mat Z\), and
\(\vect\beta_{0,\mathrm{st}}=\vect\beta_0\otimes\ones{T}\).
Then
\begin{align}
L_{\mathrm{aug}}&=\phi_{KT}(\vect y_{\mathrm{st}};
 \vect\beta_{0,\mathrm{st}}+\mat Z_{\mathrm{st}}\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}
 +\vect d,\mat\Omega),
\label{eq:supp_joint_stacked_likelihood}\\
\pi(\cdot\mid\vect y,\mat Z)&\propto L_{\mathrm{aug}}p(\vect\beta_0)
 p(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}\mid\mat D_\Delta)
 C_\Delta(\Theta_\Delta)p(\Theta_\Delta)\nonumber\\
&\quad\times\prod_k\{p(\sigma_k)\pi_{\gamma_k}(\gamma_k)
 \prod_t p(v_{k,t}\mid\sigma_k)p(s_{k,t})\}.
\label{eq:supp_joint_complete_posterior}
\end{align}
The first line evaluates a Gaussian density at repeated observations; it
is not a nonsingular Gaussian sampling model for that vector. AL omits
\(s,\gamma\) and their factors. Normalizers and supported priors remain
in the augmented composite target.

With \(\vect r=\vect y_{\mathrm{st}}-\vect\beta_{0,\mathrm{st}}-\vect d\),
the slope conditional is Gaussian with
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Slope precision:}\quad
\mat K_\beta&=\mat Z_{\mathrm{st}}^\top\mat\Omega^{-1}
 \mat Z_{\mathrm{st}}+\mat H^\top\mat D_\Delta^{-1}\mat H,\\
\text{Conditional mean:}\quad
\vect m_\beta&=\mat K_\beta^{-1}\mat Z_{\mathrm{st}}^\top
 \mat\Omega^{-1}\vect r.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_joint_beta_conditional}
\end{equation}
Its covariance is \(\mat K_\beta^{-1}\). An intercept prior
\(\Normal(b_{0k},V_{0k})\) gives precision
\(K_{0k}=V_{0k}^{-1}+\sum_t\omega_{k,t}^{-1}\) and mean
\(K_{0k}^{-1}\{V_{0k}^{-1}b_{0k}+\sum_t\omega_{k,t}^{-1}
(y_t-d_{k,t}-\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top\vect\beta_k)\}\).
Ordered intercepts truncate this Normal between its neighbors, with
unbounded extreme endpoints.

The common algorithms apply indexed latent and likelihood updates at each
level. In Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_rhs_mcmc}, substitute
\((\beta_j,\lambda_j,\tau,\xi,\tau_0)\) by
\((\Delta_{k,j},\lambda^\Delta_{k,j},\tau_k^\Delta,\xi_k^\Delta,
\tau_{0,k}^\Delta)\); each global shape is \((r+1)/2\). A slab shared
across blocks has conditional
\(\IG(a_{\zeta_\Delta}+Kr/2,
b_{\zeta_\Delta}+\tfrac12\sum_{k,j}\Delta_{k,j}^2)\), unless fixed.
The variational slope update takes expected canonical terms in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_joint_beta_conditional} and retains cross-level
covariance. In particular,
\begin{equation}
\text{Difference moments:}\quad
\E_q(\vect\Delta\circ\vect\Delta)
=(\mat H\vect m)\circ(\mat H\vect m)
 +\diag(\mat H\mat\Sigma\mat H^\top).
\label{eq:supp_joint_increment_moments}
\end{equation}
Use these moments in the shrinkage rates. The reported simulation combines
this stacked slope covariance with ordered point intercepts and the
structured scale--shape factor; its variational intervals omit intercept
uncertainty. Appendix E gives the application's distinct quantile-block
approximation.

\section{Forecast construction and evaluation}
\label{sec:forecast_synthesis}

At origin \(o\), reconstruct states from observed history, without
refitting the parameter distribution. For a single level, retain one
parameter draw throughout each recursive trajectory:
\[
\text{Future response:}\quad
\tilde Y_{o+h}^{(b,p)}\sim\exAL_p\!\left(
 \vect x_{o+h}^{(b,p)\top}\vect\beta_p^{(b)},
 \sigma_p^{(b)},\gamma_p^{(b)}\right).
\]
Future features use observed lags through \(o\), then generated responses
and supplied covariates, with fresh errors at each horizon. AL fixes shape
at zero. Grid forecasts evaluate all levels on each trajectory's features,
order the grid by isotonic projection, and generate a response by linear
inverse-CDF interpolation at a uniform draw. Values outside
\([p_1,p_K]\) are clamped to the outer fitted quantiles. Independent fits
pair probability-level draws by prespecified within-chain permutations;
MQ--DESN retains joint-draw dependence.

The primary multiquantile score uses a different summary of these features:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Mean future features:}\quad
\bar{\vect x}_{o+h}&=M^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^M
 \tilde{\vect x}_{o+h}^{[j]},\\
\text{Scored location:}\quad
q_{o,h,k}^{(b)}&=\beta_{0k}^{(b)}+
 \bar{\vect x}_{o+h}^\top\vect\beta_k^{(b)}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp-forecast-mean-design}
\end{equation}
Here \([j]\) indexes trajectories, \(b\) scored coefficient draws, and
\(\bar{\vect x}\) excludes the intercept. The primary intervals condition
on this averaged design. The trajectory-specific sensitivity instead
pairs each coefficient draw with its recursively generated features.
These are different forecast functionals, not nested uncertainty bounds;
neither automatically gives quantiles of the mixture over future
histories. Single-level results assess conditional-quantile paths;
Appendix E specifies the distinct application forecasts.

For raw ordinates \(\widehat{\vect q}_t\), the simulation's ordering rule is
\begin{equation}
\text{Isotonic projection:}\quad
\vect q_t^*=\argmin_{z_1\le\cdots\le z_K}
 \sum_{k=1}^K(\widehat q_{k,t}-z_k)^2.
\label{eq:supp_monotone_projection}
\end{equation}
This equal-weight adjustment differs from sorting
\citep{BarlowBrunk1972IsotonicDual,chernozhukov2010} and from shrinkage
during estimation. Raw crossings are counted before projection;
projecting summaries need not equal averaging projected draws. GloFAS
quantiles remain unadjusted.

Main Section 4 defines check loss, finite-grid aCRPS and the oracle score.
The multiquantile grid uses trapezoidal weights
\((0.025,0.10,0.20,0.25,0.20,0.10,0.025)\), summing to 0.90 without
renormalization. These are not projection weights; GloFAS uses a different
interior probability grid. Oracle scores average loss over independent
DGP futures conditional on observed origin history, using common DGP
paths across regressions. Reported means and equal-tailed 95\% limits
summarize draw-wise aggregate scores conditional on the fitted series,
selected features and stated future design, not future-response bands.
Contrasts are descriptive under their declared draw coupling, not draws
from a joint posterior of competing models. Averaging scores also differs
from scoring a posterior-mean grid. GloFAS scores realized observations.

\subsection{Computation and reported uncertainty}
\label{subsec:supp-independent-metrics}
\label{subsec:supp-independent-exal-mcmc-details}
\label{subsec:supp-mq-computation}

In the multiquantile study, Gaussian ridge estimates initialize Gaussian
RHS--VB and then median AL. Independent AL fits proceed outward from the
median; each starts its matching exAL fit, and complete independent grids
start MQ--DESN within the same likelihood family. VB supplies MCMC starts
under the destination model, not chain-specific priors. The inherited
single-level fits have separate historical starts. Their exAL--RHS sampler
uses the collapsed shape transition in Algorithm~\ref{alg:qdesn_mcmc},
immediately followed by the scale draw. Every multiquantile exAL chain uses
that exact collapsed transition.

The exDQLM baseline refresh used \pkg{exdqlm} 1.1.1
\citep{DeLeonEtAl2026Exdqlm}; inherited Q--DESN fits identify 1.0.0
and were not refreshed. The exDQLM scale--shape calculation uses collapsed
MCMC and structured \(q(\gamma)q(\sigma\mid\gamma)\) VB. For retained
scale--shape pairs, the error moments used by its rolling-origin filter are
\(m_{\varepsilon,b}=\sigma_b\{A_p(\gamma_b)+D_p(\gamma_b)\sqrt{2/\pi}\}\)
and \(V_{\varepsilon,b}=\sigma_b^2\{B_p(\gamma_b)+A_p(\gamma_b)^2+
D_p(\gamma_b)^2(1-2/\pi)\}\).
The filter uses their mixture mean and variance,
\(\bar m_\varepsilon=B_{\mathrm{post}}^{-1}\sum_bm_{\varepsilon,b}\)
and \(\bar V_\varepsilon=B_{\mathrm{post}}^{-1}
\sum_b(V_{\varepsilon,b}+m_{\varepsilon,b}^2)-\bar m_\varepsilon^2\).
The initial parameter posterior is retained while newly available responses
update the state filter at successive origins.

The single-level comparison uses three MCMC chains, each with 5,000
burn-in and 20,000 sampling iterations without sampler thinning. Scoring
uses 4,000 aligned draws per chain, except for Gaussian AL Q--DESN at
\(p=0.05\), whose forecast MAE and check loss use 1,000 per chain.
The selected VB fits allow at most 300 iterations. Recorded times include
forecasting and metric preparation rather than isolated fitting time.
The multiquantile comparison uses five chains: AL runs for 4,000
iterations with 1,000 discarded and retains every fourth draw, giving
3,750 pooled draws; exAL runs for 8,000 with 2,000 discarded and retains
7,500. Its scores use 3,750 draws except for both Laplace exAL regressions,
which use all 7,500.

For the multiquantile comparison, Gaussian-reference prior calibration is
fixed before sampling and shared across regressions and chains. With
\(q_G\) denoting the Gaussian RHS variational fit,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Reference scale:}\quad&s_G=\sqrt{\E_{q_G}(\sigma_G^2)},\\
\text{Intercept prior:}\quad&b_{0k}=\E_{q_G}(\beta_{0G})+s_G\Phi^{-1}(p_k),
\qquad V_{0k}=(2s_G)^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp-joint-intercept-calibration}
\end{equation}
These are not the Gaussian initializer's intercept variance \(10^6\) or
dispersed chain starts. MQ--DESN orders its intercepts and anchors the
adjacent-difference slope prior at its first quantile, without an extra
random baseline. The exAL VB initializers use the structured factor in
Section~\ref{subsec:supp_structured_exal_vb}: analytic scale integration
and quadrature over shape. Their slope covariance is retained, while
variational intercepts are point estimates.

For each draw, forecast MAE averages the absolute error relative to the
known state-conditional quantile path, and check loss averages
\(\rho_p(y-q_p)\) over the scored origin--horizon pairs. The displayed
means and equal-tailed 95\% intervals summarize these draw-wise aggregate
criteria, not response prediction intervals or repeated-series variation.
Q--DESN retains one coefficient draw across targets; dynamic comparators
combine target-specific draws using the prescribed latent-Gaussian
construction. Aggregate widths therefore depend on this cross-target
coupling. Original single-level target draws were unavailable and were
reconstructed from the selected fits; both evaluation windows use those
same reconstructed identities. Criterion-specific specifications and
historical starts also preclude a controlled comparison of VB approximation
error with MCMC.

\subsection{Streamflow data, predictors and priors}
\label{sec:glofas_discrepancy}
\label{subsec:glofas_aug_design}

The reference is daily USGS discharge at gauge 11160500 on the San
Lorenzo River at Big Trees, California; the retrospective product is
GloFAS v3.1 LISFLOOD consolidated
\citep{AlfieriEtAl2013GloFAS,HarriganEtAl2023GloFASForecasts}.
Discharge is measured in \(\mathrm m^3/\mathrm s\) before the
\(\log(1+\mathrm{flow})\) transformation. There are 12,455 historical
dates from 19~November~1988 through the origin \(T=25\)~December~2022,
after excluding the initial 540 days needed for input history. The issued
ensemble supplies 51 members at each of the following 28 horizons, through
22~January~2023. Future USGS measurements are scoring-only and are
excluded from every fit and latent-path update. Weather inputs use realized
PRISM precipitation and ERA5 soil moisture, as described in main Section~6.

The two DESNs in the main model supply reference and discrepancy features.
Reference inputs use gauge history and weather; discrepancy inputs use
weather and \(a_t=g_t^{\mathrm{ret}}-y_t\), not an additional independent
response. Beyond \(T\), this lag input is the mean of transformed
issued members minus the candidate reference value. Only earlier candidate
responses enter either design at a given horizon. This ensemble mean is
an input summary; all members enter the likelihood separately.

Historical selection used 16 rainy-season development periods and 16
prespecified confirmation periods, with two additional periods in each
group to reject unstable candidates. Confirmation used four reservoir
realizations. The final forecast window did not select reservoirs, priors,
stopping rules or families. Table~\ref{tab:supp-glofas-search3-specifications}
gives both depth-one DESNs. Inputs contain response or discrepancy lags
1--\(m\) and weather lags 0--\(m_z\), standardized from training data;
readouts contain reservoir features and intercepts, not direct input

\subsection{Ensemble-conditioned target and shared updates}
\label{subsec:glofas_likelihoods}

For one level, write \(\Theta_k\) for the reference and discrepancy
coefficients, scales and shapes, \(L_{H,k}\) for the historical
likelihood, and \(\vect y_{F,k}\) for its 28 unobserved future-reference
responses. Using the locations defined in main Section~6, the target is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Target:}\quad
\pi_k(\Theta_k,\vect y_{F,k}\mid D)
&\propto\pi_k(\Theta_k)L_{H,k}R_T(\vect y_{F,k})\\
&\quad\times\prod_{h=1}^{28}\prod_{e=1}^{51}
f_{p_k}(g_{T+h,e}^{\mathrm{ens}}\mid
q_{k,T+h}+d_{k,T+h},\sigma_{G,k},\gamma_{G,k}).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_glofas_unit_member_target}
\end{equation}
Here \(D\) contains history and issued forecasts, not future USGS.
The frozen law
\(R_T(\vect y_F)=\Normal(\vect y_F;\vect m_N,\mat C_N)\) is
moment-matched from 500 truth-free recursive trajectories of the historical
Part~3 Gaussian DESN with RHS regularization. The same law is used for every
quantile family and level. It replaces, rather than multiplies,
future-reference AL or exAL working-likelihood factors. Each issued member
has unit weight and is conditionally independent given the parameters, path
and preceding histories. The 1,428 member factors therefore represent
replicate information without additional residual member dependence.
Historical retrospective and issued GloFAS observations share
source-specific scales and shapes. MQ--DESN multiplies the seven
level-specific likelihoods under its coefficient prior, with a separate
path factor for each level initialized from the same frozen law.

Stack \(\vect b_k=(\vect\beta_k^\top,\vect\delta_k^\top)^\top\),
and set \(\vect h_t^Y=(\vect x_t^{\mathrm{ref}\top},\vect0^\top)^\top\)
and \(\vect h_t^G=(\vect x_t^{\mathrm{ref}\top},
\vect x_t^{\mathrm{disc}\top})^\top\). Their linear predictors are
\(q_{k,t}\) and \(q_{k,t}+d_{k,t}\). Conditional on the path, the
updates in Appendix~A apply with
\((y_t,\vect x_t,\vect\beta,\sigma,\gamma)\) replaced by
\((z_i^c,\vect h_i^c,\vect b_k,\sigma_{c,k},\gamma_{c,k})\),
for source \(c\in\{Y,G\}\). Source counts are
\(n_Y=12{,}455\) and
\(n_G=12{,}455+28\times51=13{,}883\); the future-reference responses
are latent under \(R_T\), not reference-likelihood observations. Thus an AL scale prior
\(\sigma_c\sim\IG(a_c,b_c)\) gives shape \(a_c+3n_c/2\), whereas
a Gaussian variance prior gives shape \(a_c+n_c/2\) for
\(\sigma_c^2\). Residual moments include coefficient and path uncertainty.

For exAL, suppress \(k\), let
\(r_i=z_i^c-\vect h_i^{c\top}\vect b_k\), and sum over source
\(c\). Its conditional scale kernel in Appendix~A has
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Source scale kernel:}\quad
\nu_c&=-a_c-3n_c/2,\qquad
\chi_c=2b_c+2\sum_i v_i+
\sum_i\frac{(r_i-A_\gamma v_i)^2}{B_\gamma v_i},\\
\psi_c&=\frac{D_\gamma^2}{B_\gamma}\sum_i\frac{s_i^2}{v_i}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_glofas_unit_exal_updates}
\end{equation}
The structured variational analogue uses the corresponding residual
second moments and source-specific shape normalizers. The coefficient
update sums the expected canonical precision and linear terms over both
sources; it retains reference--discrepancy covariance. Random future
designs additionally require the moments below, not only a fixed-design
substitution.

\subsection{Latent-path approximation and reported forecasts}
\label{subsec:glofas_latent_path}

The grid is \((0.05,0.20,0.35,0.50,0.65,0.80,0.95)\).
Independent fits estimate levels separately; MQ--DESN uses quantile-block
coordinate updates with separate adjacent-difference priors for reference
and discrepancy slopes. Within a level, the coefficient factor retains
reference--discrepancy covariance and the future-path factor combines the
frozen Gaussian prior with member terms in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp_glofas_unit_member_target}.
This is not a full Gaussian factor over every parameter and path.

Linearizing the design around the current path mean gives the local
quadratic expected augmented log density \(\widetilde\ell_{F,k}\).
The Gaussian path factor and its precision are
\begin{equation}
\text{Latent path:}\quad
q_{F,k}\approx\Normal(\vect m_k,\mat V_k),\qquad
\mat V_k^{-1}=\mat C_N^{-1}
-\nabla^2\widetilde\ell_{F,k}^{\mathrm{ens}}+\epsilon\mat I_{28}.
\label{eq:supp_glofas_future_path_factor}
\end{equation}
For \(\vect a_i^c=\vect h_i^c(\vect m)\) and its Jacobian
\(\mat J_i\), suppressing the level subscript, the propagated moments are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Design moments:}\quad
\E(\vect h_i^c)&\approx\vect a_i^c,\qquad
\E(\vect h_i^c\vect h_i^{c\top})
\approx\vect a_i^c\vect a_i^{c\top}+\mat J_i\mat V\mat J_i^\top,\\
\text{Response--design moment:}\quad
\E(\vect h_i^c y_{T+h})&\approx\vect a_i^c m_h+\mat J_i\mat V\vect e_h.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:supp_glofas_future_path_delta}
\end{equation}
Here \(\vect e_h\) is the \(h\)th coordinate vector. The last term
retains covariance between a latent response and its predictor; coefficient
covariance also enters the quadratic path update. The precision stabilizer
is \(\epsilon=10^{-8}\), with a \(10^{-12}\) coefficient ridge floor.
These numerical regularizations do not define a replacement future model.
Repeated first-order updates give a local Gaussian--Delta approximation.

As specified in the main article, each reported quantile forecast is
\(\E_q\{q_{p_k,T+h}(\vect y_{F,1:h-1})\}\), evaluated by Monte Carlo
over the shared driver paths. This is a mean conditional-quantile readout
location, not a quantile of \(q_{F,k}\) and not a credible limit. Separate
fits need not produce an ordered assembled grid; no post-hoc projection is
applied. The inverse transform is \(\exp(\widehat q)-1\). Historical AL and
exAL curves instead evaluate the conditional linear predictor at the
coefficient mean. Gaussian forecast curves use empirical type-8 quantiles
of their retained path draws. The conditional-scale moment and complete
historical-curve calculation remain archived.
