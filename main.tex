\begin{abstract}
\normalsize
\noindent
Quantile forecasts for nonlinear time series must accommodate complex temporal
dependence. We develop quantile deep echo state networks (Q--DESN) as Bayesian
quantile regressions on specified nonlinear temporal features. Conditional on
a reservoir realization, the quantile location is linear in regression
coefficients, with ridge or regularized-horseshoe regularization. Asymmetric
Laplace and extended asymmetric Laplace working likelihoods support independent
quantile regressions and a joint extension that shrinks adjacent
quantile-specific coefficient differences. Posterior computation uses Markov
chain Monte Carlo and model-specific variational approximations. Two
simulations examine quantile-path recovery, forecast scores, and ordering.
\textit{[Simulation findings to be updated.]}
A retrospective streamflow analysis uses
gauge records and Global Flood Awareness System ensembles to correct forecast
discrepancies. \textit{[GloFAS findings to be updated.]}
The electricity-price application compares region-specific regressions with
matched PriceFM forecasts. \textit{[PriceFM findings to be updated.]}
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textit{Key words:} Asymmetric Laplace likelihood; Bayesian shrinkage;
probabilistic forecasting; reservoir computing; variational inference.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Conditional quantiles describe tail risk and provide forecasts suited to
asymmetric decision losses and prediction intervals
\citep{KoenkerBassett1978,Koenker2005,Gneiting2011}. Let \(Y_{t+h}\) be a
future response at horizon \(h\), and let \(\mathcal F_t\) contain the
information available at forecast origin \(t\). Its level-\(p\) conditional
quantile is
\[
\text{Forecast quantile:}\quad
Q_{t,h}(p)=\inf\{u:\Pr(Y_{t+h}\le u\mid\mathcal F_t)\ge p\},
\qquad 0<p<1.
\]
In nonlinear time series, estimating these quantiles requires temporal
predictors that represent more than a linear dependence on recent responses.
Bayesian inference becomes costly if those predictors are themselves
determined by a large collection of unknown recurrent-network weights,
particularly when fitting is repeated across probability levels or forecast
origins.

Q--DESN separates temporal feature construction from quantile regression.
A specified deep echo state network transforms response history and covariates
into recurrent nonlinear predictors. Conditional on these predictors, the
quantile location is linear in the regression coefficients, and the reservoir
weights are held fixed. This construction retains a regularized regression
structure for Bayesian inference while allowing nonlinear temporal dependence
to enter through the predictors. Ridge priors shrink the feature coefficients
collectively; regularized-horseshoe (RHS) priors allow coefficient-specific
shrinkage with a slab bound on their conditional variances
\citep{CarvalhoPolsonScott2010HS,PiironenVehtari2017RHS}. The intercept is
specified separately. For several probability levels, a joint extension also
regularizes adjacent quantile-specific slope differences. Posterior uncertainty
conditions on the selected feature map and reservoir realization.

The asymmetric Laplace (AL) likelihood links its location to a specified
conditional quantile and admits a normal--exponential augmentation
\citep{yu2001bayesian,KozumiKobayashi2011Gibbs}. We also use the
extended asymmetric Laplace (exAL) likelihood, by which we mean the
quantile-fixed generalized asymmetric Laplace family of \citet{yan2025new}.
It adds shape flexibility while retaining the selected quantile location.
These are working likelihoods, not assertions about the data-generating errors.
Under misspecification, posterior coefficient intervals need not have nominal
repeated-sampling coverage. Covariance and sandwich adjustments for ordinary
AL regression require assumptions on the quantile model, design, prior, and
sampling process \citep{YangWangHe2016ALInference,JiLeeRabeHesketh2025ValidSE}.
They do not by themselves calibrate future-response intervals or establish
validity for the exAL, variational, dependent-data, and composite constructions
used here. We apply no post hoc covariance correction and assess predictive
calibration separately.

Echo state networks (ESNs) generate dynamic predictors from recurrent states
with fixed internal weights
\citep{Jaeger2001EchoState,LukoseviciusJaeger2009ReservoirComputing}.
Stacking reservoirs extends this construction to a deep ESN
\citep{GallicchioMicheliPedrelli2018DeepESNDesign}. Bayesian regression on
these features already has precedent in the hierarchical DESN of
\citet{McDermottWikle2018DeepESNUQ}.

Quantile reservoir methods also have precedent.
\citet{LvZhaoLiuWang2016QRESNE} estimate ESN output weights by quantile
regression and combine quantile fits in a bootstrap ensemble for confidence
and prediction intervals. \citet{BonasWikleCastruccio2024CalibratedDeepESN}
first fit DESN point forecasts by ridge regression, then jointly estimate
lead-dependent residual quantiles with penalized quantile sheets and
calibrate the resulting intervals.

Q--DESN estimates response quantiles through Bayesian regression on specified
DESN features. The AL or exAL working likelihood combines coefficient
shrinkage with posterior inference for regression and likelihood parameters.
Pairs of fitted quantiles define interval forecasts without a separate
residual-calibration regression or bootstrap ensemble. The joint extension
couples quantile-specific readout coefficients through adjacent-difference
shrinkage priors.
For fixed-design AL regressions with ridge or product RHS priors, Gaussian
augmentation yields closed-form full-conditional distributions, supporting
Gibbs-based posterior computation \citep{KozumiKobayashi2011Gibbs}.
Model-specific variational approximations support repeated fitting without
requiring MCMC for every fit. This posterior uncertainty conditions on the specified
reservoir; direct estimation does not itself guarantee interval coverage.

For several probability levels, independent regressions can be ordered after
fitting by isotonic projection or rearrangement
\citep{BarlowBrunk1972IsotonicDual,chernozhukov2010}. The joint Q--DESN
regression instead shares information through RHS priors on adjacent
coefficient differences, adapting the Quantile-Varying Parameter construction
of \citet{KohnsSzendrei2025QVP}. This regularization can reduce crossings
before projection, but it does not impose noncrossing. Constrained or process
models provide other ways to estimate quantiles jointly
\citep{WuLiu2009StepwiseNCQR,BondellReichWang2010NoncrossingQR,
YangTokdar2017JointQuantilePlanes}. We distinguish estimation across levels
from the monotone transformation used to report an ordered grid.

Dynamic quantile linear models provide a complementary representation of
temporal dependence through evolving linear states
\citep{GoncalvesMigonBastos2020DQLM,barata2021_flex_quantile}. The earlier
hydrologic model of \citet{DeLeonPradoSanso2026HydrologicProducts} combines
products through evolving linear quantile states and source-specific
discrepancies, fitting levels separately before predictive synthesis. It
supports both MCMC and variational inference. Q--DESN uses recurrent nonlinear
predictors with coefficients static over time; its joint extension couples
coefficients across probability levels. The streamflow analysis retains
reference--discrepancy modeling but changes the predictors and posterior
approximation. Differences in products, covariates, and evaluation prevent a
matched performance comparison with the earlier analysis.

The first simulation compares quantile-path recovery and forecast performance
with dynamic quantile linear models. The second examines forecast scores and
ordering for independent and joint estimation on a common seven-level grid.
The applications evaluate retrospective GloFAS streamflow correction and
independent electricity-price regressions against PriceFM. Sections~%
\ref{sec:model}--%
\ref{sec:forecast} define the model, computation, and forecast evaluation;
Sections~\ref{sec:simulation}--\ref{sec:pricefm-application} give the
evidence. Derivations and additional results are in the supplement.

\section{Fixed-Feature Bayesian Quantile Models}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Single-level quantile regression}

Let \(Y_t\) be the response, with observed value \(y_t\), and let
\(\vect x_t=(1,\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top)^\top\in\R^{r+1}\) be a specified
temporal predictor vector. Write
\(\vect\beta=(\beta_0,\vect\beta_{\mathrm{slope}}^\top)^\top\in\R^{r+1}\).
At probability level \(p_0\in(0,1)\), the working regression is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Quantile location:}\quad &\mu_t=\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta,\\
\text{Likelihood:}\quad
&Y_t\mid\vect x_t,\vect\beta,\sigma,\gamma
  \sim\exAL_{p_0}(\mu_t,\sigma,\gamma),\qquad t=1,\ldots,T.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:qdesn-marginal-main}
\end{equation}
Under this likelihood, \(\mu_t\) is the conditional \(p_0\)-quantile,
\(\sigma>0\) controls scale, and \(\gamma\in(L,U)\) controls shape. The
support endpoints are fixed by \(p_0\). Setting \(\gamma=0\) recovers the AL
regression. The next subsection constructs \(\vect x_t\) from the response
history and covariates. Conditional on that design, the location in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:qdesn-marginal-main} is linear in \(\vect\beta\), not a
regression with evolving coefficients. Posterior inference conditions on the
selected feature map; its intervals and likelihood-based forecast bands are
model-based summaries whose coverage must be assessed separately.

\subsection{Deep echo state network features}

Let \(\vect z_t\in\R^q\) contain the exogenous variables and let
\(\vect u_t=(y_{t-1},\ldots,y_{t-m},\vect z_t^\top)^\top\in\R^{m+q}\)
contain them with response lags. A reservoir state
\(\vect h_{t,d}\in\R^{n_d}\) is a recurrent nonlinear transformation of
these inputs. For layer \(d\), \(\bar{\mat W}_d\) is a fixed sparse recurrent
matrix scaled to target spectral radius \(\rho_d\),
\(\mat W_d^{\mathrm{in}}\) is a fixed input matrix, and \(\alpha_d\) is the
leak rate. The reducer \(\mat Q_{d-1}\) gives
\(\tilde{\vect h}_{t,d-1}=\mat Q_{d-1}\vect h_{t,d-1}\).
With a fixed first-layer input bias \(\vect b_1^{\mathrm{in}}\), the
recursion and general feature vector are
\begin{equation}
\begin{alignedat}{2}
\text{First layer:}\quad &\vect h_{t,1}
&&=(1-\alpha_1)\vect h_{t-1,1}\\
&&&\quad+\alpha_1 f(\bar{\mat W}_1\vect h_{t-1,1}
+\mat W_1^{\mathrm{in}}\vect u_t+\vect b_1^{\mathrm{in}}),\\
\text{Higher layers:}\quad &\vect h_{t,d}
&&=(1-\alpha_d)\vect h_{t-1,d}\\
&&&\quad+\alpha_d f(\bar{\mat W}_d\vect h_{t-1,d}
+\mat W_d^{\mathrm{in}}\tilde{\vect h}_{t,d-1}),\quad d=2,\ldots,D,\\
\text{Features:}\quad &\tilde{\vect x}_t
&&=\big[\vect h_{t,D}^\top,k(\tilde{\vect h}_{t,1})^\top,\ldots,
k(\tilde{\vect h}_{t,D-1})^\top,\vect u_t^\top\big]^\top.
\end{alignedat}
\label{eq:desn-hierarchy}
\end{equation}
Here \(\bar{\mat W}_d\) is \(n_d\times n_d\),
\(\mat W_1^{\mathrm{in}}\) is \(n_1\times(m+q)\), and
\(\mat W_d^{\mathrm{in}}\) is \(n_d\times\tilde n_{d-1}\) for \(d>1\).
The fixed reducer \(\mat Q_d\) maps \(\R^{n_d}\) to \(\R^{\tilde n_d}\).
The activation \(f\) acts componentwise; a small leak rate retains more of
the previous state. If \(k\) preserves dimension, the displayed feature vector
has dimension \(r=n_D+\sum_{d<D}\tilde n_d+m+q\); a dimension-expanding
transformation changes this count. Appending the intercept gives \(\vect x_t\).
Reservoir and input weights are drawn once. Scaling,
reducers, and washout use fitting information and are held fixed with the
weights. The bias in Eq.~\eqref{eq:desn-hierarchy} is part of the reservoir
construction, not the regression intercept.

Each empirical design specifies its selected state blocks, transformations,
and use of direct inputs. The supplement gives sparse weight generation and
preprocessing details. With observed fitting inputs, the resulting
\(\mat X=(\vect x_1,\ldots,\vect x_T)^\top\) is deterministic. Future rows
must be constructed by the forecasting rule; the GloFAS latent-path analysis
makes them functions of an unknown future reference path.

\subsection{Coefficient regularization}

The intercept has a separately specified Gaussian prior on the response scale
and is excluded from slope shrinkage. Study-specific calibration and ordering
restrictions are stated with each design. For the \(r\) non-intercept
coefficients, ridge regularization uses
\(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{slope}}\sim
\Normal(\zeros{r},\kappa_\beta^2\Id{r})\). The adaptive alternative uses the
Nishimura--Suchard product form of the regularized horseshoe
\citep{MakalicSchmidt2016SimpleSampler,NishimuraSuchard2023SSS}. Conditional
on global, local, and slab scales \((\tau,\lambda_j,\zeta)\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Slope prior:}\quad
&\beta_j\mid\tau,\lambda_j,\zeta\sim\Normal(0,V_j),\\
\text{Effective variance:}\quad
&V_j=\left(\zeta^{-2}+\tau^{-2}\lambda_j^{-2}\right)^{-1},
\qquad j=1,\ldots,r.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:rhs-var}
\end{equation}
The scales use the half-Cauchy auxiliary hierarchy, with local reference scale
one and global reference scale \(\tau_0\). The full product density retains
its scale-dependent Gaussian factors and is not identified by the conditional
variance alone. The supplement distinguishes this hierarchy from the direct
RHS specification; both give the variance in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rhs-var}, whose
slab bounds large coefficient variances.

For standardized predictors, a Gaussian regression calculation provides a
reference for the global shrinkage prior \citep{PiironenVehtari2017RHS}.
It depends on residual scale, fitting-sample size, and a chosen reference
number of coefficients expected to escape strong shrinkage. The supplement
gives the derivation and likelihood-information alternatives; the empirical
designs report the actual fixed hyperparameters. The reference is not an
effective-size identity for correlated reservoir features or the product prior.

\subsection{Joint quantile-vector regression}
\label{subsec:joint-qdesn}

Independent fits at \(0<p_1<\cdots<p_K<1\) assign a separate working
regression to each level. The joint alternative uses the locations
\begin{equation}
\text{Quantile location:}\quad
\mu_{k,t}=\beta_{0k}+\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top\vect\beta_k,
\qquad k=1,\ldots,K.
\label{eq:joint-qdesn-readout}
\end{equation}
Let \(\theta\) collect the regression, likelihood, and prior-scale parameters,
and let \(f_{p_k}\) denote the AL or exAL density at level \(p_k\).
The composite update is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Composite likelihood:}\quad
&L_c(\theta;\vect y,\mat X)
 =\prod_{t=1}^T\prod_{k=1}^K
 f_{p_k}(y_t\mid\mu_{k,t},\sigma_k,\gamma_k),\\
\text{Composite posterior:}\quad
&\pi_c(\theta\mid\vect y,\mat X)
 \propto\exp\!\left\{-\ell_c(\theta;\vect y,\mat X)\right\}\pi(\theta).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:joint-composite-posterior-main}
\end{equation}
Here \(\ell_c(\theta;\vect y,\mat X)=-\log L_c(\theta;\vect y,\mat X)\)
is the negative composite log-likelihood. The posterior is defined when its
normalizing constant is finite.
Each factor is a normalized density, but their product reuses the same
response across levels and is not a normalized scalar-response sampling
density. We use learning rate one. The prior regularizes the first slope
vector and adjacent differences,
\[
\text{Anchor and differences:}\quad
\vect\Delta_1=\vect\beta_1,\qquad
\vect\Delta_k=\vect\beta_k-\vect\beta_{k-1},\quad k=2,\ldots,K.
\]
Separate RHS hierarchies shrink these blocks. When \(K=1\), this is the
single-level slope prior. The first block remains random; shrinkage toward
zero does not fix it at zero.

Adjacent-difference shrinkage favors similar neighboring slopes, but the gap
\[
\text{Adjacent location gap:}\quad
\mu_{k,t}-\mu_{k-1,t}
=(\beta_{0k}-\beta_{0,k-1})
+\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top(\vect\beta_k-\vect\beta_{k-1})
\]
need not be positive. Crossings are reported before projection, with an
explicit monotone rule when ordered output is required. The supplement gives
the full target, block precision, and executed intercept restrictions.

\section{Posterior Computation}
\label{sec:inference}

Conditional on the design, the posterior combines the working likelihood in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:qdesn-marginal-main} with the coefficient, scale, and shape
priors. The joint model uses the untempered likelihood product and
adjacent-difference hierarchy. For computation, exAL admits the equivalent
augmentation
\begin{equation}
\begin{alignedat}{2}
\text{Latent variables:}\quad
&v_t\mid\sigma&&\sim\Exp(\mathrm{rate}=1/\sigma),
\qquad s_t\sim\TN(0,1),\\
\text{Gaussian conditional:}\quad
&Y_t\mid\cdot&&\sim\Normal\!\left(
\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta+D_{p_0}(\gamma)\sigma s_t
+A_{p_0}(\gamma)v_t,
B_{p_0}(\gamma)\sigma v_t\right).
\end{alignedat}
\label{eq:qdesn-aug}
\end{equation}
The probability-specific functions \(A_{p_0}\), \(B_{p_0}\), and \(D_{p_0}\)
are defined in the supplement; \(D_{p_0}\) is the latent-shift coefficient.
Here \(\TN(0,1)\) is supported on \((0,\infty)\), and the second
Gaussian argument is a variance.
Integrating out \((s_t,v_t)\) recovers the marginal exAL model. AL omits the
\(s_t\) and \(\gamma\) blocks.

Conditional on latent variables and prior scales, unconstrained coefficient
blocks have Gaussian updates; ordered intercepts use Gaussian conditionals
truncated between neighboring intercepts. Under the product RHS hierarchy,
local and global scale auxiliaries have inverse-gamma updates. AL mixture
variables have generalized inverse-Gaussian updates, and its likelihood scale
has an inverse-gamma update. exAL adds a positive truncated-normal shift.
Conditional on shape, its scale has a generalized inverse-Gaussian law, but
the joint scale--shape block remains nonconjugate. The exAL simulation MCMC
transitions integrate scale out of the shape update and then redraw it from
its conditional law. Convergence assessment includes rank-normalized
\(\widehat R\), effective sample sizes, chain comparisons of reported
functionals, and quantile-crossing diagnostics.

Variational inference approximates the specified target through a stated
family of factors. The supplement gives the generic Laplace--Delta (VB--LD)
construction of \citet{wang2013nonconjugatevb} for transformed exAL
scale--shape coordinates. The joint simulation instead uses
\(q(\gamma)q(\sigma\mid\gamma)\): the conditional scale normalizer is
analytic, and one-dimensional quadrature over bounded shape supplies
normalization and mixed moments. It retains a covariance for stacked slopes
but optimizes ordered intercepts as point values, so its VB uncertainty is
partial. GloFAS additionally uses local Gaussian--Delta updates for its latent
future reference path. The supplement distinguishes these approximations and
their stopping criteria from MCMC convergence assessment. Variational intervals
are identified separately from MCMC posterior intervals.

\section{Forecasting, Scoring, and Model Selection}
\label{sec:forecast}
\label{sec:selection}

A forecast origin \(o\) is the last time included in observed history; horizon
\(h\) denotes prediction of \(Y_{o+h}\). Reservoir states are reconstructed
from that history. Within each recursive forecast, unknown responses are
generated under the stated response law, and their lags enter subsequent
updates. A parameter and path draw yields the conditional-quantile location
\(\mu_{o+h,p}^{(b)}=\beta_{0p}^{(b)}
+\tilde{\vect x}_{o+h}^{(b)\top}\vect\beta_p^{(b)}\).
A marginal posterior predictive quantile instead integrates the response
distribution over parameter and future-path uncertainty before taking its
quantile. The joint simulation generates responses by ordered-grid inverse-CDF
interpolation with endpoint clamping; single-level procedures use their
documented likelihood or state-space forecast law. GloFAS Part 4 instead
approximates a latent future reference path informed by issued members.

Separate fits at \(0<p_1<\cdots<p_K<1\), with \(K\geq2\), may cross.
Where an ordered grid is required, the simulation reporting procedure uses
equal-weight isotonic projection after fitting. With \(T\) denoting an origin,
\begin{equation}
\text{Ordered grid:}\quad \vect q_{T,h}^*
=\argmin_{z_1\le\cdots\le z_K}
\sum_{k=1}^K(\widehat q_{T,h,k}-z_k)^2.
\label{eq:monotone-reporting-main}
\end{equation}
This least-squares projection differs from rearrangement by sorting and from
shrinkage during estimation. Its equal adjustment weights are not the scoring
weights below. Each study states whether draws or summaries are projected;
raw crossings and adjustment magnitudes are reported separately. Linear
interpolation specifies the curve over \([p_1,p_K]\) only; response simulation
also requires a tail rule.

Let the check loss be
\begin{equation}
\text{Check loss:}\quad \rho_p(e)=e\{p-\ind(e<0)\}.
\label{eq:check-loss-main}
\end{equation}
For a predictive distribution \(F\) with finite first moment, the CRPS has the
integrated check-loss representation
\citep{GneitingRaftery2007}
\begin{equation}
\text{CRPS:}\quad \operatorname{CRPS}(F,y)
=2\int_0^1\rho_p\{y-F^{-1}(p)\}\,dp.
\label{eq:crps-integrated-check-main}
\end{equation}
For the finite evaluation grid \(p_1,\ldots,p_K\), we approximate
Eq.~\eqref{eq:crps-integrated-check-main} by the trapezoidal rule over its
covered range,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Finite-grid score:}\quad
&\aCRPS_{p_1:p_K}(y;\vect q^*)
 =2\sum_{k=1}^K\omega_k\rho_{p_k}(y-q_k^*),\\
\text{Trapezoidal weights:}\quad
&\omega_1=\frac{p_2-p_1}{2},\\
&\omega_k=\frac{p_{k+1}-p_{k-1}}{2},\quad 2\leq k\leq K-1,\\
&\omega_K=\frac{p_K-p_{K-1}}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:acrps-main}
\end{equation}
The weights satisfy
\(\sum_{k=1}^K\omega_k=p_K-p_1\). Thus \(\aCRPS\) retains the scale of integrated
check loss over the evaluation range rather than representing a full-distribution
CRPS. The evaluation grid is common to the competing methods within each
analysis, not necessarily across analyses.

In the joint simulation the conditional DGP is known. Let
\(\mathcal E=\{(o_\ell,h_\ell):\ell=1,\ldots,N_{\mathrm{eval}}\}\)
be the held-out origin--horizon pairs, and let
\(F_{0,\ell}(y)=\Pr_0(Y_{o_\ell+h_\ell}\le y
\mid\mathcal F_{o_\ell}^{\mathrm{obs}})\)
be the conditional DGP distribution given observed origin history. For posterior draw \(b\),
the DGP-integrated finite-grid quantile score (aCRPS) is
\begin{equation}
\text{DGP score:}\quad S_{\mathrm{DGP}}^{(b)}
=\frac{2}{N_{\mathrm{eval}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{eval}}}
\sum_{k=1}^K\omega_k
\int\rho_{p_k}\!\left(y-q_{\ell,k}^{*,(b)}\right)\,dF_{0,\ell}(y).
\label{eq:dgp-integrated-score-main}
\end{equation}
Common simulated DGP paths approximate these expectations over future
responses. The forecasts remain estimated from a particular fitting
realization. Posterior readout draws are evaluated at each model's averaged
recursive features; score intervals condition on that summary and exclude
repeated-training-sample and model-selection uncertainty. The supplement
reports sensitivity to draw-specific features and numerical integration checks.

Reservoir selection and posterior initialization serve different purposes.
Candidate feature maps are screened and refined using the development
observations and criterion stated for each analysis. Gaussian ridge
regressions support exploration, followed where specified by Gaussian RHS
variational fits and quantile refinement. For the reference model \(M\) used
in selection and
candidates \(R_1,\ldots,R_J\),
\begin{equation}
\text{Selected reservoir:}\quad \widehat R_M
\in\argmin_{R_j:\,j=1,\ldots,J}
S_{\mathrm{val}}(R_j;M).
\label{eq:reservoir-selection-main}
\end{equation}
The chosen specification is held fixed for evaluation; the joint simulation
uses one selected backbone for all four regression classes. The staged exploration
limits expensive quantile fits; it does not establish a globally optimal
reservoir. The independent single-quantile study uses its separate,
criterion-specific procedure. Estimates used to calibrate prior hyperparameters
are identified as prior calibration and fixed before subsequent comparison.
State checks cover finite values, activation saturation, near-constant or
highly dependent columns, effective rank, conditioning, and variation across
reservoir realizations. These checks are needed because spectral-radius
scaling alone does not guarantee suitable driven dynamics
\citep{JaegerLukoseviciusPopoviciSiewert2007LeakyESN,
YildizJaegerKiebel2012ESP}.

For the joint simulation and applications, compatible Gaussian ridge estimates
initialize Gaussian RHS--VB, then the median AL regression. AL fits proceed
outward from the median; each exAL fit starts from its matching AL level.
In the joint simulation and GloFAS, complete independent grids initialize joint
fits within the same likelihood family; PriceFM uses independent regressions
only. Where MCMC is used, VB supplies starting values for the same statistical
model. Transfers respect the destination scale, support, feature ordering, and
prior. They do not themselves redefine its likelihood or prior. The complete
sequence and compatibility conditions are in the supplement.

\section{Simulation Studies}
\label{sec:simulation}

\noindent
Two studies separate recovery of known conditional quantile paths from
held-out forecast scoring. The first follows the error-family comparisons of
\citet{yan2025new}, with a dynamic rather than static quantile path, and
compares single-level Q--DESN with dynamic linear baselines. The second
compares independent and joint quantile regressions on a shared reservoir
design. We use \(p\) for a single level and \(p_k\), \(k=1,\ldots,K\),
for a grid. Main-text figures report MCMC forecast summaries; the supplement
contains fitting-sample recovery, numerical tables, and approximate VB results.

\subsection{Single-quantile design and evaluation}

For each error family \(f\) and target quantile level
\(p\in\{0.05,0.25,0.50\}\), the synthetic series and its conditional
quantile path are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Centered innovation:}\quad
&\varepsilon_{t,p,f}=e_{t,f}-H_f^{-1}(p),\\
\text{Observation:}\quad
&y_{t,p,f}=\mu_{t,f}+\varepsilon_{t,p,f},\\
\text{Conditional quantile:}\quad
&Q_p(Y_{t,p,f}\mid\vect\theta_{t,f})=\mu_{t,f}
=\vect F^\top\vect\theta_{t,f}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:single-quantile-dgp-main}
\end{equation}
Here \(\vect F=(1,0,1,0,1,0)^\top\), and \(H_f\) is the distribution
function of the raw error \(e_{t,f}\), so the centering makes the
\(p\)-quantile of \(\varepsilon_{t,p,f}\) zero. The
six-dimensional state contains a local level, local slope, and two
sine--cosine harmonic pairs. The seasonal period is \(90\), the harmonics are
\(1\) and \(2\), and the state innovation covariance satisfies
\(\sqrt{\diag(\mat W_\theta)}
=(0.005,0.00002,0.004,0.004,0.003,0.003)\).
The initial covariance is \(\mat C_0=0.01\Id{6}\), and each simulated
trajectory is initialized deterministically at the family-specific state in
Table~\ref{tab:simulation-dgp}. For a fixed family, the same latent path
\(\{\mu_{t,f}\}\) is used across the three values of \(p\); only the centering
constant \(H_f^{-1}(p)\) changes.

Each synthetic series is first run for \(T_{\mathrm{warm}}=2000\) warmup
observations, which are discarded before analysis. The next
\(T_{\mathrm{main}}=10000\) observations form the retained series. The first
9000 support model specification and fitting, and the final 1000 are held out for
forecast evaluation. The reported single-quantile comparison uses
\(T_{\mathrm{fit}}=500\), namely observations 8501--9000 immediately preceding
the fitting origin. Forecast
scores are computed from rolling origins within the held-out period; origins
are spaced 30 observations apart, and each origin is scored for horizons up to
30 steps ahead, yielding 34 origins and 1000 scored pairs after truncation
at the held-out boundary. All methods use these same pairs.

For each family and target quantile level in the single-quantile simulation
comparison, the reported Q--DESN rows vary the working likelihood and
inference method while using regularized-horseshoe (RHS) coefficient shrinkage.
Entries labeled Q--DESN AL--RHS use the AL working likelihood, whereas entries
labeled Q--DESN exAL--RHS use the exAL working likelihood. The main comparison
focuses on regularized-horseshoe fits. For each displayed metric, the reservoir
specification and realization were selected before held-out evaluation and then
held fixed.
Dynamic quantile linear model (DQLM) and extended dynamic quantile linear
model (exDQLM) baselines are fit to the same family, quantile level,
fitting-sample size, and forecast-evaluation design using version 1.1.1 of the
CRAN package \pkg{exdqlm} \citep{exdqlmRPackage}. The exDQLM calculations use its
partially collapsed MCMC update and structured
\(q(\gamma)q(\sigma\mid\gamma)\) variational approximation for the
scale--asymmetry block.
For rolling-origin MCMC evaluation, the initial parameter posterior is retained
while the filtering distribution is updated sequentially using the responses
available at each origin. In the exDQLM analysis, the observation-error mean
and variance used in this update are averaged over paired retained draws of the
scale and asymmetry parameters. The updated state is then propagated over the
requested forecast horizons.

The primary comparison is a fixed-design benchmark against structured dynamic
quantile linear models under linear dynamic mechanisms that favor those
baselines. Repeated data-generating realizations, broader reservoir-parameter comparisons, and
non-Bayesian ESN regression baselines remain topics for future sensitivity
analyses.

Let \(q_{p,t}^{(b)}\) be retained draw \(b\) of the conditional \(p\)-quantile
at fitting index \(t\), and let \(q_{p,\ell}^{(b)}\) be the corresponding
forecast draw for origin--horizon pair \(\ell\). With
\(N_{\mathrm{fit}}=500\) and \(N_{\mathrm{eval}}=1000\), the draw-wise
criteria are
\begin{align}
\text{Fit RMSE:}\quad R_{\mathrm{fit}}^{(b)}
  &=\left\{\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{fit}}}\sum_{t=1}^{N_{\mathrm{fit}}}
    \bigl(q_{p,t}^{(b)}-\mu_t\bigr)^2\right\}^{1/2}, \\
\text{Forecast MAE:}\quad A_{\mathrm{fore}}^{(b)}
  &=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{eval}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{eval}}}
    \left|q_{p,\ell}^{(b)}-\mu_\ell\right|, \\
\text{Forecast check loss:}\quad C_{\mathrm{fore}}^{(b)}
  &=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{eval}}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{\mathrm{eval}}}
    \rho_p\!\left(y_\ell-q_{p,\ell}^{(b)}\right),
\end{align}
where \(\rho_p\) is the check loss in Eq.~\eqref{eq:check-loss-main}, and
\(\mu_\ell\) and \(y_\ell\) denote the oracle quantile and realized outcome
for pair \(\ell\). Fit RMSE and forecast MAE measure recovery of the
state-conditional oracle path, which is not generally the predictive quantile
after integrating over future states. Forecast check loss instead scores the
reported quantile against realized observations.

For each criterion \(M^{(b)}\), the tables report the posterior mean
\(B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^B M^{(b)}\) and the empirical 0.025 and 0.975 posterior
quantiles. The VB panels use draws from the variational approximation and are
therefore approximate. Because the criteria are nonlinear functions of the
quantile path, these posterior means generally differ from applying a criterion
once to a posterior point path.

For each model, error family, quantile level, and criterion, the reservoir
specification was selected in a separate calibration analysis before the
posterior metric distribution was evaluated. Consequently, different criteria
within a model may correspond to different reservoir specifications. The
intervals condition on the selected specification, simulated series,
rolling-origin design, and reservoir realization; repeated-simulation,
reservoir-selection, and model-selection uncertainty are excluded.

\subsection{Joint Multi-Quantile Study with a Shared Feature Design}

The second simulation study compares joint and independent estimation at the
seven levels
\(p_k\in\{0.05,0.10,0.25,0.50,0.75,0.90,0.95\}\)

Student-\(t\), regime-shift, and nonlinear dynamics. For each mechanism, one

\section{Application: GloFAS Streamflow Quantile Forecasting}
\label{sec:data}

Ensemble river-discharge forecasts need not reproduce the distribution
observed at a local gauge. A statistical correction must represent these
differences while retaining the temporal variation relevant to forecast
quantiles. We study this problem for the San Lorenzo River at Big Trees,
California, using United States Geological Survey (USGS) gauge 11160500 as
the reference. Historical and issued Global Flood Awareness System (GloFAS)
products provide additional information. GloFAS supports flood early warning
and verification \citep{AlfieriEtAl2013GloFAS,HarriganEtAl2023GloFASForecasts,
CEMS2026GloFASDocumentation}. Q--DESN supplies recurrent nonlinear predictors
while keeping the quantile location linear in the coefficients, with
variational computation for repeated fitting. The experiment assesses this
correction at one retrospective origin; it does not establish operational
accuracy or a measured computation-time advantage.

\subsection{Data and Forecast-Origin Design}

Let \(T\) be the forecast origin, \(y_t=\log(y_t^{\mathrm{USGS}}+1)\) the
transformed gauge record, in \(\mathrm{m}^3/\mathrm{s}\) before transformation,
through \(T\), \(g_t^{\mathrm{ret}}\) the transformed
retrospective GloFAS series, and \(g_{T+h,e}^{\mathrm{ens}}\) transformed
issued ensemble member \(e\) at horizon \(h\). The origin is
\GlofasApplicationCurrentOriginDate{}. Parts 1--3 produce 30-day recursive
paths, from 26~December~2022 through 24~January~2023. Part 4 uses the
\GlofasApplicationCurrentScoredHorizons{} issued GloFAS horizons through
22~January~2023, retaining all 51 members at each horizon. Future
USGS values are excluded from fitting and used only for scoring. The
forecast-period precipitation and soil-moisture covariates are realized
PRISM/ERA5 values. The experiment is therefore a retrospective
oracle-covariate diagnostic, not a real-time forecast based only on information
available at \(T\).

\subsection{Four-Part Design and Internal Selection}

Part 1 estimates a univariate USGS reference process and its recursive
forecast. Part 2 estimates the discrepancy
\(g_t^{\mathrm{ret}}-y_t\) and its recursive path. The latter is unscored
after \(T\), because retrospective GloFAS ends at the cutoff. Part 3 combines
the reference and discrepancy processes in a joint historical model and
produces a recursive USGS forecast. Part 4 instead treats future USGS values
as latent during fitting and incorporates the issued GloFAS ensemble into
their working likelihood. It is not an ordinary post-fit forecast stage.

The reference and discrepancy DESN specifications used in these components
were fixed by Search III using internal rainy-season development and locked
confirmation folds, with four reservoir seeds. The selected depth-one reservoirs have
1500 and 1000 units, respectively. The protected December~2022--January~2023
window was not used to select reservoirs, priors, stopping rules, or model
families. Parts 1 and 2 establish the separate reference and discrepancy
components used downstream; the supplement reports their specifications and
the family-specific prior settings.

\subsection{Application Model}

\noindent\textbf{Latent-path discrepancy model.}
Two fixed DESN feature maps represent the reference process and the
GloFAS-minus-USGS discrepancy. For probability level \(p\), write
\(q_{p,t}=\vect x_t^{\mathrm{ref}\top}\vect\beta_p\) for the reference
quantile and \(d_{p,t}=\vect x_t^{\mathrm{disc}\top}\vect\delta_p\) for the
additive discrepancy. On the transformed scale, the historical and issued
working-likelihood contributions are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Reference history:}\quad
y_t\mid\cdot&\sim\AL_p(q_{p,t},\sigma_Y),\\
\text{Retrospective GloFAS:}\quad
g_t^{\mathrm{ret}}\mid\cdot&\sim\AL_p(q_{p,t}+d_{p,t},\sigma_G),\\
\text{Issued member:}\quad
g_{T+h,e}^{\mathrm{ens}}\mid\cdot&\sim
\AL_p(q_{p,T+h}+d_{p,T+h},\sigma_G).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:glofas-part4-main}
\end{equation}
The unavailable Part 4 path \(y_{T+1:T+28}\) is represented as latent during fitting
and recursively supplies future response lags to the reference reservoir. Each
of the 51 issued members has weight \(1/51\), so every issued horizon has total
weight one. This weighting is applied to the augmented complete-data working
likelihood. The additive location relation in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:glofas-part4-main} is a model assumption, not an identity for
quantiles of sums or differences. Combining reference and discrepancy
processes is distinct from jointly estimating probability levels; the latter
links adjacent coefficient vectors separately within each component.

The comparison uses the seven levels \(0.05\), \(0.20\), \(0.35\), \(0.50\),
\(0.65\), \(0.80\), and \(0.95\). Independent AL and exAL fit the levels
separately; Joint AL and exAL link the reference and discrepancy coefficient
paths through adjacent-quantile RHS shrinkage. The exAL families replace the
AL working likelihood in Eq.~\eqref{eq:glofas-part4-main} by its shape-extended
counterpart. Two Gaussian predictive baselines and the raw GloFAS ensemble
complete the comparison. Application inference uses VB; no GloFAS MCMC fit
is reported. No crossing correction is applied, and the reported Part 4 quantile grids
have zero crossings. The supplement gives the weighted augmentation,
latent-path approximation, and convergence criteria, including the Gaussian
future-path prior derived from the Part 3 Normal RHS/VB recursive paths.

\subsection{Retrospective Application Results}

Both corrected joint variational fits meet the specified inner, outer, and
shrinkage-scale stopping criteria. This does not establish predictive
calibration; the supplement gives the criteria and numerical assessment.

\section{Application: Region-Frozen PriceFM Comparison}
\label{sec:pricefm-application}

Day-ahead electricity-price forecasting requires distributional summaries
in markets with changing demand and renewable generation. The second
application compares region-specific Q--DESN quantile regressions with the
released forecasts of PriceFM, a pretrained neural model that uses regional
covariates and transmission-network information \citep{YuEtAl2026PriceFM}.
Version~4 covers
European bidding regions at 15-minute resolution from 1~January~2022 through
1~January~2026 and includes price, load, solar, and wind forecasts. Average
quantile loss (AQL) is evaluated on the PriceFM probability grid at matched
held-out timestamps for 114 region--fold comparisons.

\subsection{Comparison Design}

The comparison contains \PricefmFullRegionFolds{} region--fold evaluations,
spanning \PricefmFullRegions{} European bidding regions and
\PricefmFullFolds{} rolling folds. For each region, one Q--DESN specification
was selected using temporal validation periods within the first training
interval. The first outer validation period determined the AL or exAL family.
The complete specification was then frozen before R98 test scoring. The
reservoir specification, RHS global-scale hyperparameter, feature
set, and working-likelihood family are therefore common to the three folds of
that region. The selected specifications use AL in \PricefmCurrentAlRegions{}
regions and exAL in \PricefmCurrentExalRegions{} regions. There were no post-test
changes and no substitutions from the earlier case-specific comparison.

Each evaluation is scored on the fold-aligned held-out timestamps and
probability levels \PricefmFullPaperQuantiles. Throughout this section,
\(\Delta\) AQL denotes Q--DESN AQL minus PriceFM AQL. Negative values indicate
lower Q--DESN AQL; positive values indicate lower PriceFM AQL.
For region \(r\) and fold \(f\), with \(N_{r,f}\) scored timestamps, the score is
\begin{equation}
\text{Average quantile loss:}\quad
\operatorname{AQL}_{r,f}
=\frac{1}{N_{r,f}K}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r,f}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}
\rho_{p_k}\!\left(y_{r,f,i}-\widehat q_{r,f,i,k}\right),
\label{eq:pricefm-aql-main}
\end{equation}
where \(K=7\) and both outcomes and forecasts are in EUR/MWh.
Unlike \(\aCRPS\), AQL equally weights the probability levels and has no
factor of two. Aggregate scores give equal weight to the 114 region--fold
evaluations, rather than pooling all timestamps.

\noindent\textbf{Information set.}
The predictors include the released load, solar, and wind forecast
covariates; spatial specifications also use neighboring-region features or
their summaries. Their issue-time vintages were not separately verified.
We therefore interpret the results as a retrospective benchmark comparison,
without asserting equivalence to an operational forecast-origin information
set. The prospective designation concerns the R98 selection and freeze
procedure; overlapping periods had previously been examined in the historical
case-specific comparison. The supplement records the fold dates and
training-only preprocessing.

\subsection{Results}

Across the aligned benchmark, Q--DESN has lower AQL in
\PricefmFullQdesnWins{} of \PricefmFullRegionFolds{} region--fold evaluations
(\PricefmFullQdesnWinRate{}), while PriceFM has lower AQL in
\PricefmFullPricefmWins{}. The mean Q--DESN AQL is
\PricefmFullMeanQdesnAql{}, compared with \PricefmFullMeanPricefmAql{} for
PriceFM, giving mean \(\Delta\) AQL \PricefmFullMeanDeltaAql{} and median
\(\Delta\) AQL \PricefmFullMedianDeltaAql{}. Thus, under the prospectively
frozen region-level specifications, Q--DESN has slightly higher aggregate AQL.
When scores are first averaged within region, Q--DESN is lower in
\PricefmCurrentRegionWins{} of \PricefmFullRegions{} regions and PriceFM is lower
in \PricefmCurrentRegionLosses{}.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Q--DESN retains a regularized linear regression for the quantile location
while using fixed recurrent features to represent nonlinear temporal
dependence. Its posterior concerns the regression, likelihood, and shrinkage
parameters, conditional on the reservoir. This distinction makes the model
accessible through Bayesian regression calculations without treating every
network weight as an unknown parameter. In the GloFAS latent-path analysis,
however, future features depend on unknown reference responses; its complete
posterior is not an ordinary fixed-design regression problem.

The simulations do not identify a uniformly preferable likelihood or
regression class. Joint estimation reduces raw crossings, especially under
exAL, but does not give a general forecast-score advantage. Shrinking adjacent
coefficient differences and imposing ordered intercepts are not substitutes
for noncrossing quantile curves. Isotonic projection orders the reported
simulation grids, while changing the action being scored. Posterior score
intervals remain conditional on the selected features, and their overlap
does not establish equivalence of competing methods.

The applications illustrate different uses of the regression. GloFAS combines
reference and product information at one retrospective origin, with realized
future weather covariates. Independent AL has the lowest descriptive issued-window
score, but modeled intervals remain undercovered; strict convergence of the
joint fits does not establish calibration. The Part 1 crossings and unscored
Part 2 paths further limit the conclusions. PriceFM uses region-frozen
specifications across three test folds and has slightly higher aggregate AQL
than the matched release, with substantial fold and region variation.
Unauthenticated covariate issue times preclude a claim of operational
information parity. Neither application establishes broad predictive superiority.

Further work should evaluate repeated-realization stability and broader
forecast uncertainty, including feature selection, recursive inputs, and
covariate vintages. Multi-origin GloFAS evaluation with issuance-time
covariates and paired PriceFM uncertainty analyses would extend the present
conditional analysis and require separate empirical assessment.
