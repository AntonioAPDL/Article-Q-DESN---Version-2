\date{August 14, 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Conditional on a generated and selected reservoir, Q--DESN is a Bayesian
conditional-quantile readout on a fixed nonlinear design. This supplement
defines the inferential objects and conditioning set; records the reservoir
construction; gives asymmetric Laplace (AL) and quantile-fixed generalized
asymmetric Laplace (qf-GAL) working-likelihood targets; and derives ridge and
regularized-horseshoe priors, partially Gibbs MCMC updates, and the variational
approximations that are actually used. The repository labels \(\exAL\) and
exQDESN denote qf-GAL fits; they are aliases rather than a different
distribution. Independent quantile fits and their monotone reporting action are
primary. A repeated-response composite likelihood with quantile-varying-parameter
(QVP) shrinkage is treated as a secondary multi-quantile extension. The final
sections distinguish prediction from reporting actions, summarize simulation
evidence, state the GloFAS and PriceFM information sets, identify the Gaussian
DESN mean-regression baseline, and map scientific claims to reproducibility
artifacts. In particular, qf-GAL regularity is only branchwise away from
\(\gamma=0\), Article-v2 point and branch-quadrature variational routines are
distinguished from the external Hessian--Delta engine, and the selected GloFAS
analysis is identified as AL-only.
\end{abstract}

\section{Inferential Objects and Fixed Quantities}
\label{sec:supp_scope}

At forecast origin \(T\), horizon \(h\), and level \(p\in(0,1)\), let
\(Y_{T+h}\) be the future random response and let \(y_{T+h}\) denote its later
realization. The target is the generalized-inverse conditional quantile
\[
q_p(T,h)=\inf\{u:\Pr(Y_{T+h}\le u\mid\mathcal F_T)\ge p\},
\]
where \(\mathcal F_T\) contains only information available at the forecast
origin. A declared future scenario \(\mathcal S_{T,1:H}\) can augment the
conditioning set, in which case the target is understood conditionally on
\((\mathcal F_T,\mathcal S_{T,1:H})\). If that scenario contains realized
future predictors, the resulting quantity is a retrospective conditional
estimand rather than an issue-time forecast. This distinction is maintained
below: lowercase values enter likelihood kernels, whereas forecast probability
statements concern the random response.

Let \(\mathcal R\) collect the reservoir architecture, recurrent and input
weights, reducers, leak rates, spectral-radius targets, sparsity parameters,
activation functions, response lags, exogenous inputs, scaling constants,
washout, random seed, and selection rule. Once \(\mathcal R\) has been generated
and selected using training and validation data, it produces the deterministic
design
\(\mat X(\mathcal R)=[\vect x_1^\top;\ldots;\vect x_T^\top]\).
Posterior and variational statements condition on \(\mat X(\mathcal R)\).
They do not integrate over architecture, seed, reducer, scaling, washout, or
reservoir-weight uncertainty.

Conditional on that design, the single-level readout is a static Bayesian
quantile regression. The AL and qf-GAL likelihoods are proper working
likelihoods used to target the selected conditional quantile; neither is
asserted to be the data-generating error law. Their auxiliary variables give
equivalent augmented targets for computation. Ridge is the dense Gaussian
baseline. The regularized horseshoe (RHS) is the adaptive global--local option;
\texttt{rhs\_ns} below names only the Nishimura--Suchard product representation
used to obtain inverse-gamma scale updates. Static AL/qf-GAL derivations in
\pkg{exdqlm} \citep{barata2021_flex_quantile,exdqlmRPackage} apply after
ordinary covariate rows are replaced by fixed DESN feature rows, but the
Article-v2 and external variational implementations are not identical.

\section{Fixed Reservoir Construction and Diagnostics}
\label{sec:supp_reservoir_diagnostics}
\label{sec:desn_features}

Let \(\vect u_t=(y_{t-1},\ldots,y_{t-m},\vect z_t^\top)^\top\) collect only
response lags and exogenous quantities admitted by the declared information
set. For layers \(d=1,\ldots,D\), Q--DESN draws sparse reservoir matrices
\(\mat W_d\), input matrices \(\mat W^{\mathrm{in}}_d\), and optional reducers
\(\mat Q_d\), \(d=1,\ldots,D-1\), once and then holds them fixed. With
layer-specific leak rates \(\alpha_d\), activation \(f\), and spectral-radius
targets \(\rho_d\),
\begin{align}
\vect h_{t,1}
&=(1-\alpha_1)\vect h_{t-1,1}
+\alpha_1 f\{\bar{\mat W}_1\vect h_{t-1,1}+\mat W^{\mathrm{in}}_1\vect u_t\},
\label{eq:supp_desn_layer1}\\
\vect h_{t,d}
&=(1-\alpha_d)\vect h_{t-1,d}
+\alpha_d f\{\bar{\mat W}_d\vect h_{t-1,d}
+\mat W^{\mathrm{in}}_d\tilde{\vect h}_{t,d-1}\},\qquad d\ge2,
\label{eq:supp_desn_layerd}\\
\tilde{\vect h}_{t,d-1}&=\mat Q_{d-1}\vect h_{t,d-1},
\end{align}
where \(\bar{\mat W}_d=\rho_d\mat W_d/\rho(\mat W_d)\). Spectral-radius
rescaling is a design heuristic; by itself it is not a general proof of the
echo-state property for the layered, leaky, driven system.

The readout feature vector is
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\vect x}_t
=\{ \vect h_{t,D}^\top,\ k(\tilde{\vect h}_{t,1})^\top,\ldots,
k(\tilde{\vect h}_{t,D-1})^\top,\ \vect u_t^\top\}^\top,
\qquad
\vect x_t=(1,\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top)^\top.
\label{eq:supp_feature_stack}
\end{equation}
After a fixed washout period, these quantities are deterministic conditional on
\(\mathcal R\). With
\(\vect\beta=(\beta_0,\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_r)^\top\),
\[
\mat X=[\vect x_1^\top;\ldots;\vect x_T^\top]\in\R^{T\times(r+1)},\qquad
\mu_t=\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta .
\]
All readout inference below conditions on \(\mat X\). Table~\ref{tab:supp-reservoir-selection}
records component-level checks for this deterministic construction. These are
design and reproducibility diagnostics, not posterior uncertainty over recurrent
weights.

\section{AL and qf-GAL Working-Likelihood Readouts}
\label{sec:dist_conventions}

The posterior kernels below use the following distributional
parameterizations. These conventions are inherited by the MCMC, CAVI, and ELBO
derivations below; in particular, all inverse-gamma distributions use a rate in
the reciprocal scale.

The inverse-gamma (IG) parameterization is
\[
p(x\mid a,b)=\frac{b^a}{\Gamma(a)}x^{-a-1}\exp(-b/x),\qquad x>0,
\]
and the generalized inverse Gaussian (GIG) parameterization
\[
p(x\mid \lambda,\chi,\psi)
\propto x^{\lambda-1}\exp\left\{-\frac12(\chi/x+\psi x)\right\},
\qquad x>0.
\]
The notation \(\TN(m,V)\) denotes a Normal distribution with mean \(m\) and
variance \(V\), truncated to \((0,\infty)\). The functions \(\Phi\) and
\(\phi\) denote the standard Normal distribution and density functions.

For a fixed target quantile \(p_0\in(0,1)\), the AL constants are
\[
A_0=\frac{1-2p_0}{p_0(1-p_0)},\qquad
B_0=\frac{2}{p_0(1-p_0)}.
\]
The quantile-fixed generalized asymmetric Laplace (qf-GAL) distribution of
\citet{yan2025new} preserves the parameterization in which \(\mu\) is the
target conditional quantile. The qf-GAL likelihood
introduces an asymmetry parameter \(\gamma\in(L,U)\), where \(L\) and
\(U\) are determined by the quantile-identifying construction. Define
\[
g(\gamma)=2\Phi(-|\gamma|)\exp(\gamma^2/2),\qquad
p_\gamma=\ind\{\gamma<0\}
+\frac{p_0-\ind\{\gamma<0\}}{g(\gamma)}.
\]
Then
\[
A_\gamma=\frac{1-2p_\gamma}{p_\gamma(1-p_\gamma)},\quad
B_\gamma=\frac{2}{p_\gamma(1-p_\gamma)},\quad
C_\gamma=\{\ind\{\gamma>0\}-p_\gamma\}^{-1},\quad
D_\gamma=C_\gamma|\gamma|.
\]
The main article denotes the same qf-GAL shift coefficient by
\(\lambda(\gamma)=C(\gamma)|\gamma|\); the supplement uses \(D_\gamma\)
to reduce visual clutter in posterior kernels.
When \(\gamma=0\) and the \(s_t\) latents are omitted, the qf-GAL hierarchy
reduces to the AL working likelihood with constants \(A_0,B_0\). This is a
model reduction, not a global differentiability statement at \(\gamma=0\).

\subsection{Single-Level Augmented Models}
\label{sec:model_joint}

The complete-data posterior targets below are used by both computational
schemes. The AL and qf-GAL likelihoods are working likelihoods for a
fixed target quantile, while the auxiliary variables provide equivalent
mixture representations for computation.

The qf-GAL Q--DESN observation hierarchy is
\begin{align}
v_t\mid\sigma &\sim \Exp(\text{rate}=1/\sigma),&
s_t&\sim\TN(0,1),\\
y_t\mid\vect\beta,\sigma,\gamma,v_t,s_t,\mat X
&\sim
\Normal\left(
\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta+\sigma D_\gamma s_t+A_\gamma v_t,
\sigma B_\gamma v_t
\right).
\label{eq:supp_exal_hierarchy}
\end{align}
The marginal \(p_0\)-quantile of \(y_t\mid\vect x_t\) is
\(\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta\). The AL special case is
\begin{align}
v_t\mid\sigma &\sim \Exp(\text{rate}=1/\sigma),\\
y_t\mid\vect\beta,\sigma,v_t,\mat X
&\sim
\Normal\left(
\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta+A_0v_t,
\sigma B_0v_t
\right).
\label{eq:supp_al_hierarchy}
\end{align}

\section{Ridge and Regularized-Horseshoe Priors}
\label{sec:priors}

The coefficient prior regularizes the high-dimensional readout after the
reservoir features have been fixed. The ridge prior is the default Gaussian
baseline. The \texttt{rhs\_ns} prior is the adaptive global-local shrinkage
alternative; its product representation keeps the local, global, and slab scale
updates in closed form.

For the ridge readout prior, let
\begin{equation}
\vect\beta\sim\Normal(\vect b_\beta,\mat V_\beta),
\qquad
\mat P_\beta=\mat V_\beta^{-1}.
\label{eq:supp_ridge_prior}
\end{equation}
Applications usually keep the intercept weakly regularized and use a
diagonal slope penalty, while \eqref{eq:supp_ridge_prior} allows a general
positive definite prior covariance.

For the \texttt{rhs\_ns} prior, the intercept is independent,
\(\beta_0\sim\Normal(b_0,V_0)\), and the non-intercept coefficients are
represented through the
Nishimura--Suchard-style regularized horseshoe construction
\citep{CarvalhoPolsonScott2010HS,PiironenVehtari2017RHS,NishimuraSuchard2023SSS}.
For \(j=1,\ldots,r\),
\begin{equation}
p_{\mathrm{ns}}(\beta_j,\lambda_j\mid\tau,\zeta)
\propto
\exp\left(-\frac{\beta_j^2}{2\tau^2\lambda_j^2}\right)
\exp\left(-\frac{\beta_j^2}{2\zeta^2}\right)
\lambda_j^{-1}(1+\lambda_j^2)^{-1}.
\label{eq:supp_ns_joint}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:supp_ns_joint} is the marginal joint description after the
half-Cauchy auxiliary variable for the local scale has been integrated out. The
MCMC and VB derivations use the equivalent augmented product
\[
\Normal(\beta_j\mid0,\tau^2\lambda_j^2)\,
\Normal(0\mid\beta_j,\zeta^2)
\]
together with inverse-gamma auxiliaries for \(\lambda_j^2\), \(\nu_j\),
\(\tau^2\), and \(\xi\). This augmented product representation is the one used
in the complete-data joints below.
Equivalently, the conditional coefficient law is Gaussian with effective
variance
\begin{equation}
\beta_j\mid\lambda_j,\tau,\zeta
\sim
\Normal(0,V_j),\qquad
V_j=\left(\zeta^{-2}+\tau^{-2}\lambda_j^{-2}\right)^{-1}
=\frac{\zeta^2\tau^2\lambda_j^2}{\zeta^2+\tau^2\lambda_j^2}.
\label{eq:supp_rhs_cond_beta}
\end{equation}
For inverse-gamma scale updates, use the half-Cauchy augmentation
\citep{MakalicSchmidt2016SimpleSampler}:
\begin{align}
\lambda_j^2\mid\nu_j&\sim\IG(1/2,1/\nu_j),&
\nu_j&\sim\IG(1/2,1),\\
\tau^2\mid\xi&\sim\IG(1/2,1/\xi),&
\xi&\sim\IG(1/2,1/\tau_0^2),
\label{eq:supp_hs_aux}\\
\zeta^2&\sim\IG(a_\zeta,b_\zeta),
\label{eq:supp_zeta_prior}
\end{align}
where the last line is omitted when \(\zeta^2\) is fixed.

\section{MCMC Target, Updates, and Diagnostics}
\label{sec:mcmc}

The augmented posterior targets below include each AL or qf-GAL mixture variable
once. The inverse-gamma variables in the \texttt{rhs\_ns} prior augment only
the shrinkage scales, not the likelihood. MCMC targets the posterior induced by
these working-likelihood joints conditional on \((\vect y,\mat X)\). Direct
conditional draws and invariant slice or Metropolis transitions can target this
posterior exactly in the Markov-chain sense, but finite retained draws still
have Monte Carlo error and do not make the working likelihood generatively true.

Let \(\vect v=(v_1,\ldots,v_T)^\top\) and
\(\vect s=(s_1,\ldots,s_T)^\top\). Under qf-GAL and ridge,
\begin{align}
p(\vect y,\vect v,\vect s,\vect\beta,\sigma,\gamma\mid\mat X)
&=
\left\{\prod_{t=1}^T
p(y_t\mid\vect\beta,\sigma,\gamma,v_t,s_t,\vect x_t)
p(v_t\mid\sigma)p(s_t)\right\}
p(\vect\beta)p(\sigma)\pi_\gamma(\gamma)\ind\{L<\gamma<U\}.
\label{eq:supp_joint_exal_ridge}
\end{align}
Under qf-GAL and \texttt{rhs\_ns}, with
\(\Theta_{\mathrm{rhs}}=(\vect\lambda^2,\vect\nu,\tau^2,\xi,\zeta^2)\),
\begin{align}
&p(\vect y,\vect v,\vect s,\vect\beta,\sigma,\gamma,\Theta_{\mathrm{rhs}}\mid\mat X)
\nonumber\\
&\quad=
\left\{\prod_{t=1}^T
p(y_t\mid\vect\beta,\sigma,\gamma,v_t,s_t,\vect x_t)
p(v_t\mid\sigma)p(s_t)\right\}
p(\beta_0)
\prod_{j=1}^r
\left\{\Normal(\beta_j\mid0,\tau^2\lambda_j^2)
\Normal(0\mid\beta_j,\zeta^2)\right\}
\nonumber\\
&\qquad\times
\prod_{j=1}^r p(\lambda_j^2\mid\nu_j)p(\nu_j)
p(\tau^2\mid\xi)p(\xi)p(\zeta^2)
p(\sigma)\pi_\gamma(\gamma)\ind\{L<\gamma<U\}.
\label{eq:supp_joint_exal_rhs}
\end{align}
The AL joint distributions are obtained by dropping \(\vect s\) and \(\gamma\),
replacing \(A_\gamma,B_\gamma\) by \(A_0,B_0\), and using
\eqref{eq:supp_al_hierarchy}. Thus, for example, the AL target with a ridge
prior is
\begin{align}
p(\vect y,\vect v,\vect\beta,\sigma\mid\mat X)
&=
\left\{\prod_{t=1}^T
p(y_t\mid\vect\beta,\sigma,v_t,\vect x_t)p(v_t\mid\sigma)\right\}
p(\vect\beta)p(\sigma).
\label{eq:supp_joint_al_ridge}
\end{align}
The AL target with \texttt{rhs\_ns} is, analogously,
\begin{align}
&p(\vect y,\vect v,\vect\beta,\sigma,\Theta_{\mathrm{rhs}}\mid\mat X)
\nonumber\\
&\quad=
\left\{\prod_{t=1}^T
p(y_t\mid\vect\beta,\sigma,v_t,\vect x_t)p(v_t\mid\sigma)\right\}
p(\beta_0)
\prod_{j=1}^r
\left\{\Normal(\beta_j\mid0,\tau^2\lambda_j^2)
\Normal(0\mid\beta_j,\zeta^2)\right\}
\nonumber\\
&\qquad\times
\prod_{j=1}^r p(\lambda_j^2\mid\nu_j)p(\nu_j)
p(\tau^2\mid\xi)p(\xi)p(\zeta^2)p(\sigma).
\label{eq:supp_joint_al_rhs}
\end{align}
The posterior distribution is proportional to the corresponding joint density,
viewed as a function of unknown parameters and latent variables conditional on
\((\vect y,\mat X)\).

\subsection{Partially Gibbs Updates}

The full conditionals below apply to the Q--DESN readout after the
reservoir features are fixed. The ridge and \texttt{rhs\_ns} samplers
differ only through the coefficient prior precision and the presence of
the \texttt{rhs\_ns} scale updates. Conditional on the complete-data joint
above, the Gaussian, GIG, truncated-normal, and inverse-gamma distributions
below are closed-form full conditionals. Gaussian, truncated-normal, and
inverse-gamma blocks are sampled directly when the corresponding generator is
available. The production implementation realizes GIG full conditionals by
invariant slice transitions on the log scale rather than by independent GIG
random-number generation. The qf-GAL \((\sigma,\gamma)\) block requires an
invariant nonconjugate transition when it is not split conditionally. The
\texttt{rhs\_ns} shrinkage hierarchy is
therefore not the source of the non-conjugate posterior block under this
augmentation.

\subsection{Coefficient Block}

For qf-GAL, define
\begin{equation}
y_t^\star=y_t-\sigma D_\gamma s_t-A_\gamma v_t,\qquad
w_t=(\sigma B_\gamma v_t)^{-1},\qquad
\mat\Omega=\diag(w_1,\ldots,w_T).
\end{equation}
Let \(\vect y^\star=(y_1^\star,\ldots,y_T^\star)^\top\). For a ridge prior,
set \(\mat P=\mat P_\beta\) and \(\vect b=\vect b_\beta\). For
\texttt{rhs\_ns}, set
\begin{equation}
\mat P=\diag\left(V_0^{-1},
\tau^{-2}\lambda_1^{-2}+\zeta^{-2},\ldots,
\tau^{-2}\lambda_r^{-2}+\zeta^{-2}\right),
\qquad
\vect b=(b_0,0,\ldots,0)^\top.
\label{eq:supp_prior_precision_mcmc}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
\mat\Sigma_{\beta\mid\cdot}
&=(\mat X^\top\mat\Omega\mat X+\mat P)^{-1},\\
\vect m_{\beta\mid\cdot}
&=\mat\Sigma_{\beta\mid\cdot}
(\mat X^\top\mat\Omega\vect y^\star+\mat P\vect b),\\
\vect\beta\mid\cdot
&\sim
\Normal(\vect m_{\beta\mid\cdot},\mat\Sigma_{\beta\mid\cdot}).
\label{eq:supp_beta_mcmc}
\end{align}
For AL, use the same update with
\[
y_t^\star=y_t-A_0v_t,\qquad
w_t=(\sigma B_0v_t)^{-1},\qquad
\mat\Omega=\diag(w_1,\ldots,w_T).
\]

\subsection{qf-GAL Latent-Variable Blocks}

For \(v_t\), define
\[
\delta_t=y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta-\sigma D_\gamma s_t,\qquad
\chi_t=\frac{\delta_t^2}{\sigma B_\gamma},\qquad
\psi_t=\frac{A_\gamma^2}{\sigma B_\gamma}+\frac{2}{\sigma}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
v_t\mid\cdot\sim\GIG(1/2,\chi_t,\psi_t).
\label{eq:supp_v_mcmc}
\end{equation}
For \(s_t\), define \(\mu_{0t}=y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta-A_\gamma v_t\).
Then
\begin{align}
a_{s,t}&=1+\frac{\sigma D_\gamma^2}{B_\gamma v_t},&
m_{s,t}&=\frac{D_\gamma\mu_{0t}}{B_\gamma v_t+\sigma D_\gamma^2},\\
s_t\mid\cdot&\sim\TN(m_{s,t},a_{s,t}^{-1}).
\label{eq:supp_s_mcmc}
\end{align}

\subsection{AL Latent-Variable and Scale Blocks}

For AL,
\begin{equation}
v_t\mid\cdot
\sim
\GIG\left(
1/2,
\frac{(y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta)^2}{\sigma B_0},
\frac{A_0^2}{\sigma B_0}+\frac{2}{\sigma}
\right).
\label{eq:supp_al_v_mcmc}
\end{equation}
With \(\sigma\sim\IG(a_\sigma,b_\sigma)\),
\begin{align}
\sigma\mid\cdot
&\sim
\IG\left(
a_\sigma+\frac{3T}{2},
b_\sigma+\sum_{t=1}^T v_t
+\frac12\sum_{t=1}^T
\frac{(y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta-A_0v_t)^2}{B_0v_t}
\right).
\label{eq:supp_al_sigma_mcmc}
\end{align}

\subsection{qf-GAL Scale and Asymmetry Blocks}

The pair \((\sigma,\gamma)\) is non-conjugate when updated jointly. The qf-GAL
mapping is continuous at \(\gamma=0\), but it is only branchwise smooth on
\((L,0)\) and \((0,U)\). For a nonmedian target, the left and right derivatives
of \(p_\gamma\) at zero are
\(\sqrt{2/\pi}(1-p_0)\) and \(\sqrt{2/\pi}p_0\), respectively, so a two-sided
derivative does not exist. At \(p_0=1/2\) those first derivatives agree, but a
globally valid second-order expansion at zero has not been established for the
implemented constants. AL is therefore handled as its own limiting model, and
Hessian-based qf-GAL calculations require an interior mode bounded away from
zero within one sign branch.
The joint log-kernel is
\begin{align}
\ell_{\sigma,\gamma}
&=
-\frac12\sum_{t=1}^T\log(\sigma B_\gamma v_t)
-\frac12\sum_{t=1}^T
\frac{\{y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta-\sigma D_\gamma s_t-A_\gamma v_t\}^2}
{\sigma B_\gamma v_t}
\nonumber\\
&\quad
-T\log\sigma-\sum_{t=1}^T\frac{v_t}{\sigma}
-(a_\sigma+1)\log\sigma-\frac{b_\sigma}{\sigma}
+\log\pi_\gamma(\gamma),
\label{eq:supp_sigmagam_joint_kernel}
\end{align}
for \(L<\gamma<U\), with value \(-\infty\) outside this interval.
This kernel can be sampled by random-walk Metropolis or slice updates on a
support-preserving coordinate; a Laplace-informed proposal is justified only
within a differentiable sign branch away from zero. If \(\gamma\) is held
fixed while updating \(\sigma\), a partially Gibbs implementation has
\begin{align}
\sigma\mid\gamma,\cdot
&\sim
\GIG\left(
-a_\sigma-\frac{3T}{2},
2b_\sigma+2\sum_{t=1}^T v_t
+\sum_{t=1}^T\frac{(y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta-A_\gamma v_t)^2}
{B_\gamma v_t},
\sum_{t=1}^T\frac{D_\gamma^2s_t^2}{B_\gamma v_t}
\right).
\label{eq:supp_exal_sigma_mcmc}
\end{align}
Conditioning on \(\sigma\), the one-dimensional \(\gamma\) kernel is
\begin{align}
\pi(\gamma\mid\cdot)
&\propto
\pi_\gamma(\gamma)\ind\{L<\gamma<U\}
B_\gamma^{-T/2}
\exp\left[
\sum_{t=1}^T
\frac{D_\gamma s_t\{y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta-A_\gamma v_t\}}
{B_\gamma v_t}
\right.
\nonumber\\
&\qquad\left.
-\frac{1}{2\sigma}\sum_{t=1}^T
\frac{(y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta-A_\gamma v_t)^2}{B_\gamma v_t}
-\frac{\sigma}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T
\frac{D_\gamma^2s_t^2}{B_\gamma v_t}
\right].
\label{eq:supp_gamma_mcmc}
\end{align}

\subsection{Regularized-Horseshoe Shrinkage Blocks}
\label{sec:rhs_ns}

Conditioning on \(\vect\beta\), the \texttt{rhs\_ns} scale block has
inverse-gamma full conditionals:
\begin{align}
\lambda_j^2\mid\cdot
&\sim
\IG\left(1,\frac{1}{\nu_j}+\frac{\beta_j^2}{2\tau^2}\right),\\
\nu_j\mid\cdot
&\sim
\IG\left(1,1+\frac{1}{\lambda_j^2}\right),\\
\tau^2\mid\cdot
&\sim
\IG\left(\frac{r+1}{2},
\frac{1}{\xi}+\frac12\sum_{j=1}^r\frac{\beta_j^2}{\lambda_j^2}\right),\\
\xi\mid\cdot
&\sim
\IG\left(1,\frac{1}{\tau_0^2}+\frac{1}{\tau^2}\right),\\
\zeta^2\mid\cdot
&\sim
\IG\left(a_\zeta+\frac{r}{2},
b_\zeta+\frac12\sum_{j=1}^r\beta_j^2\right),
\label{eq:supp_rhs_mcmc}
\end{align}
with the final line omitted when \(\zeta^2\) is fixed. The relevant
distinction is that the Nishimura--Suchard joint construction preserves the
ordinary global-local full conditional block given \(\vect\beta\), while the
direct Piironen--Vehtari joint prior does not preserve these inverse-gamma
updates.

\begin{suppalgorithm}{Single-level Q--DESN MCMC sampler}
\label{alg:qdesn_mcmc}
\emph{Input:} response vector \(\vect y\), fixed DESN design matrix \(\mat X\),
target quantile \(p_0\), likelihood choice AL or qf-GAL, coefficient prior ridge
or \texttt{rhs\_ns}, and MCMC controls.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Initialize \(\vect\beta,\sigma,\vect v\), and, for qf-GAL,
\(\gamma,\vect s\). Initialize \texttt{rhs\_ns} scales when used.
\item For each MCMC iteration:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item update each \(v_t\mid\cdot\) for the GIG full conditional in
  \eqref{eq:supp_v_mcmc} for qf-GAL or \eqref{eq:supp_al_v_mcmc} for AL,
  using the invariant log-scale slice transition;
  \item for qf-GAL, draw each \(s_t\mid\cdot\) from the positive-truncated
  Normal full conditional in \eqref{eq:supp_s_mcmc};
  \item draw \(\vect\beta\mid\cdot\) from the Gaussian full conditional in
  \eqref{eq:supp_beta_mcmc};
  \item for \texttt{rhs\_ns}, draw
  \((\lambda_j^2,\nu_j)_{j=1}^r,\tau^2,\xi,\zeta^2\) by
  \eqref{eq:supp_rhs_mcmc};
  \item for AL, draw \(\sigma\mid\cdot\) from
  \eqref{eq:supp_al_sigma_mcmc};
  \item for qf-GAL, update \((\sigma,\gamma)\) on transformed coordinates
  targeting \eqref{eq:supp_sigmagam_joint_kernel}, or use the partially Gibbs
  route \eqref{eq:supp_exal_sigma_mcmc}--\eqref{eq:supp_gamma_mcmc}, with the
  conditional GIG scale target realized by the same log-scale slice kernel;
  \item store retained draws and diagnostics after burn-in, with thinning only
  if used.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
\end{suppalgorithm}

Every reported MCMC analysis should record chain count, initialization,
warmup, retained draws, scan order, the transition used for each qf-GAL
scale--shape block, acceptance or slice diagnostics, rank-normalized
\(\widehat R\), effective sample sizes, autocorrelation or mixing warnings,
finite-path checks, and sensitivity to reservoir seed or chain initialization
when it affects the claim. Diagnostic thresholds assess Monte Carlo reliability;
they are not accuracy-selection rules and do not establish frequentist coverage.

\section{Implemented Variational Approximations and Numerical Monitors}
\label{sec:cavi}

Variational inference is used for screening, initialization, and selected
computational comparisons; MCMC remains the reference computation where it is
reported. The historical label ``VB--LD'' does not identify one implementation.
Three distinct objects must be separated.

The Article-v2 point routine \texttt{VB0\_point\_v} uses the conjugate
mean-field updates below, numerically updates one point value of each qf-GAL
shape parameter, and substitutes point scale--shape moments. It does not form a
two-dimensional Gaussian Hessian factor. The structured Article-v2 routines
\texttt{VB1\_structured\_v} and \texttt{VB2\_structured\_u} integrate the
negative and positive qf-GAL shape branches by numerical quadrature and combine
them with conditional scale moments. Branch masses and quadrature-convergence
diagnostics are retained. These Article-v2 routines return a partial numerical
coordinate monitor whose accounting omits terms; it is not a verified complete
ELBO and monotone ascent is not claimed.

The separately maintained single-level \pkg{exdqlm} engine maps its
\texttt{qdesn\_fit\_vb} path to a transformed two-dimensional scale--shape
mode, a local Hessian Gaussian approximation, and deterministic second-order
Delta moments following \citet{wang2013nonconjugatevb}. That construction is
described below as an external-engine reference. Its curvature fallback adds
diagonal regularization and clips covariance eigenvalues, but the returned fit
does not expose a dedicated flag for every repair. It therefore supports only a
qualified, branch-local Hessian--Delta claim. The selected GloFAS fit does not
use this external qf-GAL readout engine.

\subsection{Shared Conjugate Mean-Field Blocks}

For a single target level \(p_0\), the common bookkeeping factorization is
\begin{equation}
q=
q_\beta(\vect\beta)\,q_\Theta(\Theta_\beta)\,
q_{\sigma,\gamma}(\sigma,\gamma)
\prod_{t=1}^T q_v(v_t)q_s(s_t),
\label{eq:supp_generic_vb_family}
\end{equation}
where \(q_s\) and the \(\gamma\) component are omitted for AL, and
\(q_\Theta\) is omitted under a fixed ridge prior. In the Article-v2 qf-GAL
routines, \(q_{\sigma,\gamma}\) denotes the point or branch-quadrature
representation just described, not a Gaussian Hessian factor. The coefficient
factor is Gaussian. Conditional on the latent variables, both AL and qf-GAL
have the form
\[
y_t=\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta+d_t+\epsilon_t,\qquad
\epsilon_t\mid\omega_t\sim\Normal(0,\omega_t),
\]
with \(d_t=A_0v_t\) and \(\omega_t=B_0\sigma v_t\) for AL, and
\[
d_t=\lambda(\gamma)\sigma s_t+A(\gamma)v_t,\qquad
\omega_t=B(\gamma)\sigma v_t
\]
for qf-GAL. Define the variational canonical moments
\[
\bar w_t=\E_q(\omega_t^{-1}),\qquad
\bar r_t=\E_q\{\omega_t^{-1}(y_t-d_t)\}.
\]
If the prior contributes Gaussian canonical parameters
\((\bar{\mat P}_\beta,\bar{\vect h}_\beta)\), then
\begin{align}
q_\beta(\vect\beta)&=\Normal(\vect m_\beta,\mat\Sigma_\beta),\\
\mat\Sigma_\beta&=
\left(\mat X^\top\bar{\mat W}\mat X+\bar{\mat P}_\beta\right)^{-1},\\
\vect m_\beta&=
\mat\Sigma_\beta\left(\mat X^\top\bar{\vect r}
+\bar{\vect h}_\beta\right),
\label{eq:supp_generic_vb_beta}
\end{align}
where \(\bar{\mat W}=\diag(\bar w_1,\ldots,\bar w_T)\) and
\(\bar{\vect r}=(\bar r_1,\ldots,\bar r_T)^\top\). For a fixed ridge prior,
\(\bar{\mat P}_\beta=\diag(V_0^{-1},\kappa_\beta^{-2}\ones{r})\) and
\(\bar{\vect h}_\beta=(V_0^{-1}b_0,0,\ldots,0)^\top\). Under
\texttt{rhs\_ns}, the intercept block is unchanged and the slope precision
uses
\[
\bar P_{\beta,j}
=
\E_q(\tau^{-2})\E_q(\lambda_j^{-2})+\E_q(\zeta^{-2}),
\qquad j=1,\ldots,r .
\]

For AL, the latent and scale updates are conjugate after taking expectations
over \(q_\beta\):
\begin{align}
q_v(v_t)
&=
\GIG\left(
\frac12,\
B_0^{-1}\E_q(\sigma^{-1})\overline e_t^{\,2},\
\E_q(\sigma^{-1})\left(\frac{A_0^2}{B_0}+2\right)
\right),\\
q_\sigma(\sigma)
&=\IG(a_\sigma^\star,b_\sigma^\star),
\label{eq:supp_generic_vb_al_latents}
\end{align}
where
\(\overline e_t^{\,2}
=\{y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect m_\beta\}^2
+\vect x_t^\top\mat\Sigma_\beta\vect x_t\), and
\[
a_\sigma^\star=a_\sigma+\frac{3T}{2},\qquad
b_\sigma^\star=b_\sigma+\sum_{t=1}^T\E_q(v_t)
+\frac{1}{2B_0}\sum_{t=1}^T
\left[
\overline e_t^{\,2}\E_q(v_t^{-1})
-2A_0\{y_t-\vect x_t^\top\vect m_\beta\}
+A_0^2\E_q(v_t)
\right].
\]
For qf-GAL, \(q_v(v_t)\) and \(q_s(s_t)\) have the same GIG and
positive-truncated Normal forms as the MCMC blocks after replacing products by
expectations under the other variational factors. The required scale--shape
expectations that depend on \((\sigma,\gamma)\), such as
\(\E\{(\sigma B_\gamma)^{-1}\}\),
\(\E(D_\gamma/B_\gamma)\), and
\(\E\{A_\gamma^2/(\sigma B_\gamma)+2/\sigma\}\), are evaluated from point
substitution or sign-branch quadrature in Article-v2. The external engine uses
the branch-local Laplace--Delta construction below. None of these functions is
assumed globally twice differentiable through \(\gamma=0\).

The \texttt{rhs\_ns} CAVI updates are the MCMC inverse-gamma scale updates with
coefficient squares replaced by second moments
\(\overline{\beta_j^2}=m_{\beta,j}^2+(\mat\Sigma_\beta)_{jj}\):
\begin{align}
q(\lambda_j^2)
&=\IG\left(1,\ \E_q(\nu_j^{-1})
+\frac12\overline{\beta_j^2}\E_q(\tau^{-2})\right),\\
q(\nu_j)
&=\IG\left(1,\ 1+\E_q(\lambda_j^{-2})\right),\\
q(\tau^2)
&=\IG\left(\frac{r+1}{2},\
\E_q(\xi^{-1})+\frac12\sum_{j=1}^r
\overline{\beta_j^2}\E_q(\lambda_j^{-2})\right),\\
q(\xi)
&=\IG\left(1,\ \tau_0^{-2}+\E_q(\tau^{-2})\right),\\
q(\zeta^2)
&=\IG\left(a_\zeta+\frac r2,\
b_\zeta+\frac12\sum_{j=1}^r\overline{\beta_j^2}\right).
\label{eq:supp_generic_vb_rhs}
\end{align}
The slab update is omitted when \(\zeta\) is fixed.

\subsection{External-Engine Hessian--Delta Reference}

For the external-engine qf-GAL scale--asymmetry factor, map unconstrained coordinates
\(\vect\eta\) to \((\sigma,\gamma)\), for example by
\(\sigma=\exp(\eta_1)\) and a support-preserving transformation from
\(\eta_2\) to one of \((L,0)\) or \((0,U)\). Let
\[
\ell_{\mathrm{LD}}(\vect\eta)
=
\E_{q_{-(\sigma,\gamma)}}\{\log p(\vect y,\vect v,\vect s
\mid\vect\beta,\sigma,\gamma,\mat X)\}
+\log p(\sigma)+\log\pi_\gamma(\gamma)+\log|J(\vect\eta)|.
\]
At an interior nonzero mode \(\hat{\vect\eta}\), with positive-definite negative Hessian
\(\mat K_\eta=-\nabla^2\ell_{\mathrm{LD}}(\hat{\vect\eta})\), set
\[
q_{\sigma,\gamma}(\vect\eta)
\approx
\Normal(\hat{\vect\eta},\mat K_\eta^{-1}).
\]
For a function \(h(\sigma,\gamma)\) that is twice differentiable within that
same sign branch, expectations used by the coordinate updates are evaluated by
\begin{equation}
\E_q\{h(\sigma,\gamma)\}
\approx
h\{T(\hat{\vect\eta})\}
+\frac12\tr\left[
\nabla^2_\eta h\{T(\hat{\vect\eta})\}\mat K_\eta^{-1}
\right],
\label{eq:supp_generic_delta}
\end{equation}
with the first term alone giving the Laplace-mode approximation when the Delta
correction is intentionally disabled. A mode at or numerically indistinguishable
from zero does not satisfy this branchwise Hessian justification; it must be
handled as AL, excluded by a declared zero neighborhood, or reported as a
curvature failure rather than silently treated as globally smooth.

\begin{suppalgorithm}{External-engine fixed-design Hessian--Delta reference}
\label{alg:qdesn_vb_ld}
\emph{Input:} response vector \(\vect y\), fixed DESN design matrix \(\mat X\),
target quantile \(p_0\), likelihood choice AL or qf-GAL, coefficient prior ridge
or \texttt{rhs\_ns}, and convergence controls.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Initialize \(q_\beta\), latent-variable factors, likelihood scale
factors, and, when used, RHS and qf-GAL scale-asymmetry factors.
\item Repeat until the declared approximate-objective or parameter criterion has converged:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item set \(q_\beta(\vect\beta)\) to the Gaussian factor in
  \eqref{eq:supp_generic_vb_beta};
  \item for AL, set \(q_v(v_t)\) and \(q_\sigma(\sigma)\) by
  \eqref{eq:supp_generic_vb_al_latents};
  \item for qf-GAL, set \(q_v(v_t)\) and \(q_s(s_t)\) to the GIG and
  positive-truncated Normal factors implied by the corresponding MCMC blocks,
  replacing products by expectations under the other factors;
  \item for \texttt{rhs\_ns}, set the inverse-gamma RHS factors by
  \eqref{eq:supp_generic_vb_rhs};
  \item for qf-GAL, set \(q_{\sigma,\gamma}\) by the branch-local
  Laplace--Delta Gaussian approximation on transformed coordinates and recompute expectations
  using \eqref{eq:supp_generic_delta};
  \item monitor the quantity in \eqref{eq:supp_generic_elbo}--\eqref{eq:supp_generic_elbo_decomp},
  interpreted as an approximate objective when the qf-GAL block is active.
  \end{enumerate}
\item Return variational factors, fitted readout summaries, approximate-objective
history, curvature diagnostics, and convergence diagnostics.
\end{enumerate}
\end{suppalgorithm}

\subsection{Objective Identity and Implemented Monitors}
\label{sec:elbo}

For an exactly represented mean-field family, the formal evidence-lower-bound
identity is
\[
\elbo(q)
=
\E_q\{\log p(\vect y,\vect v,\vect s,\vect\beta,\Theta_\beta,\sigma,\gamma
\mid\mat X)\}+H(q),
\label{eq:supp_generic_elbo}
\]
with the \(s\)-latent and \(\gamma\) terms omitted for AL. Expanding the qf-GAL
case gives
\begin{align}
\elbo(q)
&=
\E_q\{\log p(\vect y\mid\vect\beta,\sigma,\gamma,\vect v,\vect s,\mat X)\}
+\E_q\{\log p(\vect v\mid\sigma)\}
+\E_q\{\log p(\vect s)\}
\nonumber\\
&\quad
+\E_q\{\log p(\vect\beta,\Theta_\beta)\}
+\E_q\{\log p(\sigma)\}
+\E_q\{\log\pi_\gamma(\gamma)\}
+H(q).
\label{eq:supp_generic_elbo_decomp}
\end{align}
For AL, replace the qf-GAL likelihood term by the AL likelihood term and omit
\(\E_q\{\log p(\vect s)\}\) and
\(\E_q\{\log\pi_\gamma(\gamma)\}\). The likelihood component is evaluated from
the conditional Gaussian density using the same expectations
\(\bar w_t\), \(\bar r_t\), \(\E_q(\log\sigma)\),
\(\E_q(\log B_\gamma)\), \(\E_q(\log v_t)\), and latent second moments that
enter the coordinate updates. The prior component includes the Gaussian
coefficient prior and, when \texttt{rhs\_ns} is active, the inverse-gamma
local, global, auxiliary, and slab terms. The entropy component sums the
Gaussian coefficient entropy, GIG latent entropies, truncated-normal latent
entropies, inverse-gamma scale entropies, and the Gaussian entropy of the
Laplace--Delta factor for \((\sigma,\gamma)\) when that external representation
is used. In the external engine, \eqref{eq:supp_generic_elbo_decomp} is intended
as a full-data Hessian--Delta approximation to the mean-field ELBO. In
Article-v2, the point and branch-quadrature paths compute only a partial
coordinate monitor and omit objective terms. Their values may diagnose numerical
stability within one implementation, but they are not comparable marginal
likelihoods, verified lower bounds, or evidence of exact coordinate ascent.


\section{Independent Quantile Grid and Reported Action}
\label{sec:independent_grid}

The primary multi-level construction fits one conditional-quantile model at
each declared level \(0<p_1<\cdots<p_K<1\). Conditional on the common fixed
reservoir design, its inferential law is the collection

\begin{equation}
\left\{
p(\theta_k,\ell_k\mid\vect y,\mat X,p_k):k=1,\ldots,K
\right\},
\label{eq:supp_independent_grid_posterior}
\end{equation}

where \(\theta_k\) contains the level-specific readout and likelihood
parameters and \(\ell_k\) contains its augmentation variables. Separate fits
share data, reservoir features, and prior conventions, but they do not induce
cross-level posterior dependence. Consequently, concatenating their draws by
iteration number does not create a joint posterior draw over the quantile
curve.

Let \(\widehat q_{k,t}\) be a posterior or variational summary from the fit at
\(p_k\). The raw reported grid is
\(\widehat{\vect q}_t=(\widehat q_{1,t},\ldots,\widehat q_{K,t})^\top\).
When monotone output is required, the article reports the weighted isotonic
action

\begin{equation}
\vect q_t^*
=
\argmin_{q_1\le\cdots\le q_K}
\sum_{k=1}^K w_k(\widehat q_{k,t}-q_k)^2,
\qquad w_k>0.
\label{eq:supp_independent_isotonic_action}
\end{equation}

This projection is a declared decision or reporting rule applied after
inference. It is not a posterior update, does not impose noncrossing on the raw
fits, and does not supply the missing cross-level posterior dependence. The
unprojected crossing count and the magnitude of the projection are therefore
reported whenever they are available.

For a scored response \(y_t\), the level-\(p_k\) check loss is
\(\rho_{p_k}(y_t-q_{k,t})=(y_t-q_{k,t})
\{p_k-\ind(y_t<q_{k,t})\}\). The article's grid score is the trapezoidal
approximation

\begin{equation}
\widehat{\operatorname{CRPS}}_{\mathcal P,t}
=
2\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}(p_{k+1}-p_k)
\frac{
\rho_{p_k}(y_t-q_{k,t})+
\rho_{p_{k+1}}(y_t-q_{k+1,t})}{2}.
\label{eq:supp_finite_grid_crps}
\end{equation}

It approximates the quantile representation of CRPS over the finite fitted
grid \(\mathcal P=\{p_1,\ldots,p_K\}\). Without a declared tail extension it
is neither the exact full-distribution CRPS nor evidence about behavior outside
\([p_1,p_K]\).


\section{Secondary Joint Composite Readout with QVP Shrinkage}
\label{sec:joint_qdesn_supp}

This secondary construction couples quantile-indexed slopes through a
quantile-varying-parameter (QVP) shrinkage prior. The reservoir construction is
unchanged. The new object is a prior over the quantile-indexed readout slope path,
conditional on the fixed non-intercept DESN design
\(\mat Z=[\tilde{\vect x}_1^\top;\ldots;\tilde{\vect x}_T^\top]\in\R^{T\times r}\).
The intercepts are kept separate from the high-dimensional slope shrinkage.
Unlike Section~\ref{sec:independent_grid}, this prior induces cross-level
dependence among readout coefficients. Its observation contribution is,
however, an untempered product of \(Q\) working-likelihood factors evaluated
at the same scalar response. That product is a composite target, not a
normalized sampling density for one response. It does not by itself guarantee
frequentist calibration, coherent tails, or noncrossing, and changing
quantile-level weights would define a different generalized posterior.

\subsection{Stacked qf-GAL Composite Working Likelihood}

Let \(0<p_1<\cdots<p_Q<1\) be the quantile grid. At level \(p_q\),
\[
Q_{p_q}(y_t\mid\mathcal F_t)=
\alpha_q+\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top\vect\beta_q,
\qquad
\alpha_q\in\R,\quad \vect\beta_q\in\R^r .
\]
This notation contains the single-level RHS Q--DESN as the \(Q=1\) convention
\(\alpha_1=\alpha\), \(\vect\beta_1=\vect\beta\), with no learned slope
baseline. Equivalently, for the single-quantile limit set
\(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{base}}\equiv\zeros{r}\) and assign the RHS prior
directly to \(\Delta_1=\vect\beta_1\). The baseline in the multi-quantile
derivation below is therefore a slope-path device, not an intercept term. In
this limit, the baseline RHS hierarchy and its variational factors are simply
absent; the innovation RHS hierarchy is the ordinary single-level RHS prior.
The qf-GAL augmented likelihood is a quantile-indexed copy of
\eqref{eq:supp_exal_hierarchy}:
\begin{align}
v_{q,t}\mid\sigma_q&\sim\Exp(\text{rate}=1/\sigma_q),&
s_{q,t}&\sim\TN(0,1),\\
y_t\mid\alpha_q,\vect\beta_q,\sigma_q,\gamma_q,v_{q,t},s_{q,t}
&\sim
\Normal\left(
\alpha_q+\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top\vect\beta_q
+\sigma_qD_qs_{q,t}+A_qv_{q,t},\,
\sigma_qB_qv_{q,t}
\right),
\label{eq:supp_joint_exal_hierarchy}
\end{align}
where \(A_q=A_{p_q}(\gamma_q)\), \(B_q=B_{p_q}(\gamma_q)\), and
\(D_q=\lambda_{p_q}(\gamma_q)\). The AL special case fixes the AL constants
and omits \(s_{q,t}\) and \(\gamma_q\).

Define
\[
\vect y_{\mathrm{st}}=\ones{Q}\otimes\vect y,\qquad
\mat Z_{\mathrm{st}}=\Id{Q}\otimes\mat Z,\qquad
\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}=(\vect\beta_1^\top,\ldots,\vect\beta_Q^\top)^\top,
\]
and
\[
\vect\alpha_{\mathrm{st}}
=
(\alpha_1\ones{T}^\top,\ldots,\alpha_Q\ones{T}^\top)^\top .
\]
Let
\[
d_{q,t}=\sigma_qD_qs_{q,t}+A_qv_{q,t},\qquad
\omega_{q,t}=\sigma_qB_qv_{q,t},
\]
and stack these into \(\vect d\) and
\(\mat\Omega=\diag(\omega_{q,t})\). Conditional on the latent variables and
likelihood parameters,
\begin{equation}
\vect y_{\mathrm{st}}\mid\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}},\vect\alpha,\vect d,\mat\Omega
\sim
\Normal(
\vect\alpha_{\mathrm{st}}+\mat Z_{\mathrm{st}}\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}+\vect d,\,
\mat\Omega).
\label{eq:supp_joint_stacked_likelihood}
\end{equation}
The reported joint analyses use the untempered product of these
quantile-specific working-likelihood factors. Equation
\eqref{eq:supp_joint_stacked_likelihood} is thus computational shorthand for
that composite product; it must not be interpreted as \(Q\) independent
replicates of \(y_t\) or as a uniquely normalized joint predictive density.

\subsection{QVP--RHS Prior}
\label{subsec:supp_joint_qvp_prior}

For a genuine multi-quantile grid, let
\(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{base}}\in\R^r\) denote a baseline readout. Adjacent
innovations are
\[
\Delta_1=\vect\beta_1-\vect\beta_{\mathrm{base}},\qquad
\Delta_q=\vect\beta_q-\vect\beta_{q-1},\quad q=2,\ldots,Q.
\]
The baseline slope and adjacent innovations use separate RHS hierarchies:
\begin{align}
\beta_{\mathrm{base},j}\mid\lambda^0_j,\tau^0,\zeta_0
&\sim\Normal(0,V^0_j),&
V^0_j
&=
\frac{\zeta_0^2(\tau^0)^2(\lambda^0_j)^2}
{\zeta_0^2+(\tau^0)^2(\lambda^0_j)^2},
\label{eq:supp_joint_baseline_var}\\
\Delta_{q,j}\mid\lambda^\Delta_{q,j},\tau^\Delta_q,\zeta_\Delta
&\sim\Normal(0,V^\Delta_{q,j}),&
V^\Delta_{q,j}
&=
\frac{\zeta_\Delta^2(\tau^\Delta_q)^2(\lambda^\Delta_{q,j})^2}
{\zeta_\Delta^2+(\tau^\Delta_q)^2(\lambda^\Delta_{q,j})^2}.
\label{eq:supp_joint_innovation_var}
\end{align}
The local and global half-Cauchy scales use the inverse-gamma augmentation in
\eqref{eq:supp_hs_aux}; it is applied once to the baseline coefficients and
separately to each adjacent innovation block.

Let \(\mat H\in\R^{Qr\times Qr}\) be the block first-difference matrix
satisfying
\[
\mat H\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}=
(\vect\beta_1^\top,
(\vect\beta_2-\vect\beta_1)^\top,\ldots,
(\vect\beta_Q-\vect\beta_{Q-1})^\top)^\top .
\]
Set
\[
\tilde{\vect\beta}_{\mathrm{base}}=
(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{base}}^\top,\zeros{r}^\top,\ldots,\zeros{r}^\top)^\top,
\qquad
\mat D_\Delta=\diag(V^\Delta_{q,j}:q=1,\ldots,Q,\ j=1,\ldots,r).
\]
Then the conditional QVP prior can be written as
\begin{equation}
p(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}\mid\vect\beta_{\mathrm{base}},\mat D_\Delta)
\propto
|\mat D_\Delta|^{-1/2}
\exp\left[
-\frac12
(\mat H\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}-\tilde{\vect\beta}_{\mathrm{base}})^\top
\mat D_\Delta^{-1}
(\mat H\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}-\tilde{\vect\beta}_{\mathrm{base}})
\right].
\label{eq:supp_joint_qvp_prior}
\end{equation}
This representation is sparse and block banded. It is the computational form
used by both the MCMC and VB updates.

\subsection{Complete-Data Posterior}

Let \(\Theta_{\Delta}\) collect all local, global, auxiliary, and slab scales
for the innovation RHS hierarchy, and let \(\Theta_0\) collect the
corresponding baseline RHS scales. Up to constants not depending on unknowns,
the complete-data posterior for the multi-quantile case is
\begin{align}
&p(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}},\vect\beta_{\mathrm{base}},\vect\alpha,
\{\sigma_q,\gamma_q\}_{q=1}^Q,
\{v_{q,t},s_{q,t}\}_{q,t},
\Theta_0,\Theta_\Delta\mid\vect y,\mat Z)
\nonumber\\
&\quad\propto
\exp\left[
-\frac{1}{2}
(\vect y_{\mathrm{st}}-\vect\alpha_{\mathrm{st}}
-\mat Z_{\mathrm{st}}\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}-\vect d)^\top
\mat\Omega^{-1}
(\vect y_{\mathrm{st}}-\vect\alpha_{\mathrm{st}}
-\mat Z_{\mathrm{st}}\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}-\vect d)
\right]
\nonumber\\
&\qquad\times
\prod_{q=1}^Q\prod_{t=1}^T
\left\{
(\omega_{q,t})^{-1/2}
p(v_{q,t}\mid\sigma_q)p(s_{q,t})
\right\}
\prod_{q=1}^Q p(\sigma_q)\pi_{\gamma_q}(\gamma_q)\ind\{L_q<\gamma_q<U_q\}
\nonumber\\
&\qquad\times
p(\vect\alpha)\,
p(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{base}}\mid\Theta_0)p(\Theta_0)\,
p(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}\mid\vect\beta_{\mathrm{base}},\mat D_\Delta)p(\Theta_\Delta).
\label{eq:supp_joint_complete_posterior}
\end{align}
This is the untempered complete-data posterior used for the reported joint
model. Alternative complete-data observation tempering would define a separate
generalized posterior and is not part of the article evidence path.
For \(Q=1\), remove
\(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{base}}\), \(\Theta_0\), and the baseline-prior factor
from \eqref{eq:supp_joint_complete_posterior}, set
\(\Delta_1=\vect\beta_1\), and use the innovation RHS hierarchy as the
ordinary single-level RHS hierarchy.

\subsection{Gaussian Blocks}

Let
\(\vect r_\beta=\vect y_{\mathrm{st}}-\vect\alpha_{\mathrm{st}}-\vect d\).
Combining \eqref{eq:supp_joint_stacked_likelihood} and
\eqref{eq:supp_joint_qvp_prior} gives
\begin{align}
\mat K_\beta
&=
\mat Z_{\mathrm{st}}^\top\mat\Omega^{-1}\mat Z_{\mathrm{st}}
+\mat H^\top\mat D_\Delta^{-1}\mat H,\\
\vect m_\beta
&=
\mat K_\beta^{-1}
\left\{
\mat Z_{\mathrm{st}}^\top\mat\Omega^{-1}\vect r_\beta
+\mat H^\top\mat D_\Delta^{-1}\tilde{\vect\beta}_{\mathrm{base}}
\right\}.
\label{eq:supp_joint_beta_conditional}
\end{align}
Thus
\[
\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}\mid\cdots\sim\Normal(\vect m_\beta,\mat K_\beta^{-1}).
\]
In implementation, \(\mat K_\beta\) should be treated as a sparse precision
matrix; the dense inverse is not needed for MCMC sampling.

The baseline coefficients are conditionally independent by feature under the
diagonal RHS representation. For \(j=1,\ldots,r\),
\begin{align}
K_{0,j}&=(V^0_j)^{-1}+(V^\Delta_{1,j})^{-1},\\
m_{0,j}&=K_{0,j}^{-1}(V^\Delta_{1,j})^{-1}\beta_{1,j},
\end{align}
so
\[
\beta_{\mathrm{base},j}\mid\cdots\sim\Normal(m_{0,j},K_{0,j}^{-1}).
\]
This update uses the fact that only the first innovation
\(\Delta_1=\vect\beta_1-\vect\beta_{\mathrm{base}}\) contains \(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{base}}\).

For unconstrained intercepts with independent priors
\(\alpha_q\sim\Normal(a_{\alpha,q},V_{\alpha,q})\), define
\[
e_{q,t}^{\alpha}=y_t-d_{q,t}-\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top\vect\beta_q .
\]
Then
\begin{align}
K_{\alpha,q}
&=V_{\alpha,q}^{-1}+\sum_{t=1}^T\omega_{q,t}^{-1},\\
m_{\alpha,q}
&=K_{\alpha,q}^{-1}
\left\{
V_{\alpha,q}^{-1}a_{\alpha,q}
+\sum_{t=1}^T\omega_{q,t}^{-1}e_{q,t}^{\alpha}
\right\},
\end{align}
and \(\alpha_q\mid\cdots\sim\Normal(m_{\alpha,q},K_{\alpha,q}^{-1})\). For a
monotone-gap version, write
\(\alpha_q=\alpha_1+\sum_{\ell=2}^q\exp(\eta_\ell)\) for
\(q\ge2\). The conditional log density for
\((\alpha_1,\eta_2,\ldots,\eta_Q)\) is the Gaussian likelihood term above plus
the chosen priors on the transformed coordinates and, if the prior is placed on
the positive gaps rather than on \(\eta\), the Jacobian
\(\sum_{\ell=2}^Q\eta_\ell\). This block is then updated by a
low-dimensional Metropolis--Hastings, slice, or Laplace step.

\subsection{qf-GAL Latent-Variable and Scale Blocks}

Conditional on the joint readout, each qf-GAL latent update is a
quantile-indexed copy of the single-level update. Define
\[
e_{q,t}^{v}
=y_t-\alpha_q-\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top\vect\beta_q-\sigma_qD_qs_{q,t}.
\]
Then
\[
p(v_{q,t}\mid\cdots)
\propto
v_{q,t}^{-1/2}
\exp\left[
-\frac12\left\{
\chi_{q,t}^{v}v_{q,t}^{-1}
+\psi_{q,t}^{v}v_{q,t}
\right\}
\right],
\]
which is a GIG density with
\[
\chi_{q,t}^{v}=\frac{(e_{q,t}^{v})^2}{\sigma_qB_q},
\qquad
\psi_{q,t}^{v}=\frac{A_q^2}{\sigma_qB_q}+\frac{2}{\sigma_q}.
\]
The GIG index is \(1/2\). Terms that do not depend on \(v_{q,t}\) have
been omitted from the kernel.

For the positive-shift latent variable, define
\[
e_{q,t}^{s}
=y_t-\alpha_q-\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top\vect\beta_q-A_qv_{q,t}.
\]
The conditional distribution is positive-truncated Normal with precision and
mean
\[
K_{s,q,t}=1+\frac{\sigma_qD_q^2}{B_qv_{q,t}},
\qquad
m_{s,q,t}=K_{s,q,t}^{-1}
\frac{D_qe_{q,t}^{s}}{B_qv_{q,t}},
\]
that is,
\[
s_{q,t}\mid\cdots\sim\TN(m_{s,q,t},K_{s,q,t}^{-1}).
\]
The AL case omits this update and uses the AL constants in the \(v_{q,t}\)
kernel.

For each quantile level, the non-conjugate scale-asymmetry block has log
kernel
\begin{align}
\ell_q(\sigma_q,\gamma_q)
&=
-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T
\left[
\log\{\sigma_qB_qv_{q,t}\}
+
\frac{
\{y_t-\alpha_q-\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top\vect\beta_q
-\sigma_qD_qs_{q,t}-A_qv_{q,t}\}^2}
{\sigma_qB_qv_{q,t}}
\right]
\nonumber\\
&\quad
-T\log\sigma_q-\sigma_q^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^Tv_{q,t}
+\log p(\sigma_q)+\log\pi_{\gamma_q}(\gamma_q).
\label{eq:supp_joint_sigma_gamma_kernel}
\end{align}
This kernel is evaluated on transformed coordinates
\(\eta_{q,1}=\log\sigma_q\) and
\(\eta_{q,2}=\log\{(\gamma_q-L_q)/(U_q-\gamma_q)\}\), adding the
transformation Jacobian for MCMC proposals or the Laplace--Delta approximation.

\subsection{RHS Scale Updates}

The RHS scale updates are copies of the single-level
Nishimura--Suchard product updates. For a baseline coefficient,
\[
\lambda_{0,j}^2\mid\cdots
\sim
\IG\left(
1,\,
\frac{1}{\nu_{0,j}}+\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{base},j}^2}{2(\tau^0)^2}
\right),
\qquad
\nu_{0,j}\mid\cdots
\sim
\IG\left(1,\ 1+\frac{1}{\lambda_{0,j}^2}\right),
\]
and
\[
(\tau^0)^2\mid\cdots
\sim
\IG\left(
\frac{r+1}{2},\,
\frac{1}{\xi_0}
+\frac12\sum_{j=1}^r\frac{\beta_{\mathrm{base},j}^2}{(\lambda^0_j)^2}
\right),
\qquad
\xi_0\mid\cdots
\sim
\IG\left(1,\frac{1}{(\tau^0)^2}+\frac{1}{(\tau_0^0)^2}\right).
\]
For each innovation block \(q\), replace \(\beta_{\mathrm{base},j}\),
\(\lambda^0_j\), and \(\tau^0\) by \(\Delta_{q,j}\),
\(\lambda^\Delta_{q,j}\), and \(\tau^\Delta_q\). If the slab scale is random
and shared across innovation blocks, then
\[
\zeta_\Delta^2\mid\cdots
\sim
\IG\left(
a_{\zeta_\Delta}+\frac{Qr}{2},\,
b_{\zeta_\Delta}
+\frac12\sum_{q=1}^Q\sum_{j=1}^r\Delta_{q,j}^2
\right),
\]
with the analogous \(r/2\) update for a random baseline slab scale
\(\zeta_0^2\). If slab scales are fixed, these updates are omitted.

\subsection{MCMC Algorithm and VB--LD Pointer}

\begin{suppalgorithm}{MCMC sampler for the joint QVP--Q--DESN composite posterior}
\label{alg:joint_qdesn_mcmc}
\emph{Input:} fixed non-intercept design \(\mat Z\), response \(\vect y\),
quantile grid \(p_1<\cdots<p_Q\), priors, and MCMC
controls. For \(Q=1\), use the collapsed convention
\(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{base}}\equiv\zeros{r}\) and
\(\Delta_1=\vect\beta_1\), and skip every baseline update below.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Initialize \(\vect\alpha\), \(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}\), likelihood
parameters, qf-GAL latent variables, and innovation RHS scales. If \(Q>1\),
also initialize \(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{base}}\) and baseline RHS scales.
\item Repeat:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item update each \(v_{q,t}\mid\cdot\) for its quantile-indexed GIG full
  conditional with the invariant log-scale slice transition; for AL use the
  AL constants and omit the \(s\)-latent block;
  \item for qf-GAL, draw each \(s_{q,t}\mid\cdot\) from its
  positive-truncated Normal full conditional;
  \item compute the sparse precision and mean in
  \eqref{eq:supp_joint_beta_conditional}, then draw
  \(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{st}}\mid\cdot\sim
  \Normal(\vect m_\beta,\mat K_\beta^{-1})\);
  \item if \(Q>1\), draw \(\vect\beta_{\mathrm{base}}\) featurewise from the
  Gaussian baseline conditionals;
  \item draw unconstrained intercepts from their Gaussian conditionals, or
  update the monotone-gap transformed block when exact intercept ordering is
  imposed;
  \item draw innovation RHS scales, and if \(Q>1\) draw baseline RHS scales,
  from their inverse-gamma full conditionals;
  \item update each qf-GAL \((\sigma_q,\gamma_q)\) block on transformed
  coordinates targeting \eqref{eq:supp_joint_sigma_gamma_kernel}; for AL,
  target the corresponding \(\sigma_q\) full conditional and omit
  \(\gamma_q\); realize any GIG transition with the log-scale slice kernel;
  \item store retained draws and crossing diagnostics after burn-in.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
\end{suppalgorithm}

The Article-v2 joint variational implementation uses the same Gaussian and
shrinkage blocks with either a point update for the qf-GAL shape or
sign-branch quadrature for the scale--shape block. Its stored objective is the
same partial numerical monitor described in
Sections~\ref{sec:cavi}--\ref{sec:elbo}; it is not a verified complete ELBO.
The two-dimensional Hessian--Delta factor of
Section~\ref{sec:cavi} is an external-engine reference and is not the
implemented Article-v2 joint update. For either approximation, the fitted
object remains a variational approximation to the composite posterior above,
not a normalized predictive distribution for a scalar response.

\subsection{Crossing Diagnostics}

For adjacent levels \(q-1\) and \(q\), define the fitted gap at time \(t\) by
\[
g_{q,t}=
(\alpha_q-\alpha_{q-1})
+\tilde{\vect x}_t^\top(\vect\beta_q-\vect\beta_{q-1}).
\]
The raw crossing count is
\[
C_{\mathrm{raw}}=\sum_{q=2}^Q\sum_{t=1}^T\ind\{g_{q,t}<0\}.
\]
If reported quantile curves are projected or rearranged, the corresponding
reported crossing count should be computed after that declared monotone
rule. The maximum and mean absolute projection adjustments are useful
diagnostics because a zero reported crossing count alone does not show whether
the joint prior produced coherent raw paths or whether the reporting rule
made large repairs. On a bounded feature domain
\(\tilde{\vect x}_t\in[-1,1]^r\), the sufficient condition
\[
\alpha_q-\alpha_{q-1}\ge
\|\vect\beta_q-\vect\beta_{q-1}\|_1
\]
is a diagnostic reference for soft non-crossing behavior; it is not imposed by
the QVP--RHS prior unless a separate hard-constraint step is added.

\section{Prediction and Forecast-Path Propagation}
\label{sec:forecast_synthesis}

Forecasting propagates posterior or variational uncertainty through a fixed
historical reservoir and a declared future information set. For independent
fits, monotone reporting uses the post-inference action in
\eqref{eq:supp_independent_isotonic_action}; it does not turn those fits into a
joint multi-quantile posterior. The article uses equal projection weights
unless a validation or application protocol declares otherwise. A projection
may be applied draw by draw only when joint quantile-path draws actually exist;
for separately fitted levels, projection of summary ordinates is a reporting
repair and carries no cross-level posterior interpretation.

For a single-level fit at target \(p_0\), write a posterior draw or a draw from
the variational approximation as
\(\theta_{p_0}^{(m)}
=(\vect\beta_{p_0}^{(m)},\sigma_{p_0}^{(m)},\gamma_{p_0}^{(m)})\). Forecasting
proceeds by propagating the deterministic reservoir state forward from the
forecast origin. At horizon \(h\), the current lag vector is built from
observed values for one-step forecasts and from previously simulated values for
free-running multi-step forecasts. A future exogenous covariate must likewise
come from the information available at the forecast origin; a realized future
covariate instead defines a retrospective replay. Conditional on the draw and the resulting
\(p_0\)-indexed design row, the working-likelihood draw is
\[
y_{T+h}^{(m,p_0)}
\sim
\qfGAL_{p_0}
\left(\vect x_{T+h}^{(m,p_0)\top}\vect\beta_{p_0}^{(m)},
\sigma_{p_0}^{(m)},\gamma_{p_0}^{(m)}\right),
\]
with the \(s\)-latent and \(\gamma\) block omitted for AL. Repeating this over
posterior or variational draws produces working-likelihood forecast draws,
model-based forecast bands, and \(p_0\)-level quantile summaries conditional on
the fixed reservoir and working likelihood. To report several independently
fitted levels, the same recursion is run separately for each fitted \(p_k\).

For a joint quantile-vector readout, a posterior or variational draw returns a
raw quantile vector over \(p_1,\ldots,p_Q\). It does not produce one replicated
future response for each quantile level. If recursive lag inputs require a
future response path, that path must be generated by a declared propagation rule.
For draw \(m\) at horizon \(h\), write
\[
\mu_{q,T+h}^{(m)}
=\alpha_q^{(m)}
+\tilde{\vect x}_{T+h}^{(m)\top}\vect\beta_q^{(m)},
\qquad q=1,\ldots,Q,
\]
and let
\(\vect q_{T+h}^{(m)}
=(\mu_{1,T+h}^{(m)},\ldots,\mu_{Q,T+h}^{(m)})^\top\). If exact monotone output
is required, apply the declared reporting rule, such as isotonic projection
or rearrangement
\citep{BarlowBrunk1972IsotonicDual,chernozhukov2010},
and set \(\vect q_{T+h}^{*,(m)}=\mathcal M(\vect q_{T+h}^{(m)})\). The ordered
grid defines \(Q_{T+h}^{*,(m)}(u)\) under the declared interpolation and
endpoint convention. A scalar synthesized response draw is then
\[
u_{T+h}^{(m,r)}\sim\Unif(0,1),
\qquad
y_{T+h}^{(m,r)}
=Q_{T+h}^{*,(m)}\!\left(u_{T+h}^{(m,r)}\right).
\]
This is an action-induced inverse-CDF draw from a reported quantile function
constructed from a composite-posterior sample and the declared monotone action.
It is not obtained by mixing the quantile-specific working likelihoods,
normalizing their product, or drawing separately from their AL/qf-GAL factors,
and it does not identify a unique scalar predictive density for the composite
target.

When separate models are fit at ordered quantile levels \(p_1<\cdots<p_K\),
the article uses a monotone reporting rule only after fitting. That rule
operates on fitted target-quantile summaries, not on off-target quantiles from
the working likelihoods, and may use isotonic projection
\eqref{eq:supp_independent_isotonic_action} or monotone rearrangement
\citep{BarlowBrunk1972IsotonicDual,chernozhukov2010}. The ordered grid defines
a reported quantile curve by linear interpolation on \([p_1,p_K]\). Outside the
fitted grid, the article reports grid-level summaries and avoids tail
extrapolation unless an application explicitly declares an endpoint convention.
For recursive distributional simulation from an independently fitted grid, a
common-history propagation rule is preferred: at each horizon, form all target
ordinates from the same current lag history, apply the monotone rule, sample
one \(u\sim\Unif(0,1)\), set \(y=Q^*(u)\), and feed that same simulated
response into all level-specific reservoir recursions for the next horizon.
This avoids combining quantiles produced under different future response
histories.

\section{GloFAS Discrepancy-Calibration Model}
\label{sec:glofas_discrepancy}

The streamflow application model uses one forecast system, the Global Flood
Awareness System (GloFAS), together with a reference gauge record
\citep{AlfieriEtAl2013GloFAS,HarriganEtAl2023GloFASForecasts}. The reference

historical reservoir states are fixed after preprocessing and washout.

The selected article analysis uses the Article-v2
\emph{AL-only} variational latent-path implementation. It does not use a
qf-GAL shape parameter or the external two-dimensional Hessian--Delta readout.
The qf-GAL formulas retained later in this section describe an optional model
generalization and must not be read as the inferential engine behind the
reported GloFAS results. Posterior-predictive sampling from the AL working
likelihood is disabled in the selected configuration. Its seven fitted paths
are separately estimated AL quantile paths; they are not seven replicated
outcome draws from one posterior predictive distribution.

The evaluation is response-blinded over its 28 held-out horizons, but its
future precipitation and soil covariates use a declared 50--50 blend of
realized and forecast inputs. It is therefore a retrospective, one-origin
pseudo-forecast assessment rather than a strictly operational-origin
forecast. This information-set distinction applies even though future gauge
responses are not used as predictors. The formulas below also record the
broader latent-reference-path construction: when a forecast-window reference
path is unknown, future reservoir states depend on that latent path and the
design must be recomputed conditionally.

\subsection{Data Blocks and Augmented Design}
\label{subsec:glofas_aug_design}

Fix a quantile level \(p_0\). Let \(y_t\) denote transformed reference
streamflow, \(g_t^{\mathrm{ret}}\) the retrospective GloFAS product, and
\(g_{T+h,m}^{\mathrm{ens}}\) GloFAS ensemble member \(m\) at forecast origin
\(T\) and horizon \(h\). Let \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm{ref}}\) index reference
observations and let \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm{glo}}\) index both retrospective
and issued-ensemble GloFAS values. For \(i\in\mathcal I_c\),
\(c\in\mathcal C=\{\mathrm{ref},\mathrm{glo}\}\), write the observed value as
\(z_i^c\). The reference feature vector
\(\vect x_i^{\mathrm{ref}}\) is generated from the reference-process history.
The discrepancy feature vector \(\vect x_i^{\mathrm{disc}}\) is generated from
the GloFAS-minus-reference discrepancy path. Both vectors are fixed after
conditioning on the relevant historical or latent future path.

Define
\[
\vect\theta_{p_0}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\vect\beta_{p_0}\\
\vect\alpha_{p_0}
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\vect h_t^{\mathrm{ref}}=
\begin{bmatrix}
\vect x_t^{\mathrm{ref}}\\
\vect 0
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\vect h_t^{\mathrm{glo}}=
\begin{bmatrix}
\vect x_t^{\mathrm{ref}}\\
\vect x_t^{\mathrm{disc}}
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The same definition applies to any indexed contribution \(i\). The
source-specific readout locations are
\[
q^{\mathrm{ref}}_{p_0,i}
=\vect h_i^{\mathrm{ref}\,\top}\vect\theta_{p_0}
=\vect x_i^{\mathrm{ref}\,\top}\vect\beta_{p_0},
\qquad
q^{\mathrm{glo}}_{p_0,i}
=\vect h_i^{\mathrm{glo}\,\top}\vect\theta_{p_0}
=\vect x_i^{\mathrm{ref}\,\top}\vect\beta_{p_0}
+\vect x_i^{\mathrm{disc}\,\top}\vect\alpha_{p_0}.
\]
Thus \(\vect\beta_{p_0}\) represents the corrected reference-process quantile and
\(\vect\alpha_{p_0}\) represents the GloFAS discrepancy at the same level. In
the historical rows, \(\vect x_i^{\mathrm{disc}}\) is driven by
\(g_i^{\mathrm{ret}}-y_i\). For issued horizons, the selected
persistence-anchored-innovation strategy holds the entire issued-horizon
discrepancy feature path at the last observed historical discrepancy and lets
the readout represent the subsequent innovation. The alternative recursive-level strategy instead
uses the keyed path
\(\hat q^{\mathrm{ens}}_{p_0,T+h}-y_{T+h}\), where
\(\hat q^{\mathrm{ens}}_{p_0,T+h}\) is the empirical GloFAS ensemble quantile
at the fitted level; that alternative is not the selected article result.
Stacking source contributions conditional on a fixed state path gives a vector
\(\vect z\), a design matrix \(\mat H\), and source labels \(c_i\). With
observed historical data only, \(\mat H\) is fixed. In the latent-path
ensemble-likelihood model, the forecast-window rows of \(\mat H\) are functions
of the missing future reference path and must be recomputed inside inference.

The forecast-time use of this decomposition is draw-based and subtractive. For
horizons covered by an issued GloFAS ensemble at origin \(T\), the ensemble
informs the forecast-system quantile \(q^{\mathrm{glo}}_{p_0,T+h}\). For
posterior draw \(s\), the reference-process quantile draw is
\[
q^{\mathrm{ref},(s)}_{p_0,T+h}
=
q^{\mathrm{glo},(s)}_{p_0,T+h}-d^{\mathrm{glo},(s)}_{p_0,T+h}.
\]
Point summaries are taken after this subtraction and are not the primary
Bayesian prediction object in the general model. If predictive sampling is
enabled, a reference-streamflow draw can be generated from the AL or qf-GAL
working likelihood using matched draws of the corrected reference-process quantile,
source-specific scale, and, for qf-GAL, asymmetry. That sampling step is
disabled for the selected article fit, whose outputs are quantile-path
summaries. Using future observed reference flows to construct
\(\vect x_{T+h}\) would leak validation information. The latent-path model
therefore treats those future reference values as missing and recomputes only
the \(H\) forecast-window state rows for each proposed future path. The
horizon-indexed origin-state row \((\vect x_T^\top,h/H)^\top\) remains a
documented bridge approximation and diagnostic baseline, not the final
ensemble-likelihood target.

\subsection{Selected AL Likelihood and Optional qf-GAL Generalization}
\label{subsec:glofas_likelihoods}

The selected fit uses the AL hierarchy in
\eqref{eq:supp_glofas_al_hierarchy}. For the optional qf-GAL generalization,
each source \(c\in\mathcal C\) has scale and asymmetry parameters
\((\sigma_c,\gamma_c)\). Conditional on latent variables
\(\{v_i^c,s_i^c:i\in\mathcal I_c\}\),
\begin{align}
v_i^c\mid\sigma_c&\sim\Exp(\text{rate}=1/\sigma_c),&
s_i^c&\sim\TN(0,1),\\
z_i^c\mid\vect\theta_{p_0},\sigma_c,\gamma_c,v_i^c,s_i^c
&\sim
\Normal\left(
\vect h_i^{c\top}\vect\theta_{p_0}
+\sigma_cD_{\gamma_c}s_i^c+A_{\gamma_c}v_i^c,
\sigma_cB_{\gamma_c}v_i^c
\right).
\label{eq:supp_glofas_exal_hierarchy}
\end{align}
The marginal \(p_0\)-quantile of \(z_i^c\mid\vect h_i^c\) is
\(\vect h_i^{c\top}\vect\theta_{p_0}\). The AL working likelihood is obtained
by omitting \(s_i^c\) and \(\gamma_c\), replacing \(A_{\gamma_c}\) and
\(B_{\gamma_c}\) with \(A_0\) and \(B_0\), and using
\begin{equation}
z_i^c\mid\vect\theta_{p_0},\sigma_c,v_i^c
\sim
\Normal\left(
\vect h_i^{c\top}\vect\theta_{p_0}+A_0v_i^c,
\sigma_cB_0v_i^c
\right).
\label{eq:supp_glofas_al_hierarchy}
\end{equation}

The GloFAS block \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm{glo}}\) may contain both retrospective values and
issued ensemble members. Ensemble members at a common origin and horizon enter
as conditionally independent likelihood contributions given the forecast-system
quantile path and the GloFAS likelihood parameters. This is a likelihood
factorization motivated by the operational ensemble construction, in which
perturbed initial states and model perturbations generate distinct forecast
alternatives \citep{ECMWF2026ENSGeneration}. Conditional independence is a
working-likelihood assumption here, not an established property of member or
hydrologic forecast errors. If diagnostics show
that retrospective and issued-ensemble residual behavior differs materially,
\(\mathcal I_{\mathrm{glo}}\) can be split into two GloFAS source labels with separate
scale and asymmetry blocks.

\subsection{Default Regularized-Horseshoe Readout Prior}
\label{subsec:glofas_rhs_prior}

For the application derivation, the regularized horseshoe is the default
readout prior. Let \(\mathcal J_\beta\) and \(\mathcal J_\alpha\) denote the
non-intercept entries of \(\vect\beta_{p_0}\) and \(\vect\alpha_{p_0}\). The
reference and discrepancy intercepts are assigned weak Gaussian priors. For
block \(b\in\{\beta,\alpha\}\) and \(j\in\mathcal J_b\), write the corresponding
coefficient as \(\theta_{b,j}\). The product representation uses
\begin{equation}
\Normal(\theta_{b,j}\mid0,\tau_b^2\lambda_{b,j}^2)
\Normal(0\mid\theta_{b,j},\zeta_b^2).
\label{eq:supp_glofas_rhs_product}
\end{equation}
Conditional on the scales, this factor is proportional in \(\theta_{b,j}\) to a
Gaussian density with variance
\begin{equation}
V_{b,j}=\left(\zeta_b^{-2}+\tau_b^{-2}\lambda_{b,j}^{-2}\right)^{-1}.
\label{eq:supp_glofas_rhs_cond}
\end{equation}
The auxiliary representation for
\((\lambda_{b,j}^2,\nu_{b,j},\tau_b^2,\xi_b,\zeta_b^2)\) is the same as
\eqref{eq:supp_hs_aux}--\eqref{eq:supp_zeta_prior}, with
\(\beta_j\) replaced by \(\theta_{b,j}\) and \(r\) replaced by
\(|\mathcal J_b|\). The two RHS hierarchies are independent a priori, although
the Gaussian coefficient update couples \(\vect\beta_{p_0}\) and
\(\vect\alpha_{p_0}\) through the augmented likelihood. The source-specific
qf-GAL scale and asymmetry blocks are the only non-conjugate blocks introduced by
the working likelihood.

\subsection{Complete-Data Posterior}
\label{subsec:glofas_posterior}

Let
\[
\Theta_b
=\{(\lambda_{b,j}^2,\nu_{b,j}):j\in\mathcal J_b\},\tau_b^2,\xi_b,\zeta_b^2,
\qquad b\in\{\beta,\alpha\},
\]
and let \(\vect v^c=(v_i^c:i\in\mathcal I_c)\) and
\(\vect s^c=(s_i^c:i\in\mathcal I_c)\). Under qf-GAL,
\begin{align}
&p(\vect z,\{\vect v^c,\vect s^c,\sigma_c,\gamma_c\}_{c\in\mathcal C},
\vect\theta_{p_0},\Theta_\beta,\Theta_\alpha\mid\mat H)
\nonumber\\
&\quad\propto
\prod_{c\in\mathcal C}
\left[
\left\{
\prod_{i\in\mathcal I_c}
p(z_i^c\mid\vect\theta_{p_0},\sigma_c,\gamma_c,v_i^c,s_i^c,\vect h_i^c)
p(v_i^c\mid\sigma_c)p(s_i^c)
\right\}
p(\sigma_c)\pi_{\gamma_c}(\gamma_c)\ind\{L<\gamma_c<U\}
\right]
\nonumber\\
&\qquad\times
p(\vect\theta_{\mathrm{int}})
\prod_{b\in\{\beta,\alpha\}}
\Biggl[
\prod_{j\in\mathcal J_b}
\left\{
\Normal(\theta_{b,j}\mid0,\tau_b^2\lambda_{b,j}^2)
\Normal(0\mid\theta_{b,j},\zeta_b^2)
p(\lambda_{b,j}^2\mid\nu_{b,j})p(\nu_{b,j})
\right\}
\nonumber\\
&\qquad\qquad\times
p(\tau_b^2\mid\xi_b)p(\xi_b)p(\zeta_b^2)
\Biggr].
\label{eq:supp_glofas_joint_exal_rhs}
\end{align}
Here \(p(\vect\theta_{\mathrm{int}})\) denotes the weak Gaussian prior for the
two intercepts. Under AL,
\begin{align}
&p(\vect z,\{\vect v^c,\sigma_c\}_{c\in\mathcal C},
\vect\theta_{p_0},\Theta_\beta,\Theta_\alpha\mid\mat H)
\nonumber\\
&\quad\propto
\prod_{c\in\mathcal C}
\left[
\left\{
\prod_{i\in\mathcal I_c}
p(z_i^c\mid\vect\theta_{p_0},\sigma_c,v_i^c,\vect h_i^c)
p(v_i^c\mid\sigma_c)
\right\}
p(\sigma_c)
\right]
\nonumber\\
&\qquad\times
p(\vect\theta_{\mathrm{int}})
\prod_{b\in\{\beta,\alpha\}}
\Biggl[
\prod_{j\in\mathcal J_b}
\left\{
\Normal(\theta_{b,j}\mid0,\tau_b^2\lambda_{b,j}^2)
\Normal(0\mid\theta_{b,j},\zeta_b^2)
p(\lambda_{b,j}^2\mid\nu_{b,j})p(\nu_{b,j})
\right\}
\nonumber\\
&\qquad\qquad\times
p(\tau_b^2\mid\xi_b)p(\xi_b)p(\zeta_b^2)
\Biggr].
\label{eq:supp_glofas_joint_al_rhs}
\end{align}
The fixed-design posterior is the corresponding joint density viewed as a
function of unknown quantities conditional on \((\vect z,\mat H)\). In the
latent-path ensemble-likelihood model, \(\mat H\) should be read as
\(\mat H(\vect y_{T+1:T+H})\) for forecast-window rows, and the posterior is
augmented by the missing future reference path.
Forecast-window output inputs are strictly lagged: the design row for
\(T+h\) may depend on \(y_{T+h-1},y_{T+h-2},\ldots\), but not on
\(y_{T+h}\) itself.

\subsection{Full Conditional Posteriors}
\label{subsec:glofas_conditionals}

The full conditionals follow from the augmented Gaussian representation.
Define, for qf-GAL,
\[
z_i^{c\star}
=z_i^c-\sigma_cD_{\gamma_c}s_i^c-A_{\gamma_c}v_i^c,\qquad
w_i^c=(\sigma_cB_{\gamma_c}v_i^c)^{-1}.
\]
For AL, use
\[
z_i^{c\star}=z_i^c-A_0v_i^c,\qquad
w_i^c=(\sigma_cB_0v_i^c)^{-1}.
\]
Stacking \(z_i^{c\star}\) and \(w_i^c\) over sources gives
\(\vect z^\star\) and \(\mat\Omega=\diag(w_i^c)\). Let
\(\mat P_\theta\) denote the conditional prior precision implied by the
weak Gaussian intercept priors and the RHS scales, with diagonal entries
\(\tau^{-2}\lambda_j^{-2}+\zeta^{-2}\) for \(j\in\mathcal J\). Let
\(\vect b_\theta\) collect the prior means, with zero entries for RHS-shrunk
coefficients. Then
\begin{align}
\mat\Sigma_{\theta\mid\cdot}
&=(\mat H^\top\mat\Omega\mat H+\mat P_\theta)^{-1},\\
\vect m_{\theta\mid\cdot}
&=\mat\Sigma_{\theta\mid\cdot}
(\mat H^\top\mat\Omega\vect z^\star+\mat P_\theta\vect b_\theta),\\
\vect\theta_{p_0}\mid\cdot
&\sim
\Normal(\vect m_{\theta\mid\cdot},\mat\Sigma_{\theta\mid\cdot}).
\label{eq:supp_glofas_theta_mcmc}
\end{align}

For qf-GAL latent variables, define
\[
\delta_i^c
=z_i^c-\vect h_i^{c\top}\vect\theta_{p_0}
-\sigma_cD_{\gamma_c}s_i^c,
\qquad
\chi_i^c=\frac{(\delta_i^c)^2}{\sigma_cB_{\gamma_c}},
\qquad
\psi_i^c=\frac{A_{\gamma_c}^2}{\sigma_cB_{\gamma_c}}+\frac{2}{\sigma_c}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
v_i^c\mid\cdot\sim\GIG(1/2,\chi_i^c,\psi_i^c).
\label{eq:supp_glofas_v_mcmc}
\end{equation}
For \(s_i^c\), set
\(\mu_{0i}^c=z_i^c-\vect h_i^{c\top}\vect\theta_{p_0}
-A_{\gamma_c}v_i^c\). Then
\begin{align}
a_{s,i}^c&=1+\frac{\sigma_cD_{\gamma_c}^2}{B_{\gamma_c}v_i^c},&
m_{s,i}^c&=
\frac{D_{\gamma_c}\mu_{0i}^c}
{B_{\gamma_c}v_i^c+\sigma_cD_{\gamma_c}^2},\\
s_i^c\mid\cdot&\sim\TN(m_{s,i}^c,(a_{s,i}^c)^{-1}).
\label{eq:supp_glofas_s_mcmc}
\end{align}
For AL,
\begin{equation}
v_i^c\mid\cdot
\sim
\GIG\left(
1/2,
\frac{(z_i^c-\vect h_i^{c\top}\vect\theta_{p_0})^2}{\sigma_cB_0},
\frac{A_0^2}{\sigma_cB_0}+\frac{2}{\sigma_c}
\right).
\label{eq:supp_glofas_al_v_mcmc}
\end{equation}
If \(\sigma_c\sim\IG(a_{\sigma,c},b_{\sigma,c})\), then under AL
\begin{align}
\sigma_c\mid\cdot
&\sim
\IG\left(
a_{\sigma,c}+\frac{3n_c}{2},
b_{\sigma,c}+\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}v_i^c
+\frac12\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}
\frac{(z_i^c-\vect h_i^{c\top}\vect\theta_{p_0}-A_0v_i^c)^2}
{B_0v_i^c}
\right),
\label{eq:supp_glofas_al_sigma_mcmc}
\end{align}
where \(n_c=|\mathcal I_c|\).

For qf-GAL, update \((\sigma_c,\gamma_c)\) source by source. The joint
log-kernel is
\begin{align}
\ell_c(\sigma_c,\gamma_c)
&=
-\frac12\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}\log(\sigma_cB_{\gamma_c}v_i^c)
-\frac12\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}
\frac{
\{z_i^c-\vect h_i^{c\top}\vect\theta_{p_0}
-\sigma_cD_{\gamma_c}s_i^c-A_{\gamma_c}v_i^c\}^2}
{\sigma_cB_{\gamma_c}v_i^c}
\nonumber\\
&\quad
-n_c\log\sigma_c-\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}\frac{v_i^c}{\sigma_c}
-(a_{\sigma,c}+1)\log\sigma_c-\frac{b_{\sigma,c}}{\sigma_c}
+\log\pi_{\gamma_c}(\gamma_c),
\label{eq:supp_glofas_sigmagam_kernel}
\end{align}
for \(L<\gamma_c<U\), with value \(-\infty\) outside this interval. This
kernel is the target for the source-specific scale and asymmetry update.

For each block \(b\in\{\beta,\alpha\}\) and \(j\in\mathcal J_b\), the RHS
scale updates are
\begin{align}
\lambda_{b,j}^2\mid\cdot
&\sim
\IG\left(1,\frac{1}{\nu_{b,j}}+\frac{\theta_{b,j}^2}{2\tau_b^2}\right),\\
\nu_{b,j}\mid\cdot
&\sim
\IG\left(1,1+\frac{1}{\lambda_{b,j}^2}\right),\\
\tau_b^2\mid\cdot
&\sim
\IG\left(\frac{|\mathcal J_b|+1}{2},
\frac{1}{\xi_b}+\frac12\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_b}\frac{\theta_{b,j}^2}{\lambda_{b,j}^2}\right),\\
\xi_b\mid\cdot
&\sim
\IG\left(1,\frac{1}{\tau_{0,b}^2}+\frac{1}{\tau_b^2}\right),\\
\zeta_b^2\mid\cdot
&\sim
\IG\left(a_{\zeta,b}+\frac{|\mathcal J_b|}{2},
b_{\zeta,b}+\frac12\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_b}\theta_{b,j}^2\right),
\label{eq:supp_glofas_rhs_mcmc}
\end{align}
with the final line omitted when the slab scale is fixed.

\subsection{MCMC Sampler}
\label{subsec:glofas_mcmc}

For qf-GAL, use unconstrained coordinates
\[
\eta_{\sigma,c}=\log\sigma_c,\qquad
\eta_{\gamma,c}=\logit\{(\gamma_c-L)/(U-L)\}.
\]
The slice sampler targets
\begin{equation}
\ell_c\{T_c(\vect\eta_c)\}+\log|J_{T_c}(\vect\eta_c)|,
\qquad
\vect\eta_c=(\eta_{\gamma,c},\eta_{\sigma,c})^\top,
\label{eq:supp_glofas_slice_target}
\end{equation}
where \(T_c\) maps unconstrained coordinates to
\((\gamma_c,\sigma_c)\). This bivariate slice update may be replaced by a
Laplace-informed proposal or by one-dimensional conditional slice updates, but
the target density is \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_slice_target}.

\begin{suppalgorithm}{MCMC sampler for the GloFAS discrepancy-calibration model}
\label{alg:glofas_mcmc}
\emph{Input:} stacked observations \(\vect z\), source labels \(c_i\), target
quantile \(p_0\), likelihood choice AL or qf-GAL, RHS hyperparameters, MCMC
controls, and either a fixed augmented design \(\mat H\) or a latent-path
state-continuation map.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Initialize
\(\vect\theta_{p_0}=(\vect\beta_{p_0}^\top,\vect\alpha_{p_0}^\top)^\top\),
the separate \(\beta\)- and \(\alpha\)-block RHS scales, source scales
\(\{\sigma_c\}\), latent variables \(\{v_i^c\}\), and, for qf-GAL,
\(\{\gamma_c,s_i^c\}\). For the latent-path model, also initialize
\(\vect y_F\).
\item For each MCMC iteration:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item for the latent-path model, recompute the forecast-window rows of
  \(\mat H(\vect y_F)\);
  \item update each \(v_i^c\mid\cdot\) for the GIG full conditional in
  \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_v_mcmc} for qf-GAL or
  \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_al_v_mcmc} for AL, using the invariant log-scale
  slice transition;
  \item for qf-GAL, draw each \(s_i^c\mid\cdot\) from
  \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_s_mcmc};
  \item draw
  \(\vect\theta_{p_0}\mid\cdot\sim
  \Normal(\vect m_{\theta\mid\cdot},\mat\Sigma_{\theta\mid\cdot})\)
  using \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_theta_mcmc};
  \item for each block \(b\in\{\beta,\alpha\}\), draw the RHS local, global,
  auxiliary, and slab scales from \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_rhs_mcmc};
  \item for AL, draw each \(\sigma_c\mid\cdot\) from
  \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_al_sigma_mcmc};
  \item for qf-GAL, update each \((\sigma_c,\gamma_c)\) by slice sampling on
  the transformed target \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_slice_target};
  \item for the latent-path model, update \(\vect y_F\) by an MH, slice, or
  componentwise move targeting the complete-data posterior
  \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_joint_exal_rhs} or
  \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_joint_al_rhs} with \(\mat H=\mat H(\vect y_F)\),
  recomputing only forecast-window state rows after a proposal;
  \item store retained draws and diagnostics after burn-in, with thinning only
  if used.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
\end{suppalgorithm}

\subsection{Variational Bayes and CAVI Updates}
\label{subsec:glofas_vb}

The selected GloFAS computation uses the AL factors and updates below, together
with the Article-v2 nonlinear future-path approximation. The qf-GAL factor,
branch-local Hessian expressions, and shape expectations that follow are a
reference extension only; they were not activated for the reported fit.

For the optional qf-GAL extension, use the mean-field family
\begin{equation}
q=
q_\theta(\vect\theta_{p_0})
q_{\Theta_\beta}(\Theta_\beta)
q_{\Theta_\alpha}(\Theta_\alpha)
\prod_{c\in\mathcal C}
\left[
q_{\sigma_c,\gamma_c}(\sigma_c,\gamma_c)
\prod_{i\in\mathcal I_c}q_{v_i^c}(v_i^c)q_{s_i^c}(s_i^c)
\right]
\label{eq:supp_glofas_vb_family}
\end{equation}
where each \(q_{\Theta_b}\) factorizes over the auxiliary inverse-gamma RHS
components used in Section~\ref{subsec:glofas_rhs_prior}.
For AL, replace \(q_{\sigma_c,\gamma_c}\) by \(q_{\sigma_c}\) and omit
\(q_{s_i^c}\). The coefficient factor is Gaussian. Let
\[
\bar w_i^c=\E\{(\sigma_cB_{\gamma_c}v_i^c)^{-1}\},
\qquad
\bar m_i^c=
\E\left[
\frac{z_i^c-\sigma_cD_{\gamma_c}s_i^c-A_{\gamma_c}v_i^c}
{\sigma_cB_{\gamma_c}v_i^c}
\right]
\]
for qf-GAL, with \(A_{\gamma_c},B_{\gamma_c},D_{\gamma_c}\) replaced by
\(A_0,B_0,0\) for AL. Stacking \(\bar w_i^c\) and \(\bar m_i^c\) gives
\(\bar{\mat\Omega}\) and \(\bar{\vect m}\). Let
\(\bar{\mat P}_\theta\) replace each block-specific RHS precision by
\(\E(\tau_b^{-2})\E(\lambda_{b,j}^{-2})+\E(\zeta_b^{-2})\). Then
\begin{align}
q_\theta(\vect\theta_{p_0})&=\Normal(\vect m_\theta,\mat\Sigma_\theta),\\
\mat\Sigma_\theta&=(\mat H^\top\bar{\mat\Omega}\mat H+\bar{\mat P}_\theta)^{-1},\\
\vect m_\theta&=\mat\Sigma_\theta(\mat H^\top\bar{\vect m}
+\bar{\mat P}_\theta\vect b_\theta).
\label{eq:supp_glofas_vb_theta}
\end{align}

For each contribution, define
\[
\bar r_i^c=z_i^c-\vect h_i^{c\top}\vect m_\theta,\qquad
\overline{(r_i^c)^2}
=(\bar r_i^c)^2+\vect h_i^{c\top}\mat\Sigma_\theta\vect h_i^c .
\]
For AL,
\begin{align}
q(v_i^c)
&=
\GIG\left(
1/2,
B_0^{-1}\E(\sigma_c^{-1})\overline{(r_i^c)^2},
\E(\sigma_c^{-1})\left(\frac{A_0^2}{B_0}+2\right)
\right),
\label{eq:supp_glofas_vb_al_v}\\
q(\sigma_c)
&=
\IG(a_{\sigma,c}^\star,b_{\sigma,c}^\star),\\
a_{\sigma,c}^\star
&=a_{\sigma,c}+\frac{3n_c}{2},\\
b_{\sigma,c}^\star
&=
b_{\sigma,c}+\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}\E(v_i^c)
+\frac{1}{2B_0}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}
\left[
\overline{(r_i^c)^2}\E\{(v_i^c)^{-1}\}
-2A_0\bar r_i^c+A_0^2\E(v_i^c)
\right].
\label{eq:supp_glofas_vb_al_sigma}
\end{align}

For the optional qf-GAL extension, define source-specific branchwise
expectations
\[
M_{1c}=\E\{(\sigma_cB_{\gamma_c})^{-1}\},\quad
M_{2c}=\E(D_{\gamma_c}/B_{\gamma_c}),\quad
M_{3c}=\E(\sigma_cD_{\gamma_c}^2/B_{\gamma_c}),\quad
M_{4c}=\E(A_{\gamma_c}D_{\gamma_c}/B_{\gamma_c}),
\]
and
\[
M_{5c}=\E\{A_{\gamma_c}^2/(\sigma_cB_{\gamma_c})+2/\sigma_c\}.
\]
These functions are smooth only within one nonzero sign branch of the
source-specific scale--shape factor. An external-engine Laplace--Delta
calculation may evaluate them at an interior branch-local mode; it is not
justified across \(\gamma_c=0\), and it is not used by the selected AL fit. The
optional latent factors are
\begin{align}
q(v_i^c)
&=
\GIG(1/2,\bar\chi_i^c,\bar\psi_i^c),\\
\bar\chi_i^c
&=
M_{1c}\overline{(r_i^c)^2}
-2M_{2c}\bar r_i^c\E(s_i^c)
+M_{3c}\E\{(s_i^c)^2\},\\
\bar\psi_i^c&=M_{5c},
\label{eq:supp_glofas_vb_exal_v}\\
q(s_i^c)&=\TN(m_{s,i}^{c,\mathrm{VB}},V_{s,i}^{c,\mathrm{VB}}),\\
V_{s,i}^{c,\mathrm{VB}}
&=\left[1+M_{3c}\E\{(v_i^c)^{-1}\}\right]^{-1},\\
m_{s,i}^{c,\mathrm{VB}}
&=
V_{s,i}^{c,\mathrm{VB}}
\left[
M_{2c}\bar r_i^c\E\{(v_i^c)^{-1}\}-M_{4c}
\right].
\label{eq:supp_glofas_vb_exal_s}
\end{align}

The RHS CAVI updates are applied independently to each block
\(b\in\{\beta,\alpha\}\):
\begin{align}
q(\lambda_{b,j}^2)
&=\IG\left(1,\ \E(\nu_{b,j}^{-1})
+\frac12\overline{\theta_{b,j}^2}\E(\tau_b^{-2})\right),\\
q(\nu_{b,j})
&=\IG\left(1,\ 1+\E(\lambda_{b,j}^{-2})\right),\\
q(\tau_b^2)
&=\IG\left(\frac{|\mathcal J_b|+1}{2},
\E(\xi_b^{-1})
+\frac12\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_b}\overline{\theta_{b,j}^2}
\E(\lambda_{b,j}^{-2})\right),\\
q(\xi_b)
&=\IG\left(1,\ \tau_{0,b}^{-2}+\E(\tau_b^{-2})\right),\\
q(\zeta_b^2)
&=\IG\left(a_{\zeta,b}+\frac{|\mathcal J_b|}{2},
b_{\zeta,b}+\frac12\sum_{j\in\mathcal J_b}\overline{\theta_{b,j}^2}\right),
\label{eq:supp_glofas_vb_rhs}
\end{align}
where
\(\overline{\theta_{b,j}^2}=m_{\theta,bj}^2+(\mat\Sigma_\theta)_{bj,bj}\).

For qf-GAL, the source-specific Laplace--Delta target is
\begin{align}
\ell_{c,\mathrm{LD}}(\vect\eta_c)
&=
\E_{q_{-(\sigma_c,\gamma_c)}}\{
\log p(\vect z^c\mid\vect\theta_{p_0},\sigma_c,\gamma_c,
\vect v^c,\vect s^c,\{\vect h_i^c:i\in\mathcal I_c\})
\nonumber\\
&\qquad
+\log p(\vect v^c\mid\sigma_c)
+\log p(\sigma_c)+\log\pi_{\gamma_c}(\gamma_c)
\}
+\log|J_{T_c}(\vect\eta_c)|.
\label{eq:supp_glofas_ld_target}
\end{align}
At an interior mode \(\hat{\vect\eta}_c\) bounded away from
\(\gamma_c=0\), and conditional on its sign branch, with Hessian
\(\mat K_c=\nabla^2\ell_{c,\mathrm{LD}}(\hat{\vect\eta}_c)\), set
\begin{equation}
q_{\sigma_c,\gamma_c}(\vect\eta_c)
\approx
\Normal\{\hat{\vect\eta}_c,(-\mat K_c)^{-1}\}.
\label{eq:supp_glofas_ld_gaussian}
\end{equation}
For any function \(h(\sigma_c,\gamma_c)\) twice differentiable within that
same branch,
\begin{equation}
\E\{h(\sigma_c,\gamma_c)\}
\approx
h\{T_c(\hat{\vect\eta}_c)\}
+\frac12\tr\left[
\nabla^2_\eta h\{T_c(\hat{\vect\eta}_c)\}(-\mat K_c)^{-1}
\right].
\label{eq:supp_glofas_delta}
\end{equation}

For the latent-path ensemble-likelihood model, the variational family is
augmented by a factor \(q_F(\vect y_F)\), where
\(\vect y_F=(y_{T+1},\ldots,y_{T+H})^\top\). Write
\(z_i^c(\vect y_F)=z_{i0}^c+\vect u_i^\top\vect y_F\), where
\(\vect u_i=\vect0\) for observed rows and selects the appropriate future
reference value for forecast-window reference rows. The forecast-window
feature row is \(\vect h_i^c(\vect y_F)\), because output lags in the
reservoir input may depend on \(\vect y_F\). For a current coefficient factor
\(q_\theta=\Normal(\vect m_\theta,\mat\Sigma_\theta)\), define
\[
e_i^c(\vect y_F)
=z_i^c(\vect y_F)-\vect h_i^c(\vect y_F)^\top\vect m_\theta,
\qquad
R_i^c(\vect y_F)
=\{e_i^c(\vect y_F)\}^2+
\vect h_i^c(\vect y_F)^\top
\mat\Sigma_\theta\vect h_i^c(\vect y_F).
\]
For AL, the future-path Laplace target is, up to constants not depending on
\(\vect y_F\),
\begin{equation}
\ell_F^{\mathrm{AL}}(\vect y_F)
=
-\frac{1}{2B_0}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_F}
\E(\sigma_{c_i}^{-1})
\left[
\E\{(v_i^{c_i})^{-1}\}R_i^{c_i}(\vect y_F)
-2A_0e_i^{c_i}(\vect y_F)
\right],
\label{eq:supp_glofas_latent_path_al_ld_target}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal I_F\) contains rows whose response or feature row depends on
the future path. Terms that do not depend on \(\vect y_F\), including the
\(A_0^2\E(v_i^{c_i})\) part of the Gaussian likelihood and the exponential
latent-prior terms, are handled in the complete-data ELBO but do not enter the
future-path mode or Hessian. For qf-GAL, the corresponding target is
\begin{align}
\ell_F^{\mathrm{qf\text{-}GAL}}(\vect y_F)
&=
-\frac12\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_F}
\left[
M_{1,c_i}\E\{(v_i^{c_i})^{-1}\}R_i^{c_i}(\vect y_F)
-2M_{2,c_i}\E(s_i^{c_i})\E\{(v_i^{c_i})^{-1}\}
e_i^{c_i}(\vect y_F)
-2M_{6,c_i}e_i^{c_i}(\vect y_F)
\right],
\label{eq:supp_glofas_latent_path_exal_ld_target}
\end{align}
with
\[
M_{6,c}=\E\{A_{\gamma_c}/(\sigma_cB_{\gamma_c})\}.
\]
The remaining qf-GAL terms in the complete-data likelihood and latent-prior
density do not depend on \(\vect y_F\) and are included in the ELBO
decomposition below.
At the mode \(\hat{\vect y}_F\), set
\[
\mat K_F=-\nabla^2_{\vect y_F}\ell_F(\hat{\vect y}_F),
\qquad
q_F(\vect y_F)\approx
\Normal(\hat{\vect y}_F,\mat K_F^{-1}),
\]
with any Hessian regularization recorded in the fit object. Delta-method
moments of the nonlinear state map are then used in the remaining coordinate
updates. If \(\mat J_i\) is the Jacobian of \(\vect h_i^c(\vect y_F)\) at
\(\hat{\vect y}_F\), and \(\mat V_F=\mat K_F^{-1}\), then the default
first-order Delta moments are
\[
\vect a_i^c
\approx
\vect h_i^c(\hat{\vect y}_F),
\qquad
\mat S_i^c
\approx
\vect a_i^c\vect a_i^{c\top}
+\mat J_i\mat V_F\mat J_i^\top .
\]
Also,
\[
\bar z_i^c=z_{i0}^c+\vect u_i^\top\hat{\vect y}_F,
\qquad
\overline{(z_i^c)^2}
=(\bar z_i^c)^2+\vect u_i^\top\mat V_F\vect u_i,
\qquad
\vect b_i^c
\approx
\vect a_i^c\bar z_i^c+\mat J_i\mat V_F\vect u_i .
\]
Second-order mean corrections can be added for selected low-dimensional
summaries, but they are not required for the first implementation because they
would require Hessians for all reservoir features.
Replacing fixed-design products by
\((\vect a_i^c,\mat S_i^c,\vect b_i^c,\bar z_i^c,
\overline{(z_i^c)^2})\) gives the latent-path VB--LD updates. The entropy
contribution of the future-path factor is
\[
H(q_F)=\frac12\log |2\pi e\,\mat V_F|.
\]
The displayed construction defines a possible Laplace--Delta objective for the
nonlinear latent-path model. The selected Article-v2 implementation records a
partial numerical monitor rather than every term in that formal objective;
monotone ELBO ascent and a valid lower-bound interpretation are not claimed.

\begin{suppalgorithm}{Variational Bayes updates for the GloFAS discrepancy-calibration model}
\label{alg:glofas_vb}
\emph{Input:} stacked observations \(\vect z\), source labels \(c_i\), target
quantile \(p_0\), likelihood choice AL or qf-GAL, block-specific RHS hyperparameters,
convergence controls, and either a fixed augmented design \(\mat H\) or a
latent-path state-continuation map.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Initialize \(q_\theta\), latent factors, source scale factors, and RHS
scale factors. For the latent-path model, also initialize \(q_F\).
\item Repeat until convergence:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item for the latent-path model, set \(q_F\) by the Laplace target
  \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_latent_path_al_ld_target} for AL or
  \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_latent_path_exal_ld_target} for qf-GAL, then recompute
  the Delta-method design moments;
  \item set \(q(v_i^c)\) by \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_vb_al_v} for AL or
  \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_vb_exal_v} for qf-GAL;
  \item for qf-GAL, set \(q(s_i^c)\) by
  \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_vb_exal_s};
  \item set \(q_\theta\) by \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_vb_theta};
  \item set the beta and alpha RHS factors by \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_vb_rhs};
  \item for AL, set each \(q(\sigma_c)\) by
  \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_vb_al_sigma};
  \item for qf-GAL, set each \(q_{\sigma_c,\gamma_c}\) by
  \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_ld_target}--\eqref{eq:supp_glofas_ld_gaussian};
  \item for the optional qf-GAL extension, recompute branchwise expectations
  by \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_delta}; monitor the implementation's declared
  numerical objective rather than assuming a complete ELBO.
  \end{enumerate}
\item Return variational factors, fitted reference and discrepancy summaries,
forecast summaries, objective-monitor history, and convergence diagnostics.
\end{enumerate}
\end{suppalgorithm}

\subsection{Formal Objective Identity and Implemented Monitor}
\label{subsec:glofas_elbo}

For an exactly represented optional qf-GAL mean-field family, the formal
application ELBO identity is
\begin{align}
\elbo(q)
&=
\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}
\left[
\E_q\{\log p(\vect z^c\mid\vect\theta_{p_0},\sigma_c,\gamma_c,
\vect v^c,\vect s^c,\mat H)\}
+\E_q\{\log p(\vect v^c\mid\sigma_c)\}
+\E_q\{\log p(\vect s^c)\}
\right.
\nonumber\\
&\qquad\left.
+\E_q\{\log p(\sigma_c)\}
+\E_q\{\log\pi_{\gamma_c}(\gamma_c)\}
\right]
+\E_q\{\log p(\vect\theta_{p_0},\Theta_\beta,\Theta_\alpha)\}
+H(q).
\label{eq:supp_glofas_elbo_decomp}
\end{align}
For AL, omit the \(s\)-latent and \(\gamma_c\) prior terms. The qf-GAL
likelihood term for source \(c\) is
\begin{align}
\elbo_{\mathrm{like},c}
&=
-\frac{n_c}{2}\log(2\pi)
-\frac12\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}
\left[
\E(\log\sigma_c)+\E(\log B_{\gamma_c})+\E(\log v_i^c)
\right]
\nonumber\\
&\quad
-\frac12\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}
\E\left[
\frac{
\{z_i^c-\vect h_i^{c\top}\vect\theta_{p_0}
-\sigma_cD_{\gamma_c}s_i^c-A_{\gamma_c}v_i^c\}^2}
{\sigma_cB_{\gamma_c}v_i^c}
\right].
\label{eq:supp_glofas_elbo_like}
\end{align}
The corresponding AL likelihood term is
\begin{align}
\elbo_{\mathrm{like},c}^{\mathrm{AL}}
&=
-\frac{n_c}{2}\log(2\pi)
-\frac12\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}
\left[
\E(\log\sigma_c)+\log B_0+\E(\log v_i^c)
\right]
\nonumber\\
&\quad
-\frac12\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}
\E\left[
\frac{
\{z_i^c-\vect h_i^{c\top}\vect\theta_{p_0}-A_0v_i^c\}^2}
{\sigma_cB_0v_i^c}
\right].
\label{eq:supp_glofas_elbo_like_al}
\end{align}
For the latent-path model, these likelihood terms are evaluated with the
Laplace--Delta future-path moments above, and \(H(q)\) includes
\(H(q_F)\). These expressions specify the complete objective that an exact
accounting would need to include. The current Article-v2 GloFAS path does not
return all of these terms: its stored value is a partial convergence monitor.
It is therefore not a verified lower bound or a quantity comparable across
model families. When \(\vect y_F\) is absent or fixed, the formal identity
reduces to its fixed-design counterpart, but that algebraic reduction does not
upgrade the implemented partial monitor.
The latent and source-parameter prior terms are
\begin{align}
\elbo_{v,c}
&=
-n_c\E(\log\sigma_c)-\E(\sigma_c^{-1})
\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}\E(v_i^c),\\
\elbo_{s,c}
&=
n_c\log2-\frac{n_c}{2}\log(2\pi)
-\frac12\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_c}\E\{(s_i^c)^2\},\\
\elbo_{\sigma,c}
&=
a_{\sigma,c}\log b_{\sigma,c}-\log\Gamma(a_{\sigma,c})
-(a_{\sigma,c}+1)\E(\log\sigma_c)-b_{\sigma,c}\E(\sigma_c^{-1}),\\
\elbo_{\gamma,c}
&=\E\{\log\pi_{\gamma_c}(\gamma_c)\}.
\label{eq:supp_glofas_elbo_source_terms}
\end{align}
The RHS coefficient-prior and shrinkage-scale terms are the corresponding
blockwise RHS ELBO terms, with \(\vect\beta\) replaced separately by the
non-intercept entries of \(\vect\beta_{p_0}\) and \(\vect\alpha_{p_0}\);
the bookkeeping follows the generic \texttt{rhs\_ns} decomposition in
Sections~\ref{sec:cavi}--\ref{sec:elbo}.
Entropy terms are summed over the Gaussian coefficient factor, GIG latent
factors, truncated-normal latent factors, inverse-gamma RHS factors, and the
source-specific Laplace--Delta factors.
For the optional qf-GAL extension, nonlinear functions of
\((\sigma_c,\gamma_c)\) may be evaluated with the branch-local
Laplace--Delta approximation in \eqref{eq:supp_glofas_delta}; such a
calculation is not valid across \(\gamma_c=0\).

\subsection{Internal Checks}
\label{subsec:glofas_checks}

The derivation has several useful reductions. If the GloFAS source is removed
and \(\vect\alpha_{p_0}\) is omitted, the model reduces to the ordinary
Q--DESN readout in Sections~\ref{sec:model_joint}--\ref{sec:elbo}. If
\(\gamma_c=0\) and the \(s_i^c\) variables are omitted, the qf-GAL updates reduce
to the AL updates with constants \(A_0\) and \(B_0\). The coefficient update
remains Gaussian because the augmented likelihood and the RHS prior are
conditionally Gaussian. The RHS hierarchy contributes only inverse-gamma scale
updates under the product representation; it does not create the
source-specific non-conjugate scale and asymmetry block. Finally, an MCMC
sampler targets the declared complete-data joint density, whereas VB
approximates it within a restricted factor family. Equality of the model
specification does not imply equality of inferential output, and the current
partial VB monitor is not the log density targeted by MCMC.

PriceFM predictions \citep{YuEtAl2026PriceFM}. It contains 38 regions over

average quantile-loss implementation on the original electricity-price scale.

\section{Gaussian DESN Mean-Regression Baseline}
\label{sec:supp_gaussian_baseline}

A Gaussian DESN with a linear readout and Gaussian ridge prior provides an
exact conjugate mean-regression baseline conditional on the fixed reservoir
design. It is useful for initialization and for separating reservoir-feature
effects from quantile-likelihood effects. It is not a conditional-quantile
model, does not estimate qf-GAL asymmetry, and cannot substitute for quantile
calibration evidence. The repository retains a longer Gaussian derivation as a
separate technical note; that note is outside the article and Overleaf source
closure, so no result in this supplement depends on it.
