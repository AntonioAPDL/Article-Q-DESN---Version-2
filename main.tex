\begin{abstract}
\normalsize
\noindent

\bs{Quantile forecasts for nonlinear time series require flexible models of temporal dependence. To this end, we develop quantile deep echo state networks (Q--DESN), which combine nonlinear temporal features with Bayesian quantile regression. Our model assumes that the quantile of interest is obtained as the location parameter of an extended asymmetric Laplace likelihood. This is modeled as a linear regression, \color{red} given a particular randomly generated network, \color{blue} where the regression coefficients are subject to ridge or horseshoe prior regularization. We consider both individual quantile regressions and a multiquantile extension (MQ--DESN), which jointly estimates quantile-specific coefficients by shrinking the differences between the coefficients for adjacent quantiles. Posterior computation uses Markov chain Monte Carlo as well as model-specific variational approximations. Two simulation studies assess quantile-path recovery, forecast scores, and quantile ordering. Q--DESN achieves competitive forecast 
\color{red}  accuracy relative to dynamic linear quantile models, while MQ–DESN substantially reduces quantile crossings relative to separate quantile fits. \color{blue} 
 We illustrate the proposed methods with an application to hydrology where riverine streamflow gauge records are combined with Global Flood Awareness System (GLOFAS) prediction ensembles to reduce quantile forecast loss relative to the uncorrected ensemble. An electricity-price application compares region-specific regressions with matched forecasts from the pretrained neural model PriceFM. \textit{[PriceFM findings to be updated.]}}

\st{Quantile forecasts for nonlinear time series must accommodate complex temporal
dependence. We develop quantile deep echo state networks (Q--DESN) as Bayesian
quantile regressions on specified nonlinear temporal features. Conditional on
a reservoir realization, the quantile location is linear in regression
coefficients, with ridge or regularized-horseshoe regularization. Asymmetric
Laplace (AL) and extended asymmetric Laplace likelihoods support independent
quantile regressions and the multiquantile deep echo state network
(MQ--DESN), which estimates quantile-specific regression coefficients jointly
by shrinking adjacent slope differences. Posterior computation uses Markov
chain Monte Carlo and model-specific variational approximations. Two
simulations examine quantile-path recovery, forecast scores, and ordering.
Q--DESN achieves competitive forecast loss under linear dynamics; multiquantile
shrinkage reduces crossings without uniform score gains.
In a retrospective streamflow application, Q--DESN combines gauge records
with Global Flood Awareness System ensembles and reduces quantile forecast
loss relative to the uncorrected ensemble.
The electricity-price application compares region-specific regressions with
matched forecasts from the pretrained neural model PriceFM.

estimated at several quantile levels or refitted for rolling-origin validation.}
\bs{To deal with such nonlinear dependence}

spanning \PricefmFullRegions{} European bidding regions and
\PricefmFullFolds{} rolling folds. For each region, one Q--DESN specification
was selected using temporal validation periods within the first training
interval. The first outer validation period determined the AL or exAL family.
The DESN specification, shrinkage settings, predictors, and AL or exAL
likelihood were then held fixed across that region's three test folds.
Each daily forecast origin has 96 quarter-hourly targets.

Each evaluation is scored on the fold-aligned held-out timestamps and
probability levels \PricefmFullPaperQuantiles. We use average quantile loss
(AQL) to compare forecasts. Throughout this section,
\(\Delta\) AQL denotes Q--DESN AQL minus PriceFM AQL. Negative values indicate
lower Q--DESN AQL; positive values indicate lower PriceFM AQL.
For region \(r\) and fold \(f\), with \(N_{r,f}\) scored timestamps, the score is
\begin{equation}
\text{Average quantile loss:}\quad
\operatorname{AQL}_{r,f}
=\frac{1}{N_{r,f}K}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{r,f}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}
\rho_{p_k}\!\left(y_{r,f,i}-\widehat q_{r,f,i,k}\right),
\label{eq:pricefm-aql-main}
\end{equation}
where \(K=7\) and both outcomes and forecasts are in euros per megawatt-hour
(EUR/MWh).
Unlike \(\aCRPS\), AQL equally weights the probability levels and has no
factor of two. Aggregate scores give equal weight to the 114 region--fold
evaluations, rather than pooling all timestamps.

The predictors include the released load, solar, and wind forecast
covariates; spatial specifications also use neighboring-region features or
their summaries. Their issue-time vintages were not independently verified.
The supplement records the fold dates, training-only preprocessing, and
selection history.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Q--DESN retains a regularized linear regression for the quantile location
while using fixed recurrent features to represent nonlinear temporal
dependence. Its posterior concerns the regression, likelihood, and shrinkage
parameters, conditional on the reservoir. This distinction makes the model
accessible through Bayesian regression calculations without treating every
network weight as an unknown parameter. The AL augmentation supports
Gibbs-based computation, while VB makes repeated regression fits practical.

The single-quantile comparison is most favorable to Q--DESN at the median,
with likelihood preferences varying by observation-error family. In the
multiquantile study, MQ--DESN improves the ordering of posterior-mean
forecast grids, especially under exAL, while independent regressions have
lower mean oracle scores in most mechanisms. These results distinguish the
benefit of adjacent-coefficient regularization for quantile ordering from its
less consistent gains in forecast scores.

The applications illustrate different uses of the regression. For streamflow
correction, the reference--discrepancy regression combines gauge measurements
with GloFAS products at one retrospective forecast origin. Q--DESN under AL
has the lowest descriptive issued-window
score, but its nominal 90\% interval covers 75\% of the issued-window
observations. The electricity-price comparison holds each regional
specification fixed across three test folds and has slightly higher aggregate
AQL than PriceFM, with substantial fold and region variation. The
applications retain their stated information sets: realized weather for
streamflow correction and supplied covariates with unverified issue-time
vintages for electricity prices.

Further work should assess variation across repeated simulated series and
uncertainty from reservoir selection and recursively generated predictors.
Multi-origin GloFAS evaluation with covariates available at issuance and
paired PriceFM uncertainty analyses would extend the present comparisons.
